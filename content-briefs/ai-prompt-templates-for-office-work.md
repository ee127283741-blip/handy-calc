## Points competitors commonly cover
- Bracketed fill-in templates for email, meeting notes, summaries, reports, job ads.
- Structure: role, context, task, format, constraints.
- "Treat output as a draft" and review before sending.

## Gaps / weaknesses
- No sample outputs shown anywhere; reader cannot see what "good" looks like.
- Little explanation of why each line of a template is there, so people delete the important parts.
- Spreadsheet-formula, quote-draft and customer-complaint templates mostly missing.
- No guidance on what NOT to paste (customer personal data, confidential numbers) or on checking numbers/dates/commitments.
- No mention that long reusable prompts cost money on the API.

## Our outline
1. Two plain paragraphs: what the templates are, how to use placeholders.
2. How to use them (fill brackets, remove sensitive data, check output).
3. 11 templates: email reply, meeting recap, quote draft, policy summary, spreadsheet formula, job post, complaint reply, rewrite for audience, status update, SOP draft, proofread. Each: code block + "why each part matters" + what a good output contains.
4. Keeping a team library; cost note with real token count.
5. FAQs.

Gap summary: Competitors list bare fill-in prompts with no sample outputs and no rationale; we give 11 office templates (including quote, complaint, formula and policy-summary prompts) with a line-by-line "why this part matters", a described sample output, a data-safety check for each, and a token-cost note.
