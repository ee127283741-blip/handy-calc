ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can all write spreadsheet formulas from a plain-English description. They know the syntax of common Excel and Google Sheets functions well, and they are good at explaining a formula someone else wrote.

What they cannot know is how your sheet is laid out. Most wrong AI formulas are correct formulas pointed at the wrong columns, or written for a function your version does not have. This guide shows the prompt pattern that avoids that, eight formulas we tested on a sample sheet, and a quick way to check any formula before you rely on it.

## The prompt pattern

```text
I use [Excel 365 / Excel 2019 / Google Sheets].
Sheet "Orders", headers in row 1, data in rows 2 to [LAST ROW]:
A = Invoice, B = Date, C = Region, D = Rep, E = Amount, F = Status (Paid/Unpaid)
In [CELL] I want [RESULT].
Edge cases: [blank rows / no match / text in number column].
Give one formula, then explain each argument in one line.
```

The app and version line matters most: Microsoft's documentation says XLOOKUP is not available in Excel 2016 and Excel 2019 ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/xlookup-function-b7fd680e-6d10-43e6-84f9-88eae8bf5929)), so a formula that works in Excel 365 may fail for a colleague. You do not need to paste real data. Headers and a few made-up rows are enough, which also keeps customer details out of the chat. More templates like this are in our [office prompt templates](/guides/ai-prompt-templates-for-office-work/).

## Eight formulas, explained and verified

### 1. Total with two conditions: paid sales in the North

```text
=SUMIFS(E2:E9,C2:C9,"North",F2:F9,"Paid")
```

SUMIFS takes the range to add first, then pairs of (range, condition). Here it adds Amount where Region is "North" and Status is "Paid". **Result: 1940** (1200 + 430 + 310). Argument order is the same in [Excel](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/sumifs-function-c9e748f5-7ea7-455d-9406-611cebce642b) and [Google Sheets](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/3238496). The older SUMIF puts the sum range last, a frequent source of AI mix-ups.

### 2. Look up an invoice amount, with a fallback

```text
=XLOOKUP("INV-1004",A2:A9,E2:E9,"Not found")
```

Search for the invoice in column A and return the matching value from column E. The fourth argument is what to show if there is no match; without it you get #N/A. **Result: 2100.** With "INV-9999" instead, the result is "Not found". Microsoft calls the fourth argument if_not_found and Google calls it missing_value ([Google](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/12405947)); the position is the same. For Excel 2019 or earlier, ask for an INDEX/MATCH version instead.

### 3. Count invoices in a month

```text
=COUNTIFS(B2:B9,">="&DATE(2026,10,1),B2:B9,"<="&DATE(2026,10,31))
```

Two conditions on the same date column: on or after 1 October and on or before 31 October. The `&` joins the comparison sign to the date. **Result: 6** (rows 3 to 8). If this returns 0 on your sheet, your dates are probably stored as text; check by changing a date cell's format to Number.

### 4. Total for a date range

```text
=SUMIFS(E2:E9,B2:B9,">="&DATE(2026,10,1),B2:B9,"<"&DATE(2026,11,1))
```

Same idea, adding amounts. Using "before 1 November" instead of "on or before 31 October" also catches times late on the 31st if your dates include times. **Result: 5305** (850 + 430 + 2100 + 640 + 975 + 310).

### 5. Flag overdue invoices

```text
=IF(AND(F3<>"Paid",DATE(2026,11,15)-B3>30),"Overdue","")
```

If the invoice is not paid and more than 30 days have passed, show "Overdue"; otherwise leave the cell blank. Subtracting dates gives days. **Result for row 3 checked on 15 November: "Overdue"** (44 days). Checked on 1 November it is blank, because 30 days is not more than 30. In real use, replace `DATE(2026,11,15)` with `TODAY()` and fill the formula down.

### 6. Clean up messy names

```text
=PROPER(TRIM(D2))
```

TRIM removes extra spaces; PROPER capitalises each word. **Result: "Ana Lopez"** from "  ana LOPEZ ". This makes names match for counting and lookups. PROPER will get names like "McDonald" wrong ("Mcdonald"), so scan the results.

### 7. Average for one region

```text
=ROUND(AVERAGEIFS(E2:E9,C2:C9,"North"),2)
```

Average Amount where Region is North, rounded to two decimals. **Result: 645** ((1200 + 430 + 640 + 310) ÷ 4).

### 8. Working days between two dates

```text
=NETWORKDAYS(DATE(2026,11,2),DATE(2026,11,13))
```

Counts Monday-to-Friday days between two dates, including both ends. 2 November 2026 is a Monday and 13 November a Friday. **Result: 10.** An optional third argument takes a range of holiday dates to exclude ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/networkdays-function-48e717bf-a7a3-495f-969e-5005e3eb18e7)).

## How to check any AI formula

1. **Pick two rows where you know the answer.** Work them out by hand or with a filter, and compare.
2. **Test a "no result" case.** A region that does not exist should return 0 or your fallback text, not an error.
3. **Test an edge case.** A blank row, a date at the boundary, a name with extra spaces.
4. **Ask for the explanation, then read it against your columns.** If the AI says "column C is Amount" and it is not, the formula is wrong even if it returns a number.
5. **Check after filling down.** Ask whether ranges should be fixed with `$` (for example `$E$2:$E$9`) so they do not shift when copied.

## Excel or Sheets differences worth telling the AI

- **Version.** XLOOKUP needs Excel 2021 or later, Excel for Microsoft 365, or Google Sheets.
- **Separators.** In some regional settings, arguments are separated with semicolons rather than commas. If a pasted formula is rejected, that is the first thing to check.
- **Sheets-only functions.** Google Sheets has functions such as QUERY that Excel does not. If colleagues use Excel, ask for a formula that works in both.
