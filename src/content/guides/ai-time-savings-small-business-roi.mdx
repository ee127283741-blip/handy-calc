Most claims about AI saving time are either vague or based on someone else's office. The only figure that matters to you is how many hours your own staff get back on your own tasks, and what that time is worth compared with what the tool costs.

This guide works through one realistic example from start to finish: an 8-person office, three everyday tasks, deliberately cautious assumptions, and every step of the arithmetic shown. You can copy the method and change any number that does not fit your business.

We do not quote outside productivity studies here. Their results vary widely by task and setting, and none of them measured your team. The assumptions below are labelled as assumptions so you can swap in your own. Plan prices were last verified: November 1, 2026.

To run the same calculation with your own figures, use the **[AI ROI calculator](/ai-business/ai-roi-calculator/)**. It uses exactly the formulas shown below.

## The office

Eight people: an owner, an office manager, two sales staff, two project coordinators and two admin staff. They spend a noticeable share of the week on writing that follows a pattern.

The three tasks we count:

| Task | Without AI | With AI (assumption) | Saved per week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drafting routine emails (follow-ups, scheduling, supplier replies) | 10 emails × 6 min = 60 min | 10 emails × 3 min = 30 min | 30 min |
| Turning meeting notes into a written summary and action list | 2 meetings × 25 min = 50 min | 2 meetings × 10 min = 20 min | 30 min |
| First drafts of quotes and proposals | 2 drafts × 30 min = 60 min | 2 drafts × 15 min = 30 min | 30 min |
| **Total** | | | **90 min = 1.5 hours** |

The "with AI" times include reading and correcting the output. We assume no time saved on anything else: phone calls, site visits, bookkeeping and customer meetings are left out.

## The other assumptions

- **Loaded hourly cost: $38.** This is an assumption for illustration, not an official figure. Loaded cost means wages plus what the employer pays on top: paid leave, insurance, retirement contributions and legally required payroll taxes. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics [Employer Costs for Employee Compensation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.toc.htm) release shows how total compensation splits between wages and these benefits. Use your own payroll figures if you have them.
- **Adoption: 60%.** Of 8 staff, we assume 4.8 on average use the tool regularly. Some weeks someone is on leave, and some people simply prefer their old way.
- **Tool: $20 per seat per month, for all 8 seats.** That is the monthly price of ChatGPT Plus ([OpenAI](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6950777-what-is-chatgpt-plus)) and Claude Pro billed monthly ([claude.com/pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). Google AI Pro is $19.99 ([Google AI plans](https://one.google.com/intl/en_us/about/google-ai-plans/)). Business and team plans are priced differently, so check the provider's page if you need one.
- **One-off setup: $1,000.** Covers a couple of hours of training for each person, writing a short guide on what not to paste into the tool, and the owner's time choosing a plan. Roughly 8 people × 2 hours × $38 = $608, plus about $400 of the owner's time and admin.

## The calculation

These are the same formulas the [AI ROI calculator](/ai-business/ai-roi-calculator/) uses. A month is counted as 52 ÷ 12 = 4.333 weeks.

**1. Active users**
8 employees × 60% = **4.8 active users**

**2. Hours saved per month**
4.8 × 1.5 hours × 4.333 weeks = **31.2 hours**

**3. Value of time saved per month**
31.2 hours × $38 = **$1,185.60**

**4. Tool cost per month**
8 seats × $20 = **$160**

Seats are paid for all 8 people, even though only 4.8 are counted as saving time.

**5. Net monthly benefit**
$1,185.60 − $160 = **$1,025.60**

**6. First-year cost**
($160 × 12) + $1,000 setup = $1,920 + $1,000 = **$2,920**

**7. First-year ROI**
First-year value: $1,185.60 × 12 = $14,227.20
ROI = ($14,227.20 − $2,920) ÷ $2,920 × 100 = **387%**

**8. Payback period**
$1,000 setup ÷ $1,025.60 net per month = **0.98 months**

In plain terms: the tool costs this office $2,920 in its first year and frees up about 31 hours a month, worth $1,185.60 at the assumed loaded cost. Setup is recovered within the first month.

## How sensitive is this?

The result depends heavily on two numbers you cannot know in advance: hours saved and adoption. Here is the same office with those changed and everything else held fixed ($38 an hour, 8 seats at $20, $1,000 setup).

| Case | Hours saved per active user per week | Adoption | Hours saved per month | Monthly value | Net per month | First-year ROI | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pessimistic | 0.5 | 40% (3.2 users) | 6.9 | $263.47 | $103.47 | 8% | 9.7 months |
| Base | 1.5 | 60% (4.8 users) | 31.2 | $1,185.60 | $1,025.60 | 387% | 1.0 month |
| Optimistic | 3 | 80% (6.4 users) | 83.2 | $3,161.60 | $3,001.60 | 1,199% | 0.3 months |

The pessimistic row shows the realistic risk. If people only save half an hour a week and fewer than half use it, the tool barely beats its cost: first-year value of $263.47 × 12 = $3,161.60 against $2,920 spent, an 8% return.

The seat cost is rarely the problem. At $160 a month it is covered by about 4.2 hours of saved time ($160 ÷ $38). The bigger risks are low adoption and savings that are smaller than people report.

## Break-even: how little time has to be saved

Instead of asking how much the tool earns, ask the opposite: what is the smallest saving that still covers its cost? With 4.8 active users, one minute saved per active user per week adds up to 4.8 × 4.333 = 20.8 minutes a month across the office, so the arithmetic is short.

- **To cover the seats only:** $160 ÷ $38 = 4.21 hours a month. Spread over 4.8 users × 4.333 weeks = 20.8 user-weeks, that is 4.21 ÷ 20.8 = 0.20 hours, or about **12 minutes per active user per week**.
- **To cover the first-year cost including setup:** $2,920 ÷ 12 = $243.33 a month, ÷ $38 = 6.40 hours a month, ÷ 20.8 = 0.31 hours, or about **18.5 minutes per active user per week**.

If your pilot shows people saving less than about 20 minutes a week after checking the output, the tool is unlikely to pay for itself in year one at this adoption rate.

## Variant: buy seats only for the people who use it

Suppose the office buys 5 seats instead of 8 and nearly everyone with a seat uses it: 5 × 96% = 4.8 active users, the same as the base case. Hours saved and monthly value stay at 31.2 hours and $1,185.60. What changes:

- Tool cost: 5 × $20 = $100 a month
- Net monthly benefit: $1,185.60 − $100 = $1,085.60
- First-year cost: $100 × 12 + $1,000 = $2,200
- First-year ROI: ($14,227.20 − $2,200) ÷ $2,200 × 100 = **547%**
- Payback: $1,000 ÷ $1,085.60 = **0.92 months**

Cutting three unused seats saves $720 a year and lifts ROI from 387% to 547% without anyone saving more time.

## Turning the estimate into a decision

- **Run a short pilot first.** Give two or three people seats for a month and time a handful of real tasks before and after. Replace the 1.5-hour assumption with what you measure.
- **Buy seats for regular users only.** If two staff will rarely use the tool, leaving them out cuts the monthly cost by $40 and raises the ROI.
- **Decide where the freed time goes.** Saved hours are only worth $38 each if they go to quoting more jobs, following up more leads, or avoiding overtime or a temporary hire. Otherwise the value is real but does not show up in the accounts.
- **Re-check after three months.** Usage often drops after the first few weeks. Recalculate with actual adoption.
