  - q: Why is a cup of flour not 240 grams?
    a: A cup measures volume, not weight. 240 is roughly the number of millilitres in a US cup, and only water-like liquids weigh about 1 gram per millilitre. Flour is much less dense, so a cup of it weighs about half as much.

## Worked example: converting a cookie recipe to grams

A recipe calls for **2 1/2 cups of all-purpose flour** and **1 1/2 cups of granulated sugar**.

1. Flour: 2.5 cups × 120 g per cup = **300 g**.
2. Sugar: 1.5 cups × 198 g per cup = **297 g**.

Weighing gives more consistent results than cups, because how you fill a cup (scooped or spooned) changes how much flour goes in.

## How the conversion works

<code class="formula">grams = cups × grams per cup (for that ingredient)</code>

- **grams per cup** comes from the King Arthur Baking ingredient weight chart. Where the chart lists a smaller measure (for example 1/2 cup of cocoa = 42 g, or 1 tablespoon of honey = 21 g), we multiply up to one cup.
- The reverse works too: cups = grams ÷ grams per cup.
- **Assumptions:** US customary cups (about 236.6 mL), and flour measured by spooning it into the cup and levelling it. Scooping straight from the bag packs more flour into the cup.
