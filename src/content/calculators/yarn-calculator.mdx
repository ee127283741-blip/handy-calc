faqs:
  - q: How do I know how much yarn a swatch used?
    a: Weigh the finished swatch on a kitchen or jewellery scale that reads to 1 gram or better. Alternatively, measure the yarn before and after knitting or crocheting the swatch and use yards or metres.
  - q: Why add a buffer to the yarn estimate?
    a: Edging, seams, joining, weaving in ends and small gauge changes over a large project all use extra yarn. A buffer of 10 to 15 percent helps you avoid running out, especially since a later skein may come from a different dye lot.
  - q: Does crochet use more yarn than knitting?
    a: Crochet fabric is usually thicker and often uses more yarn for the same area than knitting with the same yarn. That is why measuring your own swatch, made in your stitch pattern, gives a better estimate than a general rule.
  - q: What yarn weight should I use for a blanket?
    a: Medium (worsted, aran, Craft Yarn Council category 4) and bulky (category 5) yarns are popular for blankets because they work up quickly. Follow your pattern's recommended weight and check your gauge.
  - q: Can I use centimetres instead of inches?
    a: Yes. The calculation only uses the ratio between the project area and the swatch area, so any unit works as long as the swatch and project use the same one.

## Worked example: a 36 × 48 in baby blanket

You knit a 4 × 4 in swatch in your blanket's stitch pattern and it weighs 5 g. (This is an example; your own swatch weight will differ by yarn and stitch.) Your yarn comes in 100 g skeins.

1. Swatch area: 4 × 4 = 16 sq in.
2. Blanket area: 36 × 48 = 1,728 sq in.
3. Ratio: 1,728 ÷ 16 = 108 swatches' worth.
4. Yarn: 108 × 5 g = 540 g.
5. Add a 10% buffer: 540 × 1.10 = **594 g**.
6. Skeins: 594 ÷ 100 = 5.94, rounded up to **6 skeins**.

## How the yarn estimate works

<code class="formula">yarn = swatch yarn × (project width × project height) ÷ (swatch width × swatch height) × (1 + buffer%)</code>

- The method assumes yarn use is proportional to fabric area, which holds when the whole project uses the same stitch pattern and gauge.
- Make your swatch at least 4 × 4 in (10 × 10 cm), and wash and block it the way you will treat the finished piece.
- **Limitations:** separate borders, colourwork, cables and fringe use different amounts of yarn per area; estimate them separately or increase the buffer.
