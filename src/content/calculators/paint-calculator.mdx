## Worked example: a 12 × 12 ft bedroom

The room is 12 ft by 12 ft with 8 ft walls, one door and two windows. You want two coats of a paint rated at 350 sq ft per gallon.

1. Perimeter: 12 + 12 + 12 + 12 = 48 ft.
2. Wall area: 48 × 8 = 384 sq ft.
3. Subtract openings: 384 − 21 (door) − 2 × 15 (windows) = **333 sq ft**.
4. Two coats: 333 × 2 = 666 sq ft of coverage.
5. Paint: 666 ÷ 350 = **1.9 gallons**, so buy **2 gallons**.

**Metric version:** a 4 × 3 m room with 2.4 m walls has 14 × 2.4 = 33.6 m², minus 1.9 m² and 1.4 m² = 30.3 m². Two coats at 8.6 m² per litre need about 7.05 litres, so three 2.5 L tins (7.5 L).

## How the paint calculation works

<code class="formula">paint = (perimeter × height − doors × door area − windows × window area) × coats ÷ coverage</code>

- **Coverage** is the area one gallon (or litre) covers in one coat. The default, 350 sq ft per gallon (about 8.6 m² per litre), is at the low end of typical interior wall paint ratings, which gives a little margin. Use the number on your can.
- **Door area** is assumed to be 21 sq ft (3 × 7 ft, about 1.9 m²) and **window area** 15 sq ft (3 × 5 ft, about 1.4 m²).
- **Limitations:** ceilings, trim and primer are not included. Textured or porous surfaces use more paint.
- **Safety:** homes built before 1978 may have lead-based paint. Follow EPA guidance before sanding or scraping old paint.
