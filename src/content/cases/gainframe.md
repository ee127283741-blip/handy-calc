A bathroom scale cannot tell you whether you lost fat or muscle. GainFrame tries to answer that with a phone photo. The solo-built iOS app launched in February 2026 and had $20,170 in lifetime revenue and $2,368 in MRR by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/gainframe)). Its founder is now trying to sell it.

## What does GainFrame do with a progress photo?

GainFrame turns a progress photo into a body fat estimate, muscle-group insights, side-by-side comparisons and an AI Coach grounded in the user's own check-ins ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/gainframe)). The listing says no account is required, photos are not stored on servers, and nutrition data comes from the fatsecret API.

The audience is anyone tracking fitness visually, with the listing naming moms and people for whom traditional tracking feels discouraging. The app is rated 4.9 out of 5 from 74 App Store ratings.

## Why is the GainFrame founder selling the app?

The founder, who signs his message as mjrode, says time is the constraint, not traction.

> "Selling because I'm a new dad with a full-time job and another project" — GainFrame founder ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/gainframe))

He adds that all current growth is organic, through SEO and App Store search, with no ad spend. The founder's full name is not disclosed.

## What lessons does GainFrame offer app founders?

- **Visual progress is a strong hook.** A photo-based check-in sells better than another calorie log.
- **Cheap plans cap MRR.** Hundreds of subscribers still produce a few thousand dollars a month.
- **Organic growth can carry a solo app.** SEO and App Store search did the work with no ad spend.
- **Founder time is a real limit.** A profitable app can still be sold when the owner has no hours left.
