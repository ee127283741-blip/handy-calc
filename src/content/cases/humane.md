Humane AI Pin sales are the reverse of most cases in this library. Humane raised more than $230M to build a screenless AI wearable ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/18/humanes-ai-pin-is-dead/)). By mid-2024 lifetime sales of the AI Pin were just over $9M, more than $1M had been returned, and for several months returns outpaced new sales, The Verge reported ([media-reported, Silicon Republic](https://siliconrepublic.com/?p=1089441)). In February 2025 HP bought the company's software, team and patents for $116M, and the Pin stopped working.

## What was the Humane AI Pin?

The AI Pin was a small device that clipped to a shirt, with no screen. Users talked to it, and it answered with a voice assistant, could take photos and projected simple text onto the palm of the hand. Imran Chaudhri and Bethany Bongiorno, both former Apple employees, founded Humane to build it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/18/humanes-ai-pin-is-dead/)). It launched at $699 plus a monthly subscription, and Humane cut the price to $499 in October 2024 ([media-reported, PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/software/ai/the-usd699-ai-pin-that-launched-less-than-a-year-ago-is-going-to-stop-working-at-the-end-of-february)). Team size and founding date are not disclosed.

## How much revenue did Humane AI Pin sales make?

Humane AI Pin sales brought in just over $9M in lifetime revenue by mid-2024, with more than $1M in returns, according to The Verge ([media-reported, Silicon Republic](https://siliconrepublic.com/?p=1089441)). At the $699 launch price, $9M is roughly 13,000 devices (calc), before returns. The Verge reported that between May and August 2024 more Pins were returned than sold. Humane disputed the report but did not give its own figures. Monthly revenue is not disclosed.

## What did HP buy from Humane for $116M?

HP paid $116M for Humane's AI software platform, most of its engineers and more than 300 patents, and the founders joined HP to lead a new AI group ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/18/humanes-ai-pin-is-dead/)). The AI Pin itself was not part of the deal. That price was less than half of what Humane had raised. Pins lost their server connection on February 28, 2025, and refunds were limited to devices still within the 90-day return window ([media-reported, PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/software/ai/the-usd699-ai-pin-that-launched-less-than-a-year-ago-is-going-to-stop-working-at-the-end-of-february)).

> "we are winding down the consumer Ai Pin as our business priorities have shifted." — Humane announcement ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/18/humanes-ai-pin-is-dead/))

## How does Humane compare with Limitless and Character.AI?

Humane, [Limitless](/cases/limitless/) and [Character.AI](/cases/character-ai/) all ended with a large tech company taking the useful parts. Limitless made an AI pendant that recorded conversations and was acquired by Meta in December 2025, which stopped selling the device to new customers. Character.AI licensed its technology to Google before filing for Chapter 11. Humane is the clearest hardware failure of the three: a $699 device whose returns overtook its sales.

Our reading: Humane asked people to replace a phone they already liked with a device that did less. Software AI startups in this library could ship weekly fixes to a free tier; Humane had sold a fixed piece of hardware at a high price, and every disappointed buyer could send it back. The money it raised went into a product that the market returned.

## What are the lessons from Humane for AI hardware?

- **Compete with the phone carefully.** A new device has to do something a phone cannot, not a subset of it.
- **Hardware returns are revenue in reverse.** Over $1M returned on about $9M of sales showed the product was not working for buyers.
- **Price follows proof.** Launching at $699 before the product was proven left no room when reviews were poor.
- **Assets can outlive the product.** HP paid $116M for patents, software and people, not for the Pin.
