Wave AI revenue reached about $7M a year, and the founder used to run Uber in New York, not write software. Josh Mohrer joined Uber at the end of 2011 as its New York general manager. Years later he built Wave AI, an app that records, transcribes and summarizes conversations, and told Mixergy in February 2026 that it was at about $7M a year ([founder-reported, Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/the-7-million-vibe-coded-app/)). Mixergy's headline calls it "The $7 Million Vibe Coded App." He runs it largely alone.

## Who is Josh Mohrer, and why does his Uber background matter for Wave AI?

Mohrer was an early leader at Uber and its New York general manager during the company's rapid expansion ([Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/the-7-million-vibe-coded-app/)). Mixergy describes him as not a developer who vibe coded Wave AI into a $7M-a-year note-taking company. Our reading: his background explains the business more than the code. Wave AI is a consumer subscription app with a paywall, and running it is closer to operating a market than to writing software.

## How fast did Wave AI's ARR grow after launch?

Wave AI grew quickly, then plateaued. Adapty's case study is headlined as reaching $2M ARR in six months ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/case-studies/wave/)). On a Lightspeed podcast in 2025, Mohrer said it hit $4M ARR within eight months of launch ([founder-reported, Lightspeed](https://rosetta.to/u/lightspeedvp/josh-mohrer-is-the-future-of-ai-businesses-a-solo-pursuit)). By February 2026 he told Mixergy it was at about $7M a year and had been flat for a few months ([founder-reported, Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/the-7-million-vibe-coded-app/)). That is about $583K a month (calc). Total revenue is not disclosed.

## Is the Wave AI founder a developer or not?

Sources disagree on the label. Mixergy says Mohrer is not a developer ([Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/the-7-million-vibe-coded-app/)), while Adapty frames Wave as an indie app built by a solo developer ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/case-studies/wave/)). Both can be true: he did not come from engineering, and AI tools turned him into the person who ships the code. He also told Mixergy that contractors have helped on specialized engineering and marketing.

## How does one person run Wave AI with Claude Code?

Mohrer said AI tools, especially Claude Code, let him handle engineering, customer support and product development himself ([Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/the-7-million-vibe-coded-app/)). One example: he asks Claude Code to find a feature in the codebase and write the help-center document for it.

> "I love Claude code. I love it." — Josh Mohrer ([founder-reported, Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/the-7-million-vibe-coded-app/))

He also answers user emails himself. Team size beyond contractors is not disclosed.

## How does Wave AI compare with Otter.ai and Granola?

[Otter.ai](/cases/otter-ai/) and [Granola](/cases/granola/) sell AI meeting notes too, but both are venture-backed teams. Otter.ai reported passing nine figures of ARR with fewer than 200 employees; Granola raised large rounds and keeps revenue private. Wave AI is a consumer phone app with a paywall, run by one person. [Fireflies.ai](/cases/fireflies-ai/) is closer in spirit, since it says it has been profitable without new capital, but it still has a full company behind it.

Our reading: the note-taker market is large enough that a solo founder can reach millions in revenue on mobile while the funded players fight over teams and enterprises. Wave's plateau also shows the limit of that model.

## What lessons does Wave AI teach solo founders?

- **Operators can ship software now.** A former Uber general manager runs a multimillion-dollar app with AI coding tools.
- **Pick a crowded but huge category.** Note taking already had big players, and Wave still found paying users.
- **Use AI beyond code.** Mohrer uses Claude Code for documentation and support too.
- **Plateaus come fast.** Growth from $2M to about $7M was followed by a flat few months.
