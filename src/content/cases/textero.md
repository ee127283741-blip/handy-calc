One day Textero had 10,000 daily clicks from Google. The next day it had dropped out of the rankings, hit by Google's October core update. That was one of many shocks for founder Nick Yasynskyi, whose first product is an AI writing assistant for students. Even so, by November 2024 it averaged $20,000 in monthly revenue ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/textero)).

## What gap did Textero's founder see after university?

Yasynskyi started Textero a few years after graduating, when he still remembered what students struggle with. Designers browse pictures for inspiration; he asked why students could not get ideas from AI before writing a paper. ChatGPT was already popular, but he wanted something more collaborative, where you could generate ideas and keep working with them inside the app.

Textero helps students research and draft academic writing. After a relaunch in 2023 it added more languages and access to more than 215 million academic sources.

## How did Textero launch with almost no budget?

He bought a domain and built the first version with a developer friend and a few outsourced developers in about a month. Money was tight, so they did as much as possible themselves. Textero was free for its first two months, partly because he could not decide on a business model and partly to gather feedback and find bugs, of which there were plenty.

Early users found it through Google search. As usage grew, the team listed Textero on AI tool directories for more traffic, then moved to a freemium model with a limited free version.

> "Every day started with a challenge and ended with five more." — Nick Yasynskyi ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/textero))

## What knocked Textero's growth off course?

Three things. First, seasonality: when the paid version launched, daily active users dropped 20%, because students study in waves. Second, Google: the October core update removed Textero from the rankings, and the team had to cut most of the homepage content to recover. Third, conversion: the free tier attracted users, but few upgraded, and Yasynskyi says he is still working on it.

Competition added a fourth problem in 2024. Rivals were shipping dozens of features while Textero had only a few, so it merged with AHelp, a learning hub that had been on the market since 2011, to gain access to more tools and technology.

## How much revenue does Textero AI make?

Textero's average monthly revenue was $20,000 in November 2024, with some months higher and some lower in line with the academic year ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/textero)). The team had grown to seven employees besides the founder. Total revenue is not disclosed.

| Channel | How Textero used it |
|---|---|
| Google search | First customers; up to 10,000 daily organic clicks before a core update |
| Google Ads | Paid traffic once the potential was clear |
| AI directories | Early extra traffic |

| Pinterest, Reddit, X | Writing guides, answering feedback, writing tips |

## How does Textero compare with Jenni AI and AiApply?

[Jenni AI](/cases/jenni-ai/) is Textero's most direct competitor: both help students and researchers write academic papers with citations. Jenni AI reached its audience through many short-video accounts and grew larger; Textero leaned on Google search until an algorithm update showed how fragile one channel can be. Both found that academic writing has peaks and valleys tied to the school calendar.

[AiApply](/cases/aiapply/) serves the next stage of the same user's life, job hunting, and also sells monthly plans to people with a deadline. Its founders reported a 98% gross margin and a mix of paid and organic channels, which is the kind of channel spread Textero had to build after its Google shock.

## What lessons does Textero teach?

- **Never depend on one channel.** A single Google update erased 10,000 daily clicks.
- **Plan for seasonality.** Student products rise and fall with the academic year.
- **Free users are not paying users.** Freemium brought traffic; conversion stayed the hardest problem.
- **Partner when competitors outpace you.** Merging with AHelp gave Textero tools it could not build fast enough.
