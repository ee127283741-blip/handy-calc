Plinq revenue reached R$2.2M ARR three months after launch, and its founder had never written a line of code. Sabrine Matos, a growth marketer in Brazil, built the women's safety app's website, desktop app and backend on Lovable, an AI app builder ([founder-reported, Judit](https://judit.io/blog/estudos-de-caso/plinq-usa-judit-api-e-combate-ao-feminicidio-com-dados-judiciais/)). Plinq lets a woman check whether a date or partner has a criminal or court record before meeting them. In a country with high rates of violence against women, that turned out to be something many people would pay for.

## How did Sabrine Matos build Plinq without coding?

Sabrine Matos built Plinq by describing each screen and feature to Lovable, which generated the code. Lovable's customer story says she launched the first version in 45 days and refined it with feedback from beta users ([founder-reported, Lovable](https://lovable.dev/blog/customer-stories/how-sabrine-matos-built-plinq)). The Judit case study says she had never programmed and worked in growth marketing. Plinq pulls public court data through Judit's API, so Matos did not need to build a records database herself. Team size is not disclosed.

> "Se o Lovable não existisse, a Plinq nunca teria visto a luz do dia." — Sabrine Matos ([founder-reported, Judit](https://judit.io/blog/estudos-de-caso/plinq-usa-judit-api-e-combate-ao-feminicidio-com-dados-judiciais/))

In English: without Lovable, Plinq would never have seen the light of day.

## How does Plinq make money, and how much?

Plinq makes money per search: a full report cost R$27 ([media-reported, Startups.com.br](https://startups.com.br/negocios/plinq-capta-r-13m-para-lancar-app-de-seguranca-para-mulheres/)). The company reported R$2.2M ARR three months after launch, after monthly growth of about 300% in the early phase, and R$500K in revenue over its first six months ([founder-reported, Judit](https://judit.io/blog/estudos-de-caso/plinq-usa-judit-api-e-combate-ao-feminicidio-com-dados-judiciais/)). Lovable's showcase put ARR at about $456K with 10,000 users ([media-reported, AI Eating the World](https://www.aieatingtheworld.com/articles/non-technical-founder-lovable-app-builder-success-story)), or about $38K a month (calc). The two ARR numbers come from different dates and currencies, so we list both.

## Why did Lovable's CEO invest in Plinq?

Plinq is the kind of company Lovable wants to exist: a real business built by someone who could not have hired engineers. Startups.com.br reported that Plinq raised R$1.3M led by Spectra, with Lovable CEO Anton Osika investing personally, and that it had more than 45,000 users and 50,000 searches in under six months ([media-reported, Startups.com.br](https://startups.com.br/negocios/plinq-capta-r-13m-para-lancar-app-de-seguranca-para-mulheres/)). For Lovable, Plinq is proof that its product works for non-developers.

## How does Plinq compare with other no-code AI founders like Magai and Formula Bot?

Plinq, [Magai](/cases/magai/) and [Formula Bot](/cases/formula-bot/) were all built by founders who could not code. Magai's Dustin Stout and Formula Bot's David Bressler used earlier no-code tools; Sabrine Matos used an AI builder, [Lovable](/cases/lovable/), that writes real code from chat. The bigger difference is the customer. Magai and Formula Bot sell productivity subscriptions to knowledge workers. Plinq sells a one-off safety check to women, a need that comes from news and fear rather than work.

Our reading: the founder's skill here was marketing, not engineering. Matos knew how to reach her audience, and Lovable removed the part she could not do. The product also leans on a data partner, Judit, for the hardest technical piece, which is a sensible pattern for non-technical founders.

[ShiftNex](/cases/shiftnex/) is another Lovable-built business from an industry insider, a healthcare staffing platform for US facilities.

## What can non-technical founders learn from Plinq?

- **Marketing skill can be the founder's edge.** A growth marketer reached tens of thousands of users once AI handled the code.
- **Rent the hard part.** Plinq uses Judit's court-data API instead of building its own records system.
- **Charge per use when the need is urgent.** A R$27 report matches a one-time worry better than a subscription.
- **Ship in weeks, then listen.** Plinq launched in 45 days and improved with beta-user feedback.
