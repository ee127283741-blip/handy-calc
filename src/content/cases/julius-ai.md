Julius AI funding came after an unusual endorsement: Harvard Business School. The business school adopted Julius, an AI data analyst, for its required Data Science and AI for Leaders course, taught to more than 900 students ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/15/fake-fired-twitter-worker-rahul-ligma-is-a-real-engineer-with-an-ai-data-startup-used-by-harvard)). Two months later Julius raised a $10M seed led by Bessemer. Its founder, Rahul Sonwalkar, was best known online for a 2022 prank. Julius has not published revenue, so this case is about traction and funding.

## Who is Rahul Sonwalkar, Julius AI's founder?

Rahul Sonwalkar built Julius after Y Combinator, where he had started a logistics company before switching to AI data analysis ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/15/fake-fired-twitter-worker-rahul-ligma-is-a-real-engineer-with-an-ai-data-startup-used-by-harvard)). In 2022 he went viral as "Rahul Ligma," posing as a laid-off Twitter employee outside its offices when Elon Musk took over. TechCrunch's 2025 profile revealed that the prankster was a working engineer whose startup Harvard used. Team size, founding date and prices are not disclosed.

> "I wanted to build something that would make data science very accessible to everyone," — Rahul Sonwalkar ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/15/fake-fired-twitter-worker-rahul-ligma-is-a-real-engineer-with-an-ai-data-startup-used-by-harvard))

## What does Julius AI do?

Julius AI lets users upload a spreadsheet or dataset and ask questions in plain English. It writes and runs the analysis code, then returns charts, tables and explanations. The target user is someone who needs answers from data but does not write Python or SQL, such as a business student or analyst. Harvard Business School used it so MBA students could analyze data without first learning to code ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/15/fake-fired-twitter-worker-rahul-ligma-is-a-real-engineer-with-an-ai-data-startup-used-by-harvard)).

## Does Julius AI make money, and how much has it raised?

Julius AI's revenue and ARR are not disclosed. It has not published figures. Its funding is public: a $10M seed led by Bessemer Venture Partners in July 2025, when it reported over 2 million registered users ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/28/ai-data-analyst-startup-julius-nabs-10m-seed-round/)). ## How does Julius AI compare with Formula Bot and Consensus?

Julius AI and [Formula Bot](/cases/formula-bot/) both help people who are stuck in spreadsheets. Formula Bot started by writing Excel formulas from plain-English requests and grew into data analysis; Julius starts at analysis and charts. [Consensus](/cases/consensus/) is another narrow AI tool for an academic audience, answering questions from research papers, and it raised an $11.5M Series A on about $1.5M in annualized revenue ([founder-reported, Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-14/consensus-nabs-11-million-to-build-ai-search-tool-for-academics)). Julius raised a similar amount without publishing revenue.

Our reading: Julius competes with the data features inside ChatGPT and other assistants, which can also run code on uploaded files. A course adoption at Harvard is a credibility signal that general assistants do not get, and students who learn on Julius may keep using it at work.

## What are the lessons from Julius AI for AI tools in education?

- **One respected customer can open doors.** Harvard Business School's adoption gave Julius credibility before its seed round.
- **Plain English beats code for many users.** Julius sells analysis to people who do not write Python or SQL.
- **Users are not revenue.** Over 2 million registered users is reach; paid conversion is still not disclosed.
- **General assistants are the competitor.** Julius has to stay better than built-in data tools in ChatGPT-style products.
