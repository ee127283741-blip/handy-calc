Wrestle AI revenue reached about $17K a month for an app built by a college freshman who had never coded. George Lampropoulos, 18, used Rork, an AI tool that builds mobile apps from prompts, to make an AI wrestling coach. He told Starter Story the app makes about $17K a month ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/how-i-grew-my-mobile-app-to-17k-per-month)). Rork's own case study puts revenue at $131K over six months, against about $1.1K of spending ([founder-reported, Rork](https://rork.com/blog/george-wrestle-ai-case-study)).

## How did George Lampropoulos build Wrestle AI with no coding experience?

George Lampropoulos built Wrestle AI by describing screens and features to Rork, which generated the mobile app, and used ChatGPT for guidance when he got stuck ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/how-i-grew-my-mobile-app-to-17k-per-month)). The backend runs on Supabase and the AI features on OpenAI models. He went from idea to App Store launch in about a month, launching in September 2025. Team size is not disclosed.

> "launched an app that makes 17K per month" — George Lampropoulos ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/how-i-grew-my-mobile-app-to-17k-per-month))

## What does Wrestle AI earn from subscriptions?

Wrestle AI makes about $17K a month, its founder says, from subscriptions at $9.99 a month or $59.99 a year ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/how-i-grew-my-mobile-app-to-17k-per-month)). Rork's case study reports $131K in total revenue over six months and about $1.1K in total spending ([founder-reported, Rork](https://rork.com/blog/george-wrestle-ai-case-study)). Rork is the tool he used, so its case study is promotional. Both figures are self-reported.

## Why does a niche sports app work for Wrestle AI?

A niche sport has a small, passionate audience that big fitness apps ignore. Wrestlers care about technique and train in teams and clubs, where word spreads quickly. An AI coach for that audience can grow through social media and word of mouth. Rork's case study credits more than 2 million social media impressions and over 17,000 downloads ([Rork](https://rork.com/blog/george-wrestle-ai-case-study)).

## How does Wrestle AI compare with Cal AI and STOPPR?

Wrestle AI, [Cal AI](/cases/cal-ai/) and [STOPPR](/cases/stoppr/) are all mobile subscription apps built by young or first-time founders and grown through social media. Cal AI's founders could code and built a calorie app for a mass market. STOPPR's founder, an ex-quant trader, cloned an existing app with Cursor. Wrestle AI's founder could not code at all and picked a narrow sport he knew personally. Rork's monthly fee and a few API costs were the whole tech budget.

Our reading: Wrestle AI is what AI app builders make possible: a teenager turning personal expertise into a paid app without any upfront investment. The audience is small, so growth may level off, but tool costs of only a few hundred dollars a month at most mean even a modest user base is very profitable.

Two more Rork-built sports coaches follow the same pattern: [3AK Track](/cases/3ak-track/), from two college sprinters with a large audience, and [Hockey AI](/cases/hockey-ai/), from brothers aged 14 and 17.

## What can teen and first-time founders learn from Wrestle AI?

- **Pick a niche big apps ignore.** A wrestling coach serves a passionate audience general fitness apps overlook.
- **Costs can be tiny.** The tech stack is a few monthly subscriptions, not engineers' salaries.
- **Niche beats crowded.** Wrestling is too small for big fitness companies but big enough for one founder.
- **Social media is the launch channel.** Rork reports more than 2 million impressions and 17,000+ downloads.
