Obsidian users keep years of notes in plain files on their own machines, and many of them want an AI that can read those notes. Logan Yang's Copilot for Obsidian started as an open-source plugin for that job, and Brevilabs is the company built around it. In November 2026 Brevilabs showed $17,901 in MRR and $920,956 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brevilabs)).

## What does Brevilabs make besides Copilot for Obsidian?

Brevilabs lists three products on its website: Copilot for Obsidian, an AI assistant that works inside an Obsidian vault; miyo, a local context hub that lets AI tools use the files a person chooses; and OpenArtifacts, a shared surface where people and agents publish and refine work ([founder-reported, Brevilabs](https://brevilabs.com)). The company also runs an AI product consultancy staffed by engineers it describes as ex-Google and ex-Uber.

> "Tools for any AI agent to access and manage your personal context" — Brevilabs, company description ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brevilabs))

## How big is the Copilot for Obsidian open-source project?

The plugin's GitHub repository, logancyang/obsidian-copilot, showed about 8,000 stars and an AGPL-3.0 license in November 2026 ([verified, GitHub](https://github.com/logancyang/obsidian-copilot)). Its current description is about running agents such as OpenCode, Codex and Claude Code inside Obsidian. The page says multi-agent use requires active Plus access, which is how the free plugin connects to paid accounts.

TrustMRR lists Logan Yang as the founder, January 2025 as the founding date and the United States as the country. The team size is not disclosed.

## What does Brevilabs charge for software and consulting?

Brevilabs sells at two very different price points. Copilot Plus, the paid tier, is not priced on the pages we checked. The consulting work is priced in public ([founder-reported, Brevilabs](https://brevilabs.com)).

| Offer | Price (Nov 2026) | Source |

## How much money does Brevilabs make from subscriptions?

Brevilabs made $17,901 in MRR and $18,146 over the last 30 days in November 2026, from 1,357 active subscriptions ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brevilabs)). All-time revenue was $920,956.

MRR divided by subscriptions is about $13.19 ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brevilabs)), a consumer software price, so the subscriptions look like Copilot Plus users rather than consulting clients. All-time revenue is about 51 times current MRR, even though TrustMRR dates the company to January 2025. Revenue was probably higher in earlier months or included large one-time payments, but the listing does not say which.

## How does Brevilabs compare with TypingMind and Magai?

[TypingMind](/cases/typingmind/) sells a better chat interface for AI models, and [Magai](/cases/magai/) sells a no-code platform for many models in one place. Both put the user in a new app. Brevilabs brings AI into an app the user already lives in, their Obsidian vault.

Our reading: that is a narrower audience but a loyal one, because Obsidian users chose local files on purpose. The open-source plugin works as free distribution through Obsidian's plugin directory, and the consultancy is a second business that uses the same AI skills.

## What lessons does Brevilabs offer open-source builders?

- **Ship where users already work.** Copilot for Obsidian lives inside the vault ([verified, GitHub](https://github.com/logancyang/obsidian-copilot)).
- **Open source can feed a paid tier.** Plus access unlocks the advanced agent features.
- **Low prices need many subscribers.** About $13 per subscription across 1,357 accounts.
- **Expertise can be sold twice.** The same team sells AI consulting at $45k a sprint ([founder-reported, Brevilabs](https://brevilabs.com)).
