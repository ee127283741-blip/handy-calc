Most job tools help you apply faster. ApplyAll applies for you, and refunds you if no interview follows. That model shows up oddly on a revenue dashboard: ApplyAll had just $2,280 in MRR in November 2026, but it collected $26,677 in the last 30 days and $878,842 all time ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/applyall)). This is a one-time service business that happens to have a small subscription line.

## What does ApplyAll do for job seekers?

ApplyAll handles the application process for tech, corporate and knowledge workers in the US and Canada. The listing says it "helps job seekers secure interviews and job offers by handling the application process for them" ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/applyall)). The homepage says it does not blast applications and instead targets roles using signals from more than 2,000,000 applications sent, with human review on top of AI ([founder-reported, ApplyAll homepage](https://www.applyall.com/)).

> "Real interviews. Real companies. Or your money back." — ApplyAll listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/applyall))

The guarantee is specific: if a customer gets no interview within 30 days of application completion, ApplyAll says it refunds every dollar. The homepage also claims a 94% interview rate and 4.8 stars from 60+ recent reviews, all company claims.

## How does ApplyAll make money with only $2,280 MRR?

ApplyAll makes most of its money from one-time packages. The listing gives Standard at $249 and Premium at $299, both one-time ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/applyall)). Daily verified revenue in the snapshot often lands at exactly $299 or multiples of it, which matches those packages, while 18 active subscriptions make up the $2,280 MRR.

## Where do ApplyAll customers come from?

They come mainly through referrals and partners. The listing names word of mouth, affiliates and LinkedIn as marketing channels ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/applyall)). TrustMRR showed 14,008 visitors in the last 30 days and $1.88 of revenue per visitor, measured through connected Google Analytics, and an estimated 4,000 users.

The build is active. TrustMRR showed 507 GitHub contributions in the last 30 days and 3,556 in the last year from a connected repository, on a stack of TypeScript, Svelte, PostgreSQL, Elasticsearch and Google Cloud. Elasticsearch suggests job search and matching are a core part of the system.

## How does ApplyAll compare with AIApply and Rezi?

All three sell to job seekers, but ApplyAll is the only one that does the applying and guarantees an outcome.

Our observation: [AIApply](/cases/aiapply/) and [Rezi](/cases/rezi/) sell software on subscription and let the user do the work. ApplyAll charges more than ten times AIApply's monthly price once, and takes on refund risk in exchange. Job searches end, so a one-time price fits the need better than a subscription that customers cancel after they are hired.

## What lessons can founders learn from ApplyAll?

- **Match the price to the need.** Job hunting is a project, so ApplyAll charges $249 or $299 once rather than monthly ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/applyall)).
- **A guarantee sells a service.** A money-back promise tied to interviews lowers the risk of a high upfront price.
- **MRR can hide a business.** At $2,280 MRR ApplyAll looks tiny; its $878,842 all-time revenue tells the real story.
- **Referrals fit outcome products.** Customers who land interviews share results, which is why word of mouth and affiliates lead its channel list.
