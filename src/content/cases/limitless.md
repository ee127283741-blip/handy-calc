The Limitless AI acquisition ended a startup that changed products twice in a few years. It began as Rewind, a Mac app that recorded everything on a user's screen and made it searchable. It became Limitless, maker of a pendant that records and transcribes conversations in the real world. In December 2025 Meta acquired it on undisclosed terms ([media-reported, Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/12/06/meta-acquires-ai-wearables-startup-limitless)), and Limitless stopped selling the pendant to new customers. Its revenue was never published.

## How did Rewind become Limitless?

Rewind and Limitless were built by Dan Siroker, the co-founder and former CEO of Optimizely, and Brett Bejcek ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/05/meta-acquires-ai-device-startup-limitless)). Rewind recorded a computer screen and audio so users could search anything they had seen or heard. The company then moved to a wearable: a small pendant that records meetings and conversations, transcribes them and lets users ask questions about their day. The name changed with the product. Team size, founding date and pendant prices are not disclosed in the sources we checked.

## How much money did Limitless raise before Meta bought it?

Limitless raised over $33M from investors including a16z, First Round Capital and NEA ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/05/meta-acquires-ai-device-startup-limitless)). Its revenue and the price Meta paid are not disclosed. Without either number, outsiders cannot tell whether the sale returned more or less than investors put in. Siroker announced the deal in a blog post.

> "We share this vision and we'll be joining Meta to help bring our shared vision to life." — Dan Siroker ([founder-reported, Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/12/06/meta-acquires-ai-wearables-startup-limitless))

## What did Meta want from Limitless?

Meta wanted the team and technology for its AI wearables. Reuters reported that the Limitless team would join the wearables group in Meta's Reality Labs, which makes the Ray-Ban Meta glasses ([media-reported, Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/12/06/meta-acquires-ai-wearables-startup-limitless)). After the deal Limitless stopped selling pendants to new buyers, kept supporting existing owners and moved them to its Unlimited plan for free, and shut down other products, including the Rewind desktop app ([TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/meta-acquires-limitless-pendant-users-moved-to-free-unlimited-plan/)).

## How does Limitless compare with Humane and Bluedot?

Limitless and [Humane](/cases/humane/) both built AI hardware and both sold to a tech giant. Humane raised more than $230M and sold its assets to HP for $116M after AI Pin returns outpaced sales ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/18/humanes-ai-pin-is-dead/)). Limitless raised far less, about $33M, and its pendant did one narrow job: recording and summarizing conversations. [Bluedot](/cases/bluedot/) does a similar job in software, recording online meetings through a Chrome extension with no device at all.

Our reading: a recording pendant is useful but hard to sell as a standalone business, because phones and meeting apps can do much of the same work. Limitless's value to Meta was the team and know-how for always-on audio AI, which matters more inside a company that already sells wearables at scale.

## What are the takeaways from Limitless for AI wearables?

- **Pivots can find a buyer.** Rewind's move from desktop app to pendant led to a sale to Meta.
- **Narrow hardware needs a big platform.** A recording pendant fits better inside a wearables maker than on its own.
- **Raise less, risk less.** About $33M of funding left Limitless room to sell without a Humane-style write-down.
- **Customers feel the exit.** New sales stopped and the Rewind app was shut down after the deal.
