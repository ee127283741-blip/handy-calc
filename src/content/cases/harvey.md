Two roommates, one a young litigator and the other an AI researcher, sat with an early version of GPT-3 and asked whether it could do the work junior lawyers do. Four years later their answer was a company valued at $15.5B ([founder-reported, Harvey blog](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-dollar550m-at-a-dollar155b-valuation-to-help-legal-teams-own-their-intelligence)). Harvey came out of stealth in 2022 with a five-person team ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/23/harvey-which-uses-ai-to-answer-legal-questions-lands-cash-from-openai/)). By August 2025 it reported more than $100M ARR ([founder-reported, Harvey blog](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harveys-three-year-anniversary)). This is the story of how a product built for the most cautious buyers in professional services grew that fast, and what it cost to get there.

## How did a litigator and an AI researcher start Harvey?

Winston Weinberg was a securities and antitrust litigator at O'Melveny & Myers, and Gabe Pereyra had been a research scientist at DeepMind, Google Brain and Meta AI ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/23/harvey-which-uses-ai-to-answer-legal-questions-lands-cash-from-openai/)). They were roommates, and Pereyra showed Weinberg GPT-3. Weinberg had practiced law for under a year, and he felt that much of what associates did could have been done before law school ([media-reported, Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/aliciapark/2024/08/08/why-openai-and-google-are-betting-on-this-ai-unicorn-with-a-100-million-deal/)).

The pain point was volume. Transactional and litigation work keeps getting more complex: a merger can involve hundreds of thousands of contracts, and a lawsuit can involve millions of emails ([founder-reported, OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harvey/)). The founders' insight was that language models could synthesize that material while lawyers reviewed the output. To test it, they took landlord and tenant questions from r/legaladvice, generated answers with GPT-3 and showed them to attorneys. For 86 of 100 answers, the attorneys said they would send the answer to a client without editing it ([founder-reported, OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harvey/)). The company describes that result as its "aha moment".

> "We’ve believed from the start that legal and professional services are uniquely suited for transformation with AI." — Gabe Pereyra and Winston Weinberg ([founder-reported, Harvey](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harveys-three-year-anniversary))

## What does Harvey do inside a law firm?

At launch, TechCrunch described Harvey as a copilot for lawyers ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/23/harvey-which-uses-ai-to-answer-legal-questions-lands-cash-from-openai/)). Instead of editing documents by hand, a lawyer writes instructions in plain language. Examples included asking for the difference between an employee and a contractor in the Fourth Circuit, or checking whether a lease clause complies with California law and rewriting it if it does not. In its beta, Harvey was meant to be used under the supervision of a licensed attorney. The company said it anonymized data, deleted it after a set period and kept clients' data separate from each other.

Since then the product has grown into a platform for law, tax and finance professionals ([founder-reported, OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harvey/)). Its custom case-law model, described below, is designed so that every sentence is supported by a cited case. By March 2026 the company said customers had built more than 25,000 custom agents on it ([founder-reported, Harvey blog](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-at-dollar11-billion-valuation-to-scale-agents-across-law-firms-and-enterprises)). The idea behind it has stayed the same: the model does the reading and drafting, and the lawyer is still the one who signs off.

Harvey started with OpenAI's models and grew closer to OpenAI over time. About two weeks after the founders emailed OpenAI about their legal Q&A chatbot, Weinberg was on a call with OpenAI's c-suite on July 4, 2022 ([media-reported, Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/aliciapark/2024/08/08/why-openai-and-google-are-betting-on-this-ai-unicorn-with-a-100-million-deal/)). He moonlighted on the company while still litigating, working more than 120 hours a week. The seed round was $5M, led by the OpenAI Startup Fund, with Jeff Dean and Elad Gil also investing ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/23/harvey-which-uses-ai-to-answer-legal-questions-lands-cash-from-openai/)).

The biggest technical step came after fine-tuning and retrieval-augmented generation stopped being enough. Harvey and OpenAI built a custom-trained case-law model together. They started with Delaware case law, then added all US case law, about 10 billion tokens of data ([founder-reported, OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harvey/)). In tests with 10 of the largest law firms, lawyers preferred its output over GPT-4 97% of the time. The team also grew through senior hires, including former Google lawyers and former Wachtell partner Gordon Moodie as chief product officer ([media-reported, Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/aliciapark/2024/08/08/why-openai-and-google-are-betting-on-this-ai-unicorn-with-a-100-million-deal/)). Later it added CTO Siva Gurumurthy and chief business officer John Haddock ([media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/04/legal-ai-startup-harvey-revenue.html)).

## How does Harvey sell to the world's largest law firms?

Harvey's marketing strategy was to win over prestige first. The founders went after the largest and most prestigious firms on purpose, because trust decides who buys in law ([media-reported, Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/aliciapark/2024/08/08/why-openai-and-google-are-betting-on-this-ai-unicorn-with-a-100-million-deal/)). Its first client was A&O Shearman and its second was PwC. The name has a story too: CNBC reported that it was partly inspired by a character in the TV drama "Suits" ([media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/04/legal-ai-startup-harvey-revenue.html)).

The sales tactic was a personalized demo. Harvey would find a prospect's latest public filing and use the product to generate counterarguments against it. A partner reading a sharp rebuttal of their own brief understands the product right away.

On pricing, Harvey sells enterprise seats. Accounts typically buy a couple hundred seats and then expand, so revenue grows with usage ([media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/04/legal-ai-startup-harvey-revenue.html)). Customers include Comcast. Specific prices are not disclosed. By August 2025 Harvey reported 500+ customers in 54 countries and 42% of the AmLaw 100 ([founder-reported, Harvey blog](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harveys-three-year-anniversary)). By September 2026 it said 80% of the Am Law 100 and five of the Fortune 10 used it ([founder-reported, Harvey blog](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-dollar550m-at-a-dollar155b-valuation-to-help-legal-teams-own-their-intelligence)).

## What makes legal AI a hard business for Harvey?

From the start, the central risk was that the model would get things wrong. At launch, observers raised concerns about confidentiality and about models making up facts ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/23/harvey-which-uses-ai-to-answer-legal-questions-lands-cash-from-openai/)). In law those failures cause real harm: Forbes pointed to hallucinated cases showing up in court filings, including the Michael Cohen example ([media-reported, Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/aliciapark/2024/08/08/why-openai-and-google-are-betting-on-this-ai-unicorn-with-a-100-million-deal/)). Harvey's response was technical and procedural. It built answers that cite a case for every sentence, required attorney supervision and promised to keep client data separate.

Competition was the second pressure. Casetext and Klarity were early rivals, and Casetext was later bought by Thomson Reuters for $650M, which put a large incumbent behind a competing product ([media-reported, Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/aliciapark/2024/08/08/why-openai-and-google-are-betting-on-this-ai-unicorn-with-a-100-million-deal/)). Spellbook was another competitor. The third pressure was growth itself. Weinberg described it as having to scale the whole experience every six months ([media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/04/legal-ai-startup-harvey-revenue.html)). A company that went from five people to 350 employees by August 2025 ([founder-reported, Harvey blog](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harveys-three-year-anniversary)) has to keep rebuilding how it operates.

## How much ARR does Harvey generate?

At launch, Harvey declined to share revenue ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/23/harvey-which-uses-ai-to-answer-legal-questions-lands-cash-from-openai/)). The first signal was growth rather than a dollar amount: revenue rose more than 10x in 2023 ([founder-reported, OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harvey/)). In August 2025 the company reported more than $100M ARR ([founder-reported, Harvey blog](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harveys-three-year-anniversary)), which CNBC described as driven by usage. By January 2026 ARR was $190M, up from $100M in August ([founder-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/25/legal-ai-startup-harvey-raises-200-million-at-11-billion-valuation.html)). A September 2026 report put ARR above $400M ([unverified, SaaS Sentinel](https://saassentinel.com/2026/09/10/harvey-raises-550m-at-15-5b-valuation-as-legal-ai-arr-tops-400m/)), but that figure does not appear in the company's own announcement. Monthly revenue and total revenue are not disclosed. Dividing ARR by twelve gives only a rough estimate, not a reported monthly figure.

Here is the timeline. In 2022 the founders contacted OpenAI, and Harvey came out of stealth with a $5M seed and five people ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/23/harvey-which-uses-ai-to-answer-legal-questions-lands-cash-from-openai/)). In 2023 revenue grew more than 10x, and a Series B raised $80M at a $715M valuation ([founder-reported, OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harvey/)). In July 2024 a $100M Series C valued Harvey at $1.5B, bringing total funding to $216M ([media-reported, Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/aliciapark/2024/08/08/why-openai-and-google-are-betting-on-this-ai-unicorn-with-a-100-million-deal/)). By August 2025 total funding was above $800M ([media-reported, PitchBook via CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/04/legal-ai-startup-harvey-revenue.html)). A December 2025 round reportedly valued Harvey at $8B ([media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/25/legal-ai-startup-harvey-raises-200-million-at-11-billion-valuation.html)). In March 2026 it raised $200M at $11B, bringing total funding above $1B ([founder-reported, Harvey blog](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-at-dollar11-billion-valuation-to-scale-agents-across-law-firms-and-enterprises)). In September 2026 it raised $550M at $15.5B ([founder-reported, Harvey blog](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-dollar550m-at-a-dollar155b-valuation-to-help-legal-teams-own-their-intelligence)).

## How does Harvey compare with Instantly and Gamma?

Harvey is the clearest venture-scale case in this library, and the contrast with bootstrapped companies is stark. [Instantly](/cases/instantly/) reached $2.4M ARR with no outside money by selling cheap software to many small agencies ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)). Harvey sells expensive enterprise seats to a few hundred large firms and raised more than $800M by August 2025 to do it. Long sales cycles, security reviews and custom work make that capital necessary.

[Gamma](/cases/gamma/) sits in between. It also passed $100M ARR in 2025, but with about 50 people and $23M in initial funding ([founder-reported, Gamma](https://gamma.app/insights/how-we-built-a-usd100m-business-differently)), because product-led growth sells itself. Harvey had 350 employees at the same revenue milestone. The difference is the buyer: a law firm buys through partners, IT and compliance, not through a free trial.

[EchoBase AI](/cases/echobase-ai/) tried to sell AI to consultants on a tiny budget and shut down in 2024, which shows the other end of the professional-services market.

For other AI companies selling large enterprise contracts, see [Glean](/cases/glean/) in workplace search and [Abridge](/cases/abridge/) in health systems.

## What lessons does Harvey offer founders?

- **Pair domain expertise with AI expertise.** A litigator and an AI researcher could build what neither could alone.
- **Win the most demanding customers first.** Large law firms became Harvey's proof for everyone else.
- **Enterprise AI needs capital.** Security, compliance and long sales cycles explain the large rounds.
- **Model access is not the moat.** Workflows, data sources and trust inside firms are what customers pay for.
