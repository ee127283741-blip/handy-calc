Shipper revenue is a story about non-technical founders selling to non-technical founders. Brothers David and Daniel, based in London, say they never liked programming. They built Shipper, an AI app builder, so people like them can describe an app in plain English and get working software. David reported $13,800 ARR 69 days after the August 2025 launch, with no paid ads ([founder-reported, Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/@chhddavid/activity)). Latka later estimated about $330K in 2025 revenue ([third-party estimate, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/shipper.now)).

## Who are the brothers behind Shipper?

David and Daniel have built SaaS products together since 2019 and say they have built, scaled and sold several companies. Stork's profile reports that they openly call themselves non-technical ([Stork](https://www.stork.ai/blog/the-billion-dollar-copycat-playbook)). Latka lists David as CEO, the company in London and a team of three ([third-party estimate, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/shipper.now)). Shipper launched on Product Hunt on August 6, 2025, finishing seventh of the day, and released Shipper 2.0 in February 2026 ([Hunted Space](https://hunted.space/dashboard/shipper-now/launches/shipper-2-0)). Prices are not disclosed.

> "Build web/mobile apps, sites and extensions by talking to AI" — Shipper 2.0 launch tagline ([founder-reported, Hunted Space](https://hunted.space/dashboard/shipper-now/launches/shipper-2-0))

## What revenue does Shipper actually make?

Shipper's revenue depends on which source you trust. David's own early update was $13,800 ARR 69 days after launch ([founder-reported, Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/@chhddavid/activity)). Latka estimated about $330K for 2025 ([third-party estimate, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/shipper.now)). Stork reported over $2M in annual revenue with zero employees but gave no primary source, so we label that figure unverified ([unverified, Stork](https://www.stork.ai/blog/the-billion-dollar-copycat-playbook)). Monthly revenue is not disclosed.

## Why did non-technical founders build an app builder?

They built the tool they wished they had. Shipper's pitch is the same as the vibe-coding leaders: describe the product, and AI writes, tests and deploys the code. Founders who could not code understand the frustrations of that customer better than engineers do. The brothers' earlier SaaS experience also gave them a playbook for launching on Product Hunt and growing without paid ads.

## How does Shipper compare with Lovable and Anything?

Shipper competes directly with [Lovable](/cases/lovable/), which reported $100M ARR eight months after launch ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/23/eight-months-in-swedish-unicorn-lovable-crosses-the-100m-arr-milestone)), and with [Anything](/cases/anything/), which reached $2M ARR in its first two weeks ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/29/vibe-coding-startup-anything-nabs-a-100m-valuation-after-hitting-2m-arr-in-its-first-two-weeks)). Shipper is far smaller, and its whole team is about three people. The difference is that Shipper's founders are themselves its target users, while Lovable and Anything were founded by experienced engineers.

Our reading: competing with well-funded leaders is hard on features, so Shipper competes on focus and cost. A three-person team using AI to build an AI builder can stay profitable at a revenue level that would barely cover one engineer at a large competitor.

## What lessons does Shipper offer non-technical founders?

- **Be your own customer.** Non-technical founders know what non-technical users need from an app builder.
- **Small teams can compete on cost.** Three people can run a product that larger rivals staff with hundreds.
- **Separate estimates from reports.** Founder updates, Latka estimates and blog claims for Shipper differ by more than 100x.
- **Launch platforms still work.** A top-ten Product Hunt day helped Shipper grow without paid ads.
