The Windsurf acquisition took three buyers and one weekend to settle. On July 11, 2025, OpenAI's $3B deal for the AI code editor fell apart, and Google instead paid $2.4B to license the technology and hire the founders ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/11/windsurfs-ceo-goes-to-google-openais-acquisition-falls-apart/)). Three days later Cognition, the maker of the Devin coding agent, bought what was left: a company with $82M in ARR and no CEO ([founder-reported via TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/cognition-maker-of-the-ai-coding-agent-devin-acquires-windsurf/)). It is the clearest example so far of an AI startup being taken apart instead of bought whole.

## What was Windsurf before the acquisition fight?

Windsurf was an AI code editor sold to developers, built by Varun Mohan and Douglas Chen. The company was previously called Codeium ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/16/openai-is-reportedly-in-talks-to-buy-windsurf-for-3b-with-news-expected-later-this-week/)). In their July 2025 statement the founders said they were proud of what Windsurf had built "over the last four years", which puts the start in 2021 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/11/windsurfs-ceo-goes-to-google-openais-acquisition-falls-apart/)). By then it had about 250 employees ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/11/windsurfs-ceo-goes-to-google-openais-acquisition-falls-apart/)).

It competed head-on with Cursor on price. In April 2025 Windsurf cut its individual plan to $15 a month, against Cursor's $20, and cut Teams from $35 to $30 a user ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/23/windsurf-slashes-prices-as-competition-with-cursor-heats-up/)).

## How fast did Windsurf's ARR grow before the deals?

Windsurf's ARR more than doubled in about two months in early 2025. A source told TechCrunch it went from about $40M in February to about $100M in April 2025 ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/22/why-openai-wanted-to-buy-cursor-but-opted-for-the-fast-growing-windsurf/)). When Cognition announced its purchase in July, the company put ARR at $82M, with enterprise ARR doubling quarter over quarter and more than 350 enterprise customers ([founder-reported via TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/cognition-maker-of-the-ai-coding-agent-devin-acquires-windsurf/)). That is about $6.8M a month (calc). The $100M and $82M figures come from different sources and may measure different things, so the curve is not a clean series. Total revenue is not disclosed.

## Why did the Windsurf acquisition by OpenAI fall through?

The public record says what happened, not why. TechCrunch reported in April 2025, citing Bloomberg, that OpenAI was in talks to buy Windsurf for about $3B ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/16/openai-is-reportedly-in-talks-to-buy-windsurf-for-3b-with-news-expected-later-this-week/)). OpenAI had first approached Cursor's maker, which said no ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/22/why-openai-wanted-to-buy-cursor-but-opted-for-the-fast-growing-windsurf/)). On July 11 the OpenAI deal was off, and Google DeepMind hired Mohan, Chen and some top researchers. Most of the team did not move ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/11/windsurfs-ceo-goes-to-google-openais-acquisition-falls-apart/)).

> "We are excited to be joining Google DeepMind along with some of the Windsurf team" — Varun Mohan and Douglas Chen ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/11/windsurfs-ceo-goes-to-google-openais-acquisition-falls-apart/))

## What did Cognition get when it bought Windsurf?

Cognition got the product, the brand, the customers and most of the staff, but not the founders. Jeff Wang, Windsurf's head of business, had become interim CEO, and he publicly welcomed the new owners ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/cognition-maker-of-the-ai-coding-agent-devin-acquires-windsurf/)). Cognition did not disclose the price. TechCrunch added that Cognition had reportedly discussed raising money at a $4B valuation that March, and that it was unclear whether that round closed.

## How does Windsurf compare with Cursor and Replit?

All three sold AI coding in 2025, and only Windsurf ended up split between buyers. [Cursor](/cases/cursor/) passed $100M in recurring revenue in January 2025 ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/series-b)), months before Windsurf got there, and stayed independent until SpaceX bought it whole. [Replit](/cases/replit/) reached $150M annualized revenue in September 2025 and raised $250M at a $3B valuation ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/replit-hits-3b-valuation-on-150m-annualized-revenue)). Windsurf's own exit price, about $3B on the OpenAI offer, was in the same range.

In our reading, Windsurf's problem was position, not growth. It sold the same kind of editor as Cursor at a lower price, so it was second in a race where buyers wanted the leader. Replit sold something different, finished apps for non-coders. That gave Replit a reason to stay independent, and Windsurf a reason to sell.

What happened after the deal is covered in the [Cognition](/cases/cognition/) case, which shows how buying Windsurf changed the company behind Devin.

## What lessons does the Windsurf acquisition hold?

- **Price cuts signal a second-place product.** Windsurf priced its individual plan below Cursor's and still trailed it on revenue.
- **Big companies can buy the team without the company.** Google's licence-and-hire deal took the founders and left the business behind.
- **The leftover company still has value.** Cognition bought a product with $82M ARR and 350+ enterprise customers after the founders left.
- **Reported numbers can disagree.** A source put ARR at $100M in April, and the company said $82M in July. Read both as estimates, not audited figures.
