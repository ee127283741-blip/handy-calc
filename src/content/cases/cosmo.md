Cosmo has only 45 active subscriptions, yet it brings in more monthly revenue than many tools with thousands. The reason is that its plan prices are not the whole story. In November 2026 the Discord commerce platform showed $16,950 in MRR and $129,959 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/cosmo)), roughly a year after Kendall Tart founded it.

## What does Cosmo let Discord server owners sell?

Cosmo lets Discord server owners sell digital products, subscriptions, physical goods, license keys and gated roles, and it automates delivery inside the server ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/cosmo)). The listing tags it as e-commerce, marketplace and community software.

> "The all-in-one commerce platform for Discord." — Cosmo listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/cosmo))

Discord communities already gather paying fans for creators, traders and gaming groups. Cosmo keeps the checkout where the community lives instead of sending members to a separate store.

## Why is Cosmo's MRR so high per subscription?

Cosmo had $16,950 in MRR from 45 active subscriptions in November 2026, and it collected $15,958 in the prior 30 days ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/cosmo)). That works out to about $376.67 of MRR per subscription ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/cosmo)).

How the rest is earned, for example through fees on sales, is not disclosed on the listing. Profit is not disclosed either. Treat the gap as an open question, not a conclusion.

## How does Kendall Tart market Cosmo?

Kendall Tart lists twelve marketing channels for Cosmo: a blog, content marketing, SEO, YouTube, X, TikTok, Facebook, Meta ads, cold DMs, Discord, an affiliate program and a referral program ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/cosmo)). That is a wide spread for a company with a team of one.

The founder's X account shows 368 followers, so distribution comes from channels rather than a personal audience. Cold DMs and Discord itself fit the product: the buyers are server owners, and they can be found inside the platform Cosmo serves.

## How does Cosmo compare with Payout and Store Stock Checker?

Cosmo, [Payout](/cases/payout/) and [Store Stock Checker](/cases/store-stock-checker/) all sell tools to people trying to earn money online, rather than to large companies. Their difference is where the sale happens. Cosmo lives inside Discord, while the other two are standalone web products.

Our observation: Cosmo's low subscription count with high MRR looks more like a payments business than classic SaaS. That model can grow with customers' sales, but it also ties Cosmo's revenue to a few large servers.

## What can founders learn from Cosmo's first year?

- **Sell where buyers already gather.** Cosmo builds checkout into Discord instead of asking communities to move.
- **Plan prices are not the whole story.** Roughly $376.67 of MRR per subscription against a $30 top plan shows revenue comes from somewhere beyond the plans ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/cosmo)).
- **Many channels can work for one person.** Twelve listed channels, cold DMs included, replace the missing audience.
- **Concentration is the risk.** With 45 subscriptions, losing a handful of big servers would move the numbers.
