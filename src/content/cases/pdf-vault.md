Most people who want to sell a digital product never finish making one. PDF Vault skips that step: it rents out finished PDFs with a sales page already built. The one-person US business, founded in March 2026, collected $9,342 in the last 30 days and $68,969 all time by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/pdf-vault)). Its pricing design is the part worth studying.

## How does the PDF Vault licensing model work?

PDF Vault members license ready-to-sell PDFs that come with a hosted sales page, and they keep 100% of what they sell ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/pdf-vault)). The company earns twice: a one-time license fee, then recurring monthly hosting. New products launch in weekly drops, and each product's licenses are capped at 15, so doors open once a week.

The audience is entrepreneurs, side hustlers and digital product sellers. The stack is React, Tailwind CSS, Resend and Stripe.

## Why do PDF Vault prices rise as licenses sell?

Rising prices create urgency. The listing gives a $29 one-time license (or a $67 bundle) plus $9 a month hosting, and Pro products are tiered from $49 to $69, with the price climbing as licenses fill ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/pdf-vault)). Early buyers pay less, so a weekly drop rewards moving fast. The 15-license cap also answers a common objection to resale content: that thousands of others sell the same file.

## What takeaways does PDF Vault offer digital product sellers?

- **Sell the shortcut, not the tool.** Buyers pay to skip creation, design and sales-page work.
- **Scarcity can be structural.** A 15-license cap and weekly drops make urgency real.
- **Stack one-time and recurring fees.** Licenses bring cash; hosting keeps a monthly base.
- **Judge it by 30-day revenue.** For a license-led model, MRR alone misses most income.
