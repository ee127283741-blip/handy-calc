Manus ARR crossed $100M in eight months, and that number was the last thing Manus announced as a fully independent company for a while. The general AI agent launched in March 2025. In December 2025 it posted that it had passed $100M ARR, with a total revenue run rate above $125M ([founder-reported, Manus on X](https://x.com/ManusAI/status/2001291341343641680)). Within weeks Meta agreed to buy it. Four months after that, Beijing ordered the deal reversed. Few startups in this library have seen that much happen in one year.

## What does Manus actually do?

Manus is an agent that does a task end to end instead of chatting about it. A user describes a goal, and Manus plans the steps, opens a browser, writes and runs code and returns a result, all inside a virtual computer it controls. In its December 2025 post the company said it had processed more than 147 trillion tokens and created more than 80 million virtual computers since launch ([founder-reported, Manus on X](https://x.com/ManusAI/status/2001291341343641680)). CNBC described it as selling the agent to businesses and individuals through subscriptions ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/30/meta-acquires-singapore-ai-agent-firm-manus-china-butterfly-effect-monicai.html)). Its team size and plan prices are not disclosed in sources we could check, so both are listed as not disclosed.

## What is Manus revenue, and how does it count ARR?

Manus reported two numbers in December 2025: over $100M ARR and a total revenue run rate over $125M ([founder-reported, Manus on X](https://x.com/ManusAI/status/2001291341343641680)). The run rate works out to about $10.4M a month (calc). The company used the stricter ARR figure for its headline and the larger one for total revenue, which suggests the extra $25M is usage revenue that it did not treat as recurring. Total revenue to date is not disclosed.

> "$0 → $100M ARR in 8 months." — Manus ([founder-reported, Manus on X](https://x.com/ManusAI/status/2001291341343641680))

## Why did China block Meta's purchase of Manus?

China blocked it because Manus started there, even though it had moved. In April 2025 Manus raised $75M led by Benchmark at about a $500M valuation ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2999073)), and later moved its headquarters to Singapore. Meta agreed to buy Manus in December 2025 in a deal reported at more than $2B; Meta did not disclose the terms ([media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/30/meta-acquires-singapore-ai-agent-firm-manus-china-butterfly-effect-monicai.html)). In April 2026 China's NDRC ordered the deal unwound under its foreign investment security review rules ([media-reported, Trivium China](https://triviumchina.com/research/china-blocks-metas-manus-acquisition-using-foreign-investment-security-review/)).

## How does Manus compare with Genspark and Cursor?

Manus and [Genspark](/cases/genspark/) are the two general agents that reached nine-figure ARR fastest. Manus launched in March 2025 and reported $100M ARR at eight months. Genspark launched its Super Agent in April 2025 and reported $100M ARR at nine months ([founder-reported, Genspark release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/genspark-launches-ai-workspace-2-150000379.html)). [Cursor](/cases/cursor/), an AI code editor, took longer to reach the same mark but sells to a narrower and more predictable buyer: developers.

The difference is the exit risk. Genspark kept raising, with a $2.6B post-money valuation in June 2026 ([media-reported, FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2026/06/genspark-ai-extends-series-b-to-485m-at-2-6-billion-valuation.html)). Manus moved to Singapore and still found that China claimed a say over its sale. For founders whose team started in one country and sells in another, the Manus case shows that a new headquarters does not end the old country's interest.

## What lessons does Manus offer agent startups?

- **A demo can become a business fast.** Manus went from a viral launch to over $100M ARR in eight months by charging for tasks people could watch the agent do.
- **Report the strict number first.** Manus led with ARR and listed the larger run rate separately, which made its claim easier to trust.
- **Acquisition risk is also political.** A signed deal with Meta did not close cleanly because of where the company began.
- **Usage is a cost as well as revenue.** An agent that runs 80 million virtual computers pays for each one, so token and compute costs shape its margins.
