Glean revenue grew faster after ChatGPT arrived, even though ChatGPT looked like its biggest threat. The enterprise search company took about three years of selling to reach $100M in ARR, which it announced in February 2025 ([founder-reported, Glean](https://www.glean.com/press/glean-achieves-100m-arr-in-three-years-delivering-true-ai-roi-to-the-enterprise)). It then reached $200M nine months later and $300M six months after that ([founder-reported via TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/08/these-ai-startups-are-growing-revenue-at-faster-and-faster-rates/)). Each $100M came faster than the one before.

## What does Glean do inside a company?

Glean connects to a company's internal systems and lets employees search and ask questions across all of them. Arvind Jain founded it in 2019 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/08/exclusive-glean-hits-200-million-arr-up-from-100-million-nine-months-back/)). The product started as search and became an AI assistant once large language models arrived. In Jain's words to Fortune, companies wanted a safe, secure version of ChatGPT for their own staff. TechCrunch names Databricks, Reddit, Pinterest and Samsung as customers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/gleans-top-line-crosses-300m-as-ai-budget-cutting-becomes-its-major-selling-point/)).

> "The first four or five years of our existence, we had no competition," — Arvind Jain ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/gleans-top-line-crosses-300m-as-ai-budget-cutting-becomes-its-major-selling-point/))

## How did Glean's ARR grow from $100M to $300M?

Glean's ARR tripled in about 15 months, and the gap between milestones shrank each time. The company counts only software subscription revenue, not consulting or services, and Jain told Fortune it signs no contracts shorter than one year ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/08/exclusive-glean-hits-200-million-arr-up-from-100-million-nine-months-back/)).

At $300M ARR, Glean brings in about $25M a month on our arithmetic (calc). There is one caveat. TechCrunch points out that some customers pay by usage, so part of the $300M is better described as a run rate than strictly recurring revenue ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/gleans-top-line-crosses-300m-as-ai-budget-cutting-becomes-its-major-selling-point/)). Total revenue and headcount are not disclosed.

## How does Glean price its AI search?

Glean offers two models and publishes no rates. One is pure consumption. The other is a hybrid with a fixed monthly fee for each active user plus separate charges for model usage ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/gleans-top-line-crosses-300m-as-ai-budget-cutting-becomes-its-major-selling-point/)). In 2026 Glean began pitching cost savings directly. Jain told TechCrunch that customers like that Glean can cut their AI bill a lot, because its context graph of company data reduces the tokens models need. In our reading, this is a clever turn: as companies spend more on AI models, Glean sells itself as the layer that makes that spending smaller.

## Is Glean's valuation keeping up with its revenue?

Glean's valuation has risen more slowly than its ARR. It raised a $150M Series F in June 2025 at $7.2B, up from $4.6B in 2024 ([media-reported, Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/08/exclusive-glean-hits-200-million-arr-up-from-100-million-nine-months-back/)). TechCrunch still described $7.2B as its last valuation in May 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/gleans-top-line-crosses-300m-as-ai-budget-cutting-becomes-its-major-selling-point/)). Against $300M ARR, that is 24 times revenue (calc). That is a low multiple for a fast-growing AI company.

## How does Glean compare with Perplexity, Harvey and TypingMind?

All four sell AI answers, but Glean searches a company's own data while [Perplexity](/cases/perplexity/) searches the public web. Perplexity's ARR was approaching $200M in September 2025, when it was valued at $20B ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/perplexity-reportedly-raised-200m-at-20b-valuation/)). Glean had about the same ARR three months later at about a third of the price. [Harvey](/cases/harvey/), which sells AI to law firms, reported $190M ARR in January 2026 ([founder-reported via CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/25/legal-ai-startup-harvey-raises-200-million-at-11-billion-valuation.html)). [TypingMind](/cases/typingmind/) is a one-person chat interface that passed $500K in total revenue after about a year ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://starterstory.com/typingmind-breakdown)).

In our view, the gap between Glean and Perplexity is about who pays. Perplexity sells mostly $20 Pro subscriptions to individuals ([founder-reported, Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/introducing-perplexity-max)), and investors price it as a possible Google rival. Glean sells multi-year contracts to IT departments. That revenue is steadier but tells a smaller story, so it earns a lower multiple. Harvey sits between them: enterprise contracts, but in a single profession.

[Sana](/cases/sana/) sold a similar workplace AI assistant and chose to sell to Workday instead of staying independent.

For a finance-focused counterpart to enterprise AI search, see [Hebbia](/cases/hebbia/).

## What are the takeaways from Glean's growth?

- **Years of slow work can set up a fast run.** Glean spent its early years building connectors to company systems, and that work paid off once AI assistants took off.
- **Sell the cost cut, not just the feature.** Glean now pitches lower AI bills, which is easier to approve in a tight budget.
- **Define ARR clearly.** Glean says it counts only software subscriptions, while TechCrunch notes the usage-based share is closer to a run rate.
- **Enterprise revenue earns lower multiples.** Glean's valuation is about 24 times ARR, far below consumer AI peers like Perplexity.
