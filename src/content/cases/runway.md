Runway's tools were used on *Everything Everywhere All At Once*, and by December 2025 its Gen-4.5 model ranked first on a public text-to-video leaderboard, ahead of Google and OpenAI ([founder-reported via CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/01/runway-gen-4-5-video-model-google-open-ai.html)). It got there with about 100 people. The company started as an NYU art-school project. In February 2026 it raised $315M at a $5.3B valuation ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/10/ai-video-startup-runway-raises-315m-at-5-3b-valuation-eyes-more-capable-world-models/)). Its full revenue is still not disclosed.

## Why did three NYU art students start Runway?

They wanted to put machine learning in the hands of artists, and they wrote code themselves to make that happen. Cristóbal Valenzuela studied economics in Santiago, Chile, then worked in film and software. Alejandro Matamala, also from Santiago, studied advertising and ran a design firm. Anastasis Germanidis started programming at 11 in Athens and moved to the US at 18 to study neuroscience and film. The three met in 2016 at NYU's ITP program and launched Runway in 2018 ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/15/runway-started-by-helping-filmmakers-now-it-wants-to-beat-google-at-ai/)). Valenzuela and Germanidis now run the company as co-CEOs, and Matamala is chief innovation officer.

> "Rules are just rules they invented," — Cristóbal Valenzuela ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/15/runway-started-by-helping-filmmakers-now-it-wants-to-beat-google-at-ai/))

## How did Runway go from creator tools to Gen-3 and world models?

Runway moved from AI editing tools to training its own video models, and then to "world models" that simulate physical reality. In 2023 its customers included Fortune 500 companies such as New Balance and "millions" of individual creators ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/29/runway-a-startup-building-generative-ai-for-content-creators-raises-141m/)). Its first video model came out in February 2023. Gen-3 Alpha followed in June 2024. It made 5- and 10-second high-resolution clips from text or still images, and TechCrunch called it a major step up from Gen-2 in speed and fidelity ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/17/runways-new-video-generating-ai-gen-3-offers-improved-controls/)).

Gen-4 arrived in March 2025, promising consistent characters and locations across scenes ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/03/runway-best-known-for-its-video-generating-models-raises-308m/)). By September 2025 Runway was pitching robotics companies on its simulation work ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/01/why-runway-is-eyeing-the-robotics-industry-for-future-revenue-growth/)). It released its first world model in December 2025. In September 2026 it brought in the Paris-based Kinetix research team for robotics ([founder-reported, Runway](https://runway.com/news/company-news/kinetix-joins-runway)).

For individual creators, Runway sells credit-based subscriptions. In November 2026 its pricing page listed a free plan plus Standard at $15, Pro at $35 and Max at $95 a month, or $12, $28 and $76 a month billed yearly ([founder-reported, Runway pricing page](https://runway.com/pricing)).

## How did Runway's valuation climb to $5.3 billion?

It climbed in three big rounds over less than three years. In June 2023 Runway raised a $141M extension from Google, Nvidia and Salesforce at a reported $1.5B valuation ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/29/runway-a-startup-building-generative-ai-for-content-creators-raises-141m/)). By the Gen-3 launch it had raised over $236.5M in total ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/17/runways-new-video-generating-ai-gen-3-offers-improved-controls/)). In April 2025 General Atlantic led a $308M Series D ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/03/runway-best-known-for-its-video-generating-models-raises-308m/)), and TechCrunch later put the valuation at $3B ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/01/why-runway-is-eyeing-the-robotics-industry-for-future-revenue-growth/)).

General Atlantic led again in February 2026: a $315M Series E at $5.3B, with Nvidia, Adobe Ventures and AMD Ventures taking part. Runway said it would use the money to pre-train its next world models ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/10/ai-video-startup-runway-raises-315m-at-5-3b-valuation-eyes-more-capable-world-models/)). By May 2026 its total raised was $860M ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/15/runway-started-by-helping-filmmakers-now-it-wants-to-beat-google-at-ai/)).

## How small is the team behind Runway's frontier models?

Very small for a company competing with Google. In December 2025 Valenzuela told CNBC that Runway had about 100 people ([founder-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/01/runway-gen-4-5-video-model-google-open-ai.html)). TechCrunch put the team at roughly 140 in February 2026 ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/10/ai-video-startup-runway-raises-315m-at-5-3b-valuation-eyes-more-capable-world-models/)) and at 155 by May 2026, across offices including New York, London, Seattle, Tel Aviv and Tokyo ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/15/runway-started-by-helping-filmmakers-now-it-wants-to-beat-google-at-ai/)). In our reading, that headcount is the most striking fact here. Runway raised $860M and kept the team under 200, so most of the money goes to compute and research rather than staff.

> "We managed to out-compete trillion-dollar companies with a team of 100 people," — Cristóbal Valenzuela ([founder-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/01/runway-gen-4-5-video-model-google-open-ai.html))

## How does Runway compare with Opus Clip, Neural Frames and Midjourney?

Runway trains its own frontier models, while the two smaller creative-media cases on this site package AI into one narrow job. [OpusClip](/cases/opus-clip/) cuts long videos into shorts. It was reported at about $20M ARR in March 2025 ([unverified, ARR Club](https://www.arr.club/signal/opusclip-20m-arr-softbank-mar-2025)), when it raised $20M from SoftBank Vision Fund 2 at a $215M valuation ([media-reported, Net Influencer](https://netinfluencer.com/opusclip-secures-20m-usd-from-softbank-vision-fund-2-launches-ai-video-search-tool)). Runway's single Series E was more than 15 times that round.

[neural frames](/cases/neural-frames/) sits at the other end. Solo founder Nicolai Klemke built it on open-source Stable Diffusion and reached $17K MRR by the end of October 2023 ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/neural-frames)). Both companies sell video to creators. The gap is in who owns the model: neural frames rents compute for an open model, while Runway raises hundreds of millions to train its own. [Midjourney](/cases/midjourney/) is the other well-known AI lab rooted in creative tools.

[Higgsfield](/cases/higgsfield/) shows the app-layer alternative: it packages video models into presets for social media marketers instead of competing on its own frontier model.

## What can AI startups learn from Runway?

- **Start with a creative audience, then move up the stack.** Runway began with tools for artists and later trained its own models, so it already had a customer base when each model launched.
- **Keep the team small while raising big.** About 100 people shipped a model that topped a public leaderboard, and $860M raised did not turn into a large headcount.
- **Every model needs a new market.** Gen-3, Gen-4 and world models pushed Runway from film and ads toward robotics and gaming.
- **A target is not a result.** The $300M figure was a reported 2025 goal, and Runway's total revenue is still not disclosed in a source we could verify.
