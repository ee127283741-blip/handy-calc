Topical Map AI revenue is one piece of a portfolio built by a blogger, not a programmer. Pete McPherson runs Do You Even Blog, a podcast and community for bloggers. Starting with no coding background, he used AI tools to build small apps for his audience. He told The Side Hustle Show he made $100K in a year from AI-coded apps, including a $45,000 sale of one of them ([founder-reported, The Side Hustle Show](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/100k-with-ai-coded-apps/)). He later wrote that AI-built apps had earned him $350,000 in total.

## What is Topical Map AI?

Topical Map AI is an SEO planning tool: a blogger enters a broad niche, and it returns about 1,000 topics grouped into categories and sub-topics, so the blogger knows what to write and how to link it ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/@petemcpherson/forums)). McPherson built it for the audience he already served, bloggers trying to rank in Google. He also built Fabb.ai, an autonomous blog builder, and Aff Tracker, for affiliate links ([Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/100k-with-ai-coded-apps/)). Prices and team size are not disclosed.

## How did Pete McPherson make money from AI-built apps?

Pete McPherson made money from AI-built apps by selling subscriptions to his audience and by selling whole apps. He reported $100K in a year from AI-coded apps ([founder-reported, The Side Hustle Show](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/100k-with-ai-coded-apps/)), including $45,000 from selling Fabb.ai on Acquire.com. In an October 2025 guest post, he put his total from AI-built apps at $350,000 ([founder-reported, Making Sense of Cents](https://www.makingsenseofcents.com/2025/10/how-ive-made-350000-from-ai-built-apps.html)). Revenue for Topical Map AI alone, and monthly revenue, are not disclosed.

## Which tools did a non-programmer use to build Topical Map AI?

McPherson wrote his apps with AI inside Cursor and VS Code, hosted them on Vercel and used Firebase for logins and data ([Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/100k-with-ai-coded-apps/)). His first project was a Chrome extension to do quick math while playing a game, a personal problem. He later created a course teaching others the same approach. The pattern is common among non-coders: start with a tiny tool for yourself, then build for your audience.

## How does Topical Map AI compare with ChatSEO and TrendFeed?

Topical Map AI, [ChatSEO](/cases/chatseo/) and [TrendFeed](/cases/trendfeed/) all sell content and marketing tools built by solo founders with AI. ChatSEO targets search visibility for businesses; TrendFeed's non-technical founder built it in four days for his audience. McPherson's approach is closest to TrendFeed's: an existing audience of bloggers bought the tools he built, and he sold one app outright when he stopped maintaining it.

Our reading: McPherson treats apps like content, small products for an audience he already owns. Selling an app for $45,000 shows another exit for non-coders: build something useful, grow it a little, then sell it to someone who wants to run it.

## What lessons does Pete McPherson offer audience owners?

- **Build for the audience you have.** Bloggers bought SEO tools from a blogging podcaster.
- **Start with your own problem.** A Chrome extension for game math was his first project.
- **Apps can be sold, not just run.** Fabb.ai sold for $45,000 on Acquire.com.
- **A portfolio spreads risk.** Several small apps added up to $350,000 over time.
