Raising a seed round starts with a list of names. Angel Match sells that list. Founded in January 2019, the investor database is close to its first million dollars: $991,861 in all-time revenue and $25,024 in monthly recurring revenue by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/angel-match)). It is an older, quieter business than most on TrustMRR, and its numbers show what a data product looks like in year seven.

## What does Angel Match sell to founders raising a seed round?

Angel Match sells access to a searchable database of angel investors and venture capitalists. The company-written listing describes it as a database of 124,000 angels and VCs, and elsewhere as connecting founders with 125,000+ investors in one platform ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/angel-match)). The target buyer is startup founders and business owners, and the listing estimates around 125,000 users.

> "Angel Match is a database of 124,000 angels and VCs to raise your seed round." — Angel Match listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/angel-match))

The Angel Match homepage returned a region restriction when we tried to load it, so the product details here come only from the listing. Plan prices are not disclosed there; the listing says a free trial is available and a subscription is required.

## How has Angel Match revenue moved over the last year?

Angel Match revenue has drifted down from a spring peak. Verified monthly revenue was $32,004 in December 2025, reached $36,197 in March 2026, then settled between about $24,000 and $28,000 a month from June to October ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/angel-match)). The 30 days before the snapshot brought in $24,474, down 12.6%, and MRR was down 3.2%.

## Why does an investor list keep earning for Angel Match years later?

Because the need comes back with every new cohort of founders. Every year a new group starts raising, and each one needs a list of investors to contact. Angel Match's tech stack is plain, Node.js on DigitalOcean with Stripe billing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/angel-match)), which suggests the value sits in the data rather than in complex software.

The risk is the same as with any list product. A founder may subscribe for one raise and then cancel, which fits a revenue line that moves with fundraising seasons rather than climbing steadily. Rashid Khasanov has 1,824 followers on X, so the business does not rest on a large founder audience.

## How does Angel Match compare with Web3Forms and DataFast?

All three sell a narrow tool to builders and show Stripe- or Paddle-verified revenue on TrustMRR, but Angel Match is the oldest and the only pure data product.

Our observation: [Web3Forms](/cases/web3forms/) and [DataFast](/cases/datafast/) are used every day once installed, so customers keep paying. Angel Match is used hardest during a raise. That difference likely explains why its revenue is flat to down while it still reaches roughly the same monthly scale.

## What lessons can founders learn from Angel Match?

- **Data can be the whole product.** Angel Match sells a list of investors on simple infrastructure and is near $1 million all time ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/angel-match)).
- **Episodic needs cap retention.** Fundraising happens in bursts, so a subscription tied to it will churn when the raise ends.
- **Small audiences can still sell.** A founder with under 2,000 X followers built this without leaning on personal reach.
- **Consistent numbers matter.** The listing gives 124,000 in one place and 125,000+ in another; a single figure would read more cleanly.
