Running a padel tournament means juggling brackets, court times and scores on paper or spreadsheets. Versus, a one-person French company founded in August 2026, automates that work. Its last 30 days brought in $2,186, most of its $2,915 lifetime revenue, while recurring MRR stayed at $392 in November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/versus)). The gap says a lot about how clubs pay.

## What problem does this padel software solve for clubs?

Versus automates the organisation of padel tournaments, including scheduling, score tracking and rankings, without manual intervention, and an AI assistant helps configure and schedule events ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/versus)). It targets clubs and leagues and reports about 200 users.

> "Automate every tournament format for every sports" — Versus value proposition on TrustMRR ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/versus))

The company is bootstrapped and run by Marc Pfeiler alone. The listing places it in the games market and classes it as B2B, since the buyers are clubs and leagues rather than individual players, with Stripe handling payments.

## Which Versus plans do clubs pay for?

Versus mixes pay-per-event and subscriptions: a free tier, €19 per tournament, a Club plan at €57.50 a month and a Pro plan at €107.50 a month ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/versus)). The company description adds a per-user subscription of 15 euros. A per-tournament price fits clubs that run events occasionally, and it explains why monthly recurring revenue is small next to total cash.

## What can niche SaaS founders learn from Versus?

- **A narrow sport can be a real market.** Padel clubs paid within three months of launch.
- **Event pricing brings cash but not MRR.** Per-tournament fees dominate last-30-day revenue.
- **Upgrade paths matter.** Club and Pro plans exist to turn one-off buyers into subscribers.
- **AI helps with setup, not the whole product.** The assistant configures tournaments; scheduling logic is the core.
