Malta's courts sell seized property at public auction, but the notices are scattered and written in Maltese. Subbasta turns that into a searchable product for investors. In November 2026 it had $3,905 in monthly recurring revenue and $64,617 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/subbasta)), less than a year after it was founded. It is a hyper-local business with a narrow but motivated audience.

## What problem does Subbasta solve in Malta's judicial sales?

Subbasta gathers Malta's judicial property auctions in one place and translates the official documents from Maltese. The company-written TrustMRR listing says it presents auction data "in a more user-friendly and accessible way" for an audience it calls smart investors ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/subbasta)). Its homepage, at [subbastamalta.com](https://subbastamalta.com/), is titled around judicial property auctions at the Law Courts.

> "Malta's Judicial Property Auctions, All in One Place." — Subbasta listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/subbasta))

## How much revenue does Subbasta make from subscriptions and services?

Subbasta had $3,905 in MRR and 134 active subscriptions in November 2026, with $64,617 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/subbasta)). TrustMRR checks these numbers through a Stripe API key. The founding date is December 2025, so most of that revenue arrived within about eleven months.

All-time revenue is large relative to MRR, which suggests one-time services and success fees add real money on top, although the split is not disclosed. Team size and profit are not disclosed.

## Why does Subbasta stack services on top of a subscription?

A buyer at a Maltese court auction may live abroad and need someone to act for them. Subbasta's add-ons follow that path: a power of attorney, a viewing, legal research and finally a fee on a winning bid ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/subbasta)). The subscription brings investors in; the services earn more as a deal gets closer.

The listing names SEO, Facebook and Instagram as marketing channels and about 130 users, roughly matching its 134 subscriptions. Almost every user pays.

Our observation: SoldComps and Subbasta both clean up public data that is technically available but painful to use. Interior AI sells to a global audience of homeowners; Subbasta picked one small country and one legal process, and it monetizes the paperwork around the purchase as well as the data.

## What lessons does Subbasta offer local data startups?

- **Small markets can still pay.** A single-country auction platform reached $3,905 in MRR within its first year.
- **Translation is a product.** Turning Maltese court documents into readable listings is the core value.
- **Follow the deal with services.** Fees for onboarding, viewings, legal research and a 1% success fee grow with each step toward a purchase.
- **Nearly every user pays.** About 130 users against 134 subscriptions means the free funnel is thin and the audience is serious.
