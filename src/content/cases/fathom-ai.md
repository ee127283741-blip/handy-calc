Fathom AI ARR reached about $300,000 within roughly 12 weeks of launch, and the company has no engineering team. Fortune reported in April 2026 that Fathom AI, an Austin-based sales platform for the medical aesthetics industry, started with $300 in total capital, three founders and 12 AI agents ([media-reported, Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/18/ai-layoff-became-founder-team-of-3-instant-profit/)). One of the founders, Sam Brown, had been let go in an AI-linked layoff about nine months earlier. His next job was a company built mostly out of AI.

## What does Fathom AI sell to medical aesthetics clinics?

Fathom AI sells sales enablement software built specifically for the medical aesthetics industry, the clinics and brands behind cosmetic treatments ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/18/ai-layoff-became-founder-team-of-3-instant-profit/)). It launched in early 2026 in Austin, Texas. Ben Hooten is the CEO, Sam Brown is president and Dan Crump is the third founder. Pricing and customer counts are not disclosed. Note that this Fathom AI is unrelated to Fathom, the better-known AI meeting notetaker.

## How much ARR did Fathom AI make in its first 12 weeks?

Fathom AI made about $300,000 in annual recurring revenue within roughly 12 weeks of launch, according to Fortune ([media-reported, Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/18/ai-layoff-became-founder-team-of-3-instant-profit/)). That is about $25K a month (calc: ARR / 12). The company started with $300 and took no venture capital. Total revenue is not disclosed, and Fortune is the only source for the figures.

## Who does the work at Fathom AI if there are no engineers?

At Fathom AI, 12 AI agents hold operational roles, Hooten told Fortune, including running customer success for a national sales force ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/18/ai-layoff-became-founder-team-of-3-instant-profit/)). Fortune described the team as having no venture funding, no engineering team and no traditional software infrastructure. The founders said investors told them they would need a large engineering team, and they disagreed.

> "I don't want to ever hire an account executive or a customer success manager" — Ben Hooten, Fathom AI CEO ([founder-reported via Fortune, republished by JBiz News](https://jbiznews.com/?p=310844))

Hooten said the only roles they want to hire are forward deployed engineers and forward deployed consultants, people who sit with customers. So "no engineers" describes how the company started, not a rule against ever hiring technical staff.

## How does Fathom AI compare with Artisan and 11x?

[Artisan](/cases/artisan/) and [11x](/cases/11x/) sell AI sales agents to other companies, and both raised venture money to build them. Fathom AI does the reverse: it uses agents inside its own company and sells software to one vertical, medical aesthetics. Artisan reported about 250 customers at its Series A; 11x faced a TechCrunch investigation into how it counted ARR. Fathom AI's only public figure comes from records Fortune reviewed, not a fundraising deck.

Our reading: a narrow industry plus agents doing back-office work let three founders skip the usual first hires. Like [GenAIPI](/cases/genaipi/), whose founder does not code, Fathom AI's advantage is knowing a specific buyer, not writing software.

## What can non-technical founders learn from Fathom AI?

- **Pick a narrow buyer.** Medical aesthetics is small enough that three founders can know it well.
- **Use agents for operations first.** Customer success and support were handed to AI before any hiring.
- **Start cheap, then prove revenue.** Fathom AI began with $300 and reached about $300,000 in ARR in roughly 12 weeks.
- **Read "no engineers" carefully.** The founders still plan to hire forward deployed engineers.
- **Check who verified the number.** Fortune reviewed the records; no second source exists yet.
