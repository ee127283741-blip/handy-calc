A bug fix that takes ten minutes to write can take days to reach users if an app store has to review it first. Capgo sells the shortcut for apps built with Capacitor. Five years after Martin Donadieu started it, the bootstrapped company showed $33,747 in MRR and $594,447 in all-time revenue in November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/capgo)).

## What does Capgo let Capacitor developers skip?

Capgo lets developers skip the store review wait for web-layer changes. Capacitor apps run a web bundle inside a native shell, so JavaScript, CSS and assets can be swapped without a new binary. Capgo pushes those bundles in minutes, with channels, staged rollouts and one-click rollback ([founder-reported, Capgo](https://capgo.app/about/)). TrustMRR sums up the problem it solves in five words: app store review delays production fixes.

The product has grown beyond updates. Capgo now sells signed iOS and Android builds in the cloud without a Mac, release monitoring, push notifications, a CLI and MCP server for AI coding agents, and over 157 open-source Capacitor plugins. The About page says Capgo is reached through apps on 90M+ devices.

## Why did Capgo gain ground when CodePush and Appflow faded?

Capgo gained a timing advantage when bigger players stepped back. Its About page says Microsoft CodePush is in legacy mode and Ionic is winding down Appflow, with existing customers able to use it until December 31, 2027, and no new customers or features ([founder-reported, Capgo](https://capgo.app/about/)).

> "Capgo is actively maintained, bootstrapped, and focused on live updates as the core product." — Capgo About page ([founder-reported, Capgo](https://capgo.app/about/))

That pitch is aimed at teams forced to migrate. Capgo also sells open-source replacements for Ionic enterprise plugins, such as storage, auth and biometrics, which lowers the cost of switching. The company is run by Digital Shift OÜ in Estonia with a US entity in Delaware, and the About page lists a five-person core team.

Capgo makes about $34K a month in recurring revenue. TrustMRR showed $33,747 MRR, $39,399 in the last 30 days and $594,447 all time in November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/capgo)).

## How does Capgo compare with Supabase and Lovable?

Capgo, [Supabase](/cases/supabase/) and [Lovable](/cases/lovable/) all sell to people building apps, and they sit at very different scales. Capgo runs its own backend on Supabase, per TrustMRR's tech stack list, and its site has a guide for shipping a Lovable app to the app stores.

Our observation: Capgo and Supabase share an open-source playbook, giving the code away and charging for the hosted service. Lovable shows the other direction: AI builders create many new web apps, and some of them need a path to the app stores, which is where a Capgo-style tool fits. The difference is market size. Supabase serves almost any web developer, while Capgo serves only Capacitor teams, which caps its ceiling but also explains why bigger rivals left the niche to it.

## What lessons can founders take from Capgo's slow climb?

- **Niches left by giants are openings.** CodePush and Appflow stepping back gave Capgo a ready list of teams that had to move ([founder-reported, Capgo](https://capgo.app/about/)).
- **Open source lowers switching costs.** Free plugins that replace paid Ionic ones make migration cheaper and feed the paid product.
- **Bootstrapped can still mean certified.** A small team chose SOC 2 and ISO 27001 to sell to enterprises.
- **Low prices need volume.** At about $27.53 per subscription, Capgo needs over 1,200 subscriptions to clear $33,747 a month ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/capgo)).
