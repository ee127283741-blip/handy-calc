Nicolai Klemke broke the first rule of startups and says so himself. He started from a technology, not a problem: Stable Diffusion's ability to animate images. The problem turned up later, in his old life as a rapper who never had videos for his songs. Once he rebuilt the product for musicians, neural frames went from about $400 a month to $17K MRR by the end of October 2023 ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/neural-frames)).

## Why does a physicist build music video software?

Klemke made music as a rapper and producer while studying physics, and he was always frustrated that every finished song needed visual content to post on social media. Images with music do not work on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube, and videos were slow or expensive to make. His music career did not take off, so he did a PhD at DESY in Hamburg, where writing simulations made him realise he never wanted to stop programming.

After a year in a normal job and the Entrepreneur First accelerator, he started playing with Deforum, an open-source library for animating Stable Diffusion. It made striking videos but was hard to use, and he wanted to put it in the hands of creatives who would never clone a GitHub repository.

> "There’s one rule when founding a startup: Never start from the technology, always start from the problem. I broke it big time." — Nicolai Klemke ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/neural-frames))

## What did neural frames' founder learn by shutting down v1?

The first version, launched in January 2023, was built in about seven weeks of winter programming. Klemke knew Python but had never done React or web development, and ChatGPT helped him move faster. He was afraid of user authentication, so users got a 12-digit code to remember instead of a login, and two long calls with AWS support taught him about autoscaling.

A post on Reddit's r/internetisbeautiful and a run to number six on Hacker News crashed his servers and brought in users, but the product made only about $400 a month. Users wanted everything from dream visualisations to children's stories. Then he decided the product should serve musicians. He took the site offline, emailed his customers an apology and rebuilt it with a proper timeline and custom model training. The relaunch came in April 2023 ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/neural-frames)).

## How did neural frames grow without paid ads?

Paid ads did not work for him, on influencer sites or Google Ads. What worked was getting the right people to use the product and talk about it. An Italian TikTok creator boosted neural frames without being paid, AI artists in the community spread it further, and a musician made a whole series of videos with it.

Search mattered too. Klemke targeted low-difficulty keywords that matched his ideal customer, wrote GPT-4-assisted blog posts and embedded example videos in them. YouTube tutorials brought customers when YouTube's search showed them to the right people. The biggest product jump was audio-reactive effects, which made neural frames a platform for musicians.

## How much revenue does neural frames AI make?

By the end of October 2023, neural frames had $17K MRR and more than 400 paying users, with 130K visitors since the April relaunch ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/neural-frames)). Total revenue is not disclosed.

Generative video is expensive to run. Klemke said cloud costs were around $7K a month depending on demand, and support for hundreds of paying users was starting to crowd out marketing and product work ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/neural-frames)).

## How does neural frames compare with Suno and BasedLabs?

[Suno](/cases/suno/) generates the song; neural frames generates the video for a song that already exists. Both sell creative output to people who are not professionals at making it. The difference is scale and capital: Suno trains its own models with large funding rounds, while neural frames is one person building on open-source Stable Diffusion.

[BasedLabs](/cases/basedlabs/) also built on Stability AI's open models, but went broad with many free AI tools and an SEO-driven funnel. neural frames went narrow, choosing musicians as its one audience. The niche gave it a clear feature roadmap, starting with audio-reactive effects that a general AI video site had no reason to build.

## Which takeaways from neural frames apply to creative tools?

- **A narrow audience can rescue a vague product.** Rebuilding for musicians turned about $400 a month into $17K MRR ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/neural-frames)).
- **It is fine to take a product offline.** Klemke apologised to customers and relaunched a better version.
- **Community beats ads for creative tools.** Unpaid creators and AI artists drove growth where paid ads failed.
- **Watch compute costs.** About $7K a month in cloud spend is the price of generative video.
