Kori is five months old and already collects more than $20,000 a month. The iOS app, which teaches beginners to say real Korean phrases out loud, took in $23,471 in the last 30 days and $75,525 since its June 2026 launch ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/kori)). Its founder is now selling it, and the reason given is unusual: the app was built to collect data, not to be kept.

## What is the Kori app and who is it for?

Kori is a mobile app with short lessons, real phrases and speaking practice, framed around a friendly Korean tutor. The listing says most apps leave learners translating in their heads, while Kori gets them speaking from the first lesson. Its audience is total beginners and people interested in Korean travel, K-pop, culture or daily chat ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/kori)).

The app is free to download and is built in Swift, with Superwall handling the paywall. It holds a 4.7 out of 5 rating from 757 App Store ratings, as shown on the listing.

## How much does Kori earn from 1,296 subscriptions?

Kori made $23,471 in the 30 days before November 10, 2026, while its MRR stood at $5,864 from 1,296 active subscriptions ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/kori)).

The gap between 30-day revenue and MRR is large: revenue is about four times MRR. A common reason in paywalled apps is annual or one-time purchases, which count fully when paid but spread thinly in MRR; the plan mix is not disclosed, so that is our reading, not a reported fact. MRR per subscription works out to about $4.52 (calc: $5,864 / 1,296, [from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/kori)).

## Why is Kori's founder selling after five months?

The founder, listed as rishi, says the apps were a way to gather creative data across niches, and that this niche has been exhausted. Paid social is the engine: Instagram and Meta Ads are the only marketing channels listed ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/kori)).

> "selling lets us free up time and capital to focus on the core business." — rishi, founder of Kori ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/kori))

A buyer would get an app with real, verified sales but a brand-new website: the listing shows an Ahrefs Domain Rating of 1 out of 100 for learnkori.com, so almost no organic search footprint.

Our observation: Verbi, which also centres on spoken practice, reports MRR far closer to their monthly takings, which suggests steadier recurring plans. Chessigma is closer to Kori in size but sells a skill people practise for years, not a beginner course. Kori's revenue leans on fresh paid-ad purchases, so its value to a buyer depends on whether the Meta Ads machine keeps working after the handover.

## What can app builders learn from Kori?

- **A narrow niche can pay fast.** One language, one audience and a clear promise reached $75,525 in about five months.
- **Speaking beats translating as a hook.** The pitch is about saying Korean out loud from lesson one, which is easy to show in a short ad.
- **Watch the MRR gap.** $23,471 in 30-day revenue against $5,864 MRR means much of the income is not recurring.
- **Paid ads need a handover plan.** With Instagram and Meta Ads as the only channels and a Domain Rating of 1, the buyer inherits the ad account risk.
