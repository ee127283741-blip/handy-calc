Brawler sells a boxing coach that lives in a phone. The app was founded in May 2026, and by November it showed $24,594 in MRR and $183,784 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brawler)). In the 30 days before that snapshot it took in $45,106, far more than its MRR, which points to a launch built heavily on annual plans.

## What does Brawler's AI boxing coach do?

Brawler offers personalized boxing coaching and technique analysis powered by AI, aimed at fighters of every level ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brawler)). The listing links to the App Store, where the app showed a 4.8 rating from 2,443 ratings at the time of the snapshot.

The build is a standard indie mobile stack: Swift and SwiftUI for the app, with Supabase and RevenueCat behind it. How the AI analyses technique, for example from camera video, is not described on the listing, so we don't speculate on it here.

> "Your AI boxing coach. Anytime. Anywhere." — Brawler listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brawler))

## How did Brawler make $183K in six months?

Brawler made $183,784 in all-time revenue between its May 2026 founding and November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brawler)). MRR was $24,594 from 4,795 active subscriptions, and the last 30 days brought in $45,106.

| Plan or metric (Nov 2026) | Value | Source |

| Yearly plan | $59.99 | [founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brawler) |

MRR per subscription is about $5.13 ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brawler)), close to $59.99 spread over 12 months. That fits a user base mostly on yearly plans. Annual plans also explain why 30-day revenue is almost double MRR: yearly payments count in full when collected but are spread across 12 months in MRR. Team size and profit are not disclosed.

## Which channels does Brawler use to find fighters?

Brawler lists two marketing channels: Meta ads and influencers ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brawler)). Both suit a visual sport, where short clips of punches and drills show the product quickly.

Paid ads also fit the pricing. A $59.99 yearly plan collected up front gives an app cash to spend on the next round of ads, which is a common pattern for subscription apps. Ad spend and acquisition cost are not disclosed, so we can't say whether Brawler's ads pay back. The listing's estimate of about 1,200 users sits oddly beside 4,795 active subscriptions, so we treat that user estimate with caution.

## What can app founders learn from Brawler?

- **Pick a sport with a skill to teach.** Technique analysis gives an AI coach a concrete job ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brawler)).
- **Annual plans front-load cash.** $45,106 in 30-day revenue against $24,594 MRR shows yearly payments at work ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brawler)).
- **Visual products suit paid social.** Meta ads and influencers are the only channels listed.
- **Ratings build trust fast.** A 4.8 App Store rating from 2,443 ratings within months helps paid traffic convert.
