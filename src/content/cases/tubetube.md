Paste a song's lyrics, wait about 15 minutes and get back a finished music video. That is TubeTube's promise, and eight months after its March 2026 founding it was worth $6,448 in monthly recurring revenue and $9,273 in 30-day sales ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tubetube)). The French startup does not name its founder, but its numbers are verified through Stripe.

## How does TubeTube turn lyrics into a video?

TubeTube is an AI video generator for storytellers and music makers. Users paste lyrics or a story; TubeTube writes the music, builds every scene and edits the final video, ready to publish in about 15 minutes ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tubetube)). The problem it targets is moving from script to upload-ready video without manual editing or a stack of separate tools.

> "Our AI video generator turns lyrics or a story into a finished video." — TubeTube description on TrustMRR ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tubetube))

Its audience is video makers, YouTubers and streamers. The founder is not disclosed, and the company says it is bootstrapped.

All three sell AI video creation to online creators, but they start from different inputs.

Our observation: Vid.AI and AutoClip serve the broad faceless-content market. TubeTube picks a narrower creative niche, songs and stories, and adds music generation, which neither of the others leads with. Its $399 Agency tier ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tubetube)) shows it also wants to sell to people producing at volume.

## What can creators learn from TubeTube?

- **A narrow input defines the product.** "Paste lyrics" is easier to grasp than a general video tool.
- **Bundle the whole pipeline.** Music, scenes and editing in one step remove the multi-tool hassle.
- **Wide price ladders catch both ends.** Plans from $22 to $399 serve hobbyists and agencies.
- **Recent momentum is real but young.** About 42% of all-time revenue came in the last 30 days.
