Faceless.video revenue reached $1M ARR on the seventh try. Jacob Seeger, a songwriter and music producer, had no technical background and failed with six products before this one. He built Faceless.video, an AI tool that makes and posts short videos for channels where no one appears on camera, on the no-code platform Bubble. It reached $1M ARR in under a year after its October 2023 launch, with more than 850,000 users ([founder-reported, Bubble](https://bubble.io/blog/faceless-video)).

## How did a songwriter build Faceless.video?

Jacob Seeger built Faceless.video visually in Bubble, connecting AI services instead of writing code ([Bubble](https://bubble.io/blog/faceless-video)). The idea came from his own TikTok feed: he kept seeing faceless videos go viral and wanted to make them himself ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/faceless-video-breakdown)). A user gives the tool a channel topic, and it produces and posts the videos automatically. Team size and prices are not disclosed.

> "Failing 6 times before hitting a 7-figure ARR in 10 months with no code" — Jacob Seeger ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/3FvQoHUh2SglJvAJGkSF))

## How did Faceless.video reach $1M ARR?

Faceless.video reached $1M ARR, about $83K a month (calc), less than a year after launching in October 2023 ([founder-reported, Bubble](https://bubble.io/blog/faceless-video)). Seeger wrote on Indie Hackers that the company was bringing in six-figure MRR and that it took six failed products to get there ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/3FvQoHUh2SglJvAJGkSF)). He told Starter Story it is 100% bootstrapped ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/faceless-video-breakdown)). Total revenue is not disclosed.

## Why does Faceless.video sell to faceless channels?

Faceless channels post videos without a person on camera, such as narrated stories, facts or quotes over stock footage. Many people want to run them for income but do not want to appear on screen or spend hours editing. Faceless.video automates the whole process, which turns a time-consuming hobby into something a user can run in the background ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/faceless-video-breakdown)). The product sells a business model as much as a tool.

## How does Faceless.video compare with Formula Bot and Submagic?

Faceless.video and [Formula Bot](/cases/formula-bot/) were both built on no-code tools by founders without technical backgrounds, before AI app builders took over that role. Formula Bot's David Bressler also used Bubble. [Submagic](/cases/submagic/) is a closer product competitor, an AI video editor for short-form clips. Faceless.video's advantage was being early to a specific trend, faceless channels, with a fully automated workflow.

Our reading: Seeger's six failures were the real training. Each product taught him more about Bubble and about what people pay for. By the seventh, he could move quickly on a trend he saw in his own feed, which is what non-coders with no-code tools have always done best.

[StoryShort](/cases/storyshort/) sells a similar AI short-video tool, built by a former optician who clones products that already have traction.

## What can no-code founders learn from Faceless.video?

- **Failure counts as practice.** Six failed products came before a $1M ARR one.
- **Your feed is market research.** Seeger built for a trend he watched go viral.
- **Automate the whole job.** Faceless.video sells a channel that runs itself, not a single editing step.
- **No-code still scales.** A Bubble app served more than 850,000 users.
