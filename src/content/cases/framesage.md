FrameSage revenue started the way few film-finance startups do: with a founder who could not code and a back end built in 10 days. Oskar Munck af Rosenschöld, a pharma project manager who had never coded outside school, used the AI app builder Lovable to build FrameSage's plumbing. A few months later the platform was live and had booked its first $50,000 in revenue, Forbes reported in July 2025 ([media-reported, Forbes](https://forbes.com.au/news/entrepreneurs/vibe-coding-turned-this-swedish-ai-unicorn-into-the-fastest-growing-software-startup-ever/)). The idea came from a film producer friend over a fika, a Swedish coffee break.

## What does FrameSage do for film investors?

FrameSage assesses film projects for the people who finance them. It combines quantitative and qualitative assessments of film investments and is open to filmmakers, producers and finance professionals, according to a post on chairman Simon Kölle's website ([Simon Kölle](https://www.simonkolle.com/post/framesage-opens-its-doors-to-the-film-industry-and-private-equity)). Its analysis tool is called Quantify. Munck af Rosenschöld came to the idea from outside both film and software, through a producer friend.

## What did Lovable do for FrameSage in its first 10 days?

He used Lovable, the Stockholm AI app builder, to build the company's back end in about 10 days, according to Forbes ([media-reported, Forbes](https://forbes.com.au/news/entrepreneurs/vibe-coding-turned-this-swedish-ai-unicorn-into-the-fastest-growing-software-startup-ever/)). He had worked as a project manager in pharma and had never coded outside school. Forbes used FrameSage as an example of the founders adopting [Lovable](/cases/lovable/), which it described as the fastest growing software startup ever.

> "This has saved us tens of thousands of dollars on developers and around four months' work." — Oskar Munck af Rosenschöld ([founder-reported via Forbes](https://forbes.com.au/news/entrepreneurs/vibe-coding-turned-this-swedish-ai-unicorn-into-the-fastest-growing-software-startup-ever/))

That saving is his own estimate, not an audited figure.

## How much revenue has FrameSage made?

FrameSage had booked its first $50,000 in revenue by the time of the Forbes story in July 2025 ([media-reported, Forbes](https://forbes.com.au/news/entrepreneurs/vibe-coding-turned-this-swedish-ai-unicorn-into-the-fastest-growing-software-startup-ever/)). Monthly revenue, recurring revenue, pricing and team size are not disclosed. We found no later figure, so the $50,000 is a milestone, not a run rate.

## How is FrameSage different from Plinq and Lumoo?

FrameSage, [Plinq](/cases/plinq/) and [Lumoo](/cases/lumoo/) were all built on Lovable by founders who are not engineers. Plinq's founder was a growth marketer and Lumoo's founders came from fashion retail, so both built for an industry they knew. Munck af Rosenschöld came from pharma, not film; the industry knowledge came from his producer friend. FrameSage also sells to a smaller, wealthier audience, film financiers, so a few customers can produce a first $50,000 milestone.

Our reading: the non-coder pattern still holds, but here the domain expert and the builder are two different people. The AI tool shrank the build; the friend supplied the market.

## What lessons does FrameSage offer founders outside tech?

- **Borrow domain knowledge.** The film insight came from a friend, not the founder's own career.
- **A back end can take days.** Lovable let FrameSage go from idea to live product in months.
- **High-value buyers make small numbers meaningful.** A handful of financiers can produce a first revenue milestone.
- **Treat savings estimates as estimates.** "Tens of thousands of dollars" is the founder's own figure.
