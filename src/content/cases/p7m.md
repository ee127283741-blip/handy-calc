Italy requires digitally signed documents in many official exchanges, and they often arrive as .p7m files that ordinary computers cannot open. P7M.io turned that annoyance into a small business. Eight months after a March 2026 launch it showed $24,314 in all-time revenue and $3,658 in the last 30 days ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/confidential-startup-70)), mostly from one-off payments rather than subscriptions.

## What problem does P7M.io solve for Italian professionals?

P7M.io opens Italian .p7m (CAdES) signed files in the browser without uploading them, and also ships iOS and Android apps. The company-written listing says no major operating system supports the format and free alternatives upload documents to third-party servers ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/confidential-startup-70)). The audience is lawyers, accountants, public-sector employees and businesses in Italy, about 50,000 users.

> "Ranked #3 on Google for the core queries." — P7M.io listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/confidential-startup-70))

## How does P7M.io make money from one-off documents?

P7M.io collected $3,658 in the 30 days before the November 2026 snapshot, against just $611 of MRR from 320 active subscriptions ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/confidential-startup-70)). All-time revenue was $24,314. Unlike most cases here, TrustMRR verified these numbers through a RevenueCat API key, not Stripe.

A yearly plan at EUR 12.99 adds only about a euro a month to MRR, which is why 320 subscriptions produce so little MRR. The real engine is pay-per-document: the listing claims 3,297 paid orders to date.

## How did a solo founder get P7M.io found?

The listing names two channels, app store optimization and SEO ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/confidential-startup-70)). That fits the product: people search for how to open a .p7m file at the moment they need it. The founder, Andrea, works alone from Italy, and the stack spans JavaScript, Swift, SwiftUI, Kotlin, Node.js, Python, Vercel, Stripe and RevenueCat. The listing also says there are no tracking SDKs, which matters for a tool handling signed legal documents.

## What lessons does P7M.io hold for utility builders?

- **Mandatory formats create demand.** Italy's .p7m files need a reader whether users like it or not.
- **Pay-per-use can beat subscriptions.** $3,658 in 30 days came mostly from one-off payments, not $611 of MRR.
- **Privacy is a feature.** No upload and no tracking SDKs suit legal paperwork.
- **Search intent is the channel.** SEO and app store optimization are the only listed channels.
