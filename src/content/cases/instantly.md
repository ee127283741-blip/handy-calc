Instantly started as a tool nobody was supposed to buy. A small lead-generation agency was tired of paying more every time it added another sending inbox, so its developer built something for internal use. The tool worked well enough that the agency became the side project and the tool became the company. Within months of its public launch it was at roughly $200K MRR ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)), with no outside money at all.

## Why did a lead-gen agency build Instantly for itself?

Raul Kaevand is Estonian and had spent more than a decade in online marketing, running agencies and working through startups that failed ([Discovered Labs](https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/zero-to-8m-arr-in-less-than-2-years-raul-kaevand-co-founder-of-instantly-ai-saas-growth)). He learned cold outreach while selling at Nick Osmond's Sales Process, where emails had to be sent manually, one account at a time. That tedium planted the seed. It was also where he met Nils Schneider, who later became a co-founder.

The sharper pain came from his own lead-generation agency, which he started around mid-2021 ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)). The existing tools charged per sending email account. Cold email at scale needs many inboxes, so growing the agency meant paying thousands every month just for the right to send. The team had a strong developer, and rather than keep paying, they built their own tool for themselves, with no plan to launch it. The idea came out of a cost line that kept growing.

## What does Instantly actually do?

Instantly is a SaaS for cold email outreach at scale, and Latka compared it to Lemlist ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)). A user connects their sending email accounts, as many as they want, and attaches them to campaigns. The tool then sends cold email campaigns across those inboxes, so volume spreads over many senders rather than piling up on one.

The second core piece is automated email warm-up. New inboxes that suddenly send lots of mail look suspicious to providers, so Instantly runs a warm-up network in which real accounts exchange mail with each other to build sender reputation before and during campaigns ([Discovered Labs](https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/zero-to-8m-arr-in-less-than-2-years-raul-kaevand-co-founder-of-instantly-ai-saas-growth)). Unlimited accounts, a shared warm-up pool and campaign sending are the product as described in the sources. Instantly deliberately kept the feature set narrow, a choice covered below.

The partnership behind it began on the internet. About two years before the Latka interview, Raul posted on Reddit's co-founder subreddit and on Indie Hackers, and met the developer he calls Sumeet ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)). Their first idea was in travel, and COVID killed it. They reconnected around summer 2021, when the agency needed a sending tool.

The company ended up with four co-founders: the developer and three marketing and sales people from the agency, including Nils and Reio, an old friend of Raul's ([Discovered Labs](https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/zero-to-8m-arr-in-less-than-2-years-raul-kaevand-co-founder-of-instantly-ai-saas-growth)). The equity split reflected who wrote the code. The developer holds 50% and the other three share the remaining 50% ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)).

The agency was making about $10-15K a month when they started building, and about $30-40K MRR when Instantly launched publicly ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)). About 22 agency clients became the first users. The choice to shut down the agency was made on a walk in Barcelona with Nils, and offboarding clients took 2-3 months.

## How did Instantly sell cold email with cold email?

Marketing started with the product itself. The team used Instantly to send cold email about Instantly, which cost almost nothing per customer ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)). The messages were short and plain, roughly: we built a tool that helped us get to 100K MRR in a few months, want to see if we can do the same for you? Next came an AppSumo launch that brought in about 3,000 people even as some of the community roasted them. Raul used the onboarding checklist to steer those signups into a Facebook group, which he named "Cold Email Masterclass by Instantly" on purpose so the category keyword came first in Facebook search. It grew to about 6,000 members, then to 18,000+ by May 2023 ([Discovered Labs](https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/zero-to-8m-arr-in-less-than-2-years-raul-kaevand-co-founder-of-instantly-ai-saas-growth)). Content on Twitter, YouTube, docs, SEO, podcasts, affiliates and eventually ads came later.

The pricing was the real innovation. Competitors charged per email account, but the developer found warm-up was cheap to build, so Instantly launched with unlimited warm-up and unlimited connected accounts instead of per-seat pricing ([Discovered Labs](https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/zero-to-8m-arr-in-less-than-2-years-raul-kaevand-co-founder-of-instantly-ai-saas-growth)). AppSumo also served to fill the warm-up pool with real accounts, a chicken-and-egg problem Raul compared to Tinder. Exact plan prices are not disclosed, but ARPU was about $70 a month ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)).

> "Can actually use our own tool to get more clients, and we can use our own success story as selling point." — Raul Kaevand ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022))

## How much revenue did Instantly make?

The best-documented figure is from October 2022. Instantly was at about $200K MRR and $2.4M ARR, with 2,900 paying monthly customers (AppSumo lifetime users excluded), an ARPU of about $70 a month, and a team of 15: four co-founders, six or seven support staff and two developers ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)). Outside funding was $0. Total lifetime revenue is not disclosed. Later claims are larger but weaker. A May 2023 podcast is titled "Zero to $8M ARR in less than 2 years", but Raul doesn't state that number in the episode ([unverified, Discovered Labs](https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/zero-to-8m-arr-in-less-than-2-years-raul-kaevand-co-founder-of-instantly-ai-saas-growth)). By then he did report a team of almost 30. A March 2024 video titled "$0 to $20M ARR in 3 years" has a description by Liam Dunne, a former growth contractor, saying he joined at $40K MRR and helped scale it past $20M ARR while it stayed bootstrapped ([unverified, YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XrDYf3_Yovc)). The latest figures for 2025-2026 are not disclosed.

The timeline: in mid-2021 Raul started the agency and reconnected with the developer, and the internal tool followed. Instantly launched publicly about nine months before October 2022. It had about 10 users before AppSumo ([Discovered Labs](https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/zero-to-8m-arr-in-less-than-2-years-raul-kaevand-co-founder-of-instantly-ai-saas-growth)), reached $15K a month in May 2022, and hit about $200K MRR by October 2022 ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)). The titled $8M (May 2023) and $20M+ (March 2024) claims remain unverified.

## What went wrong as Instantly grew?

Churn was the first hard number. Monthly churn ran at about 10% ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)), and a churn survey showed that people left because they weren't getting results. Cold email is a means to an end, and a customer who books no meetings doesn't care how many inboxes they can connect. The team responded with webinars and with proactive emails and calls to users who hadn't run a campaign recently.

The lifetime deal had a cost too. Raul warned that lifetime deals bring the wrong customers and aren't viable long term. The AppSumo deal was cut short, and the team adopted a strict rule of no more lifetime deals, going straight to MRR instead ([Discovered Labs](https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/zero-to-8m-arr-in-less-than-2-years-raul-kaevand-co-founder-of-instantly-ai-saas-growth)).

Growth brought other problems. Raul named scaling the database and support for thousands of customers, competitors copying the model, Google and Outlook cracking down on cold email, and managing people, for which he recommends the book Peopleware. The platform risk is structural: Instantly's product sits on top of inbox providers that it doesn't control.

## How does Instantly compare with Gojiberry AI and Harvey?

[Gojiberry AI](/cases/gojiberry-ai/) and Instantly both sell outbound and both sell it by using their own product. They bet on opposite ends of it. Instantly is built for volume: unlimited accounts and warm-up so a team can send a lot of cold email cheaply. Gojiberry AI is built for timing, reaching fewer people on LinkedIn when they show buying signals. Gojiberry AI's founder still sends thousands of cold emails a day for his own growth, which suggests the two approaches work together rather than against each other.

Staying bootstrapped was a choice, not a fallback. Raul turned down investor interest because he valued freedom and not having a board, and he said it was hard even to spend money when acquisition was that cheap ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)). [Harvey](/cases/harvey/) took the opposite, venture-funded path, raising large rounds to sell AI to law firms. The contrast shows two workable models: Instantly's near-zero acquisition cost made outside money unnecessary, while Harvey's long enterprise sales cycles made it essential.

[Rytr](/cases/rytr/) is the other case here that used AppSumo to seed its launch before building SEO.

For the data and research layer that feeds outreach tools, see [Clay](/cases/clay/).

Two AI sales rep startups sit on top of this kind of email infrastructure: [Artisan](/cases/artisan/), known for its "Stop Hiring Humans" billboards, and [11x](/cases/11x/), whose reported ARR was disputed.

## What are the takeaways from Instantly?

- **Attack the invoice you resent paying.** Per-account pricing was the agency's own pain, so the team knew exactly what to remove.
- **Make the product its own channel.** A cold email tool sold by cold email proves itself in every message.
- **Name the community after the category.** "Cold Email Masterclass" captured search demand that a brand name would not.
- **Use lifetime deals for one job, then stop.** AppSumo seeded the warm-up network and community; the team then went straight to MRR.
- **Treat churn as a results problem.** Customers left when they did not get results, so education became retention.
