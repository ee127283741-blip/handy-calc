"Building Autonomous Companies" is a big tagline for a one-person business. Agentibus backs it with a small, real number: $6,009 in monthly recurring revenue from eight active subscriptions in November 2026, and $66,652 all time ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/agentibus-o)). It is a clear example of the AI agent agency model, where a founder sells configured agents and ongoing care rather than self-serve software.

## What does Agentibus build for client companies?

Agentibus builds AI agents tailored to each client's processes. Its homepage, written in Italian, describes AI agents configured on the company's own processes, an Agentibus "AI Officer" as the single point of contact, and the first agent in production within 30 days of signing ([founder-reported, Agentibus homepage](https://agentibus.ai/)). The TrustMRR listing places it in the productivity market.

> "Building Autonomous Companies" — Agentibus listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/agentibus-o))

The listing names Pietro Del Franco as founder, April 2025 as the founding date and Estonia as the country, with a team of one and no outside funding ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/agentibus-o)).

On average, about $751. MRR of $6,009 across eight subscriptions works out to roughly $751 per subscription ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/agentibus-o)). Plan prices are not disclosed on the listing or the homepage, so this is the only view of what a client pays.

## Why does the Agentibus revenue line look so lumpy?

Because a handful of clients pay on a handful of dates. In the 30-day snapshot, verified revenue arrived on only six days, including one payment of $2,703 and one of $1,685, with zero on most others ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/agentibus-o)). That is normal for an agency billing a small number of accounts.

The same structure makes the business fragile. With eight subscriptions, one lost client removes about an eighth of MRR. The listing shows Stripe as the only tool in its stack, the founder had 37 followers on X, and no marketing channels are listed, so growth likely depends on direct relationships.

## How does Agentibus compare with Lindy and Sintra?

Lindy and Sintra sell self-serve AI agents to many small businesses; Agentibus configures agents for a few.

Our observation: [Lindy](/cases/lindy/) and [Sintra](/cases/sintra/) scale by keeping each customer cheap and self-serve. Agentibus charges each client far more than a Lindy seat and wins on hand-holding. That caps how many clients one founder can serve, but it can reach profitability with very few customers.

## What lessons does Agentibus offer AI agency founders?

- **Agencies need few clients.** Eight subscriptions at about $751 each already add up to $6,009 MRR ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/agentibus-o)).
- **A named contact is a feature.** An AI Officer as a single point of contact sells trust as much as technology.
- **Promise a deadline.** "First agent in production within 30 days" turns a vague AI project into a concrete purchase.
- **Concentration is the main risk.** With revenue arriving on a few days a month, every client matters.
