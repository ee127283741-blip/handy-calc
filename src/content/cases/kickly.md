Sports bettors already pay tipsters for picks. Kickly, a French product launched in June 2026, sells the same hope with an AI model instead of a person. Built by one founder, Matéo Nouyrigat, it had collected $40,623 in verified revenue by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/kickly-pronos-ia)). That is a fast first five months for a one-person product, and a useful look at a sensitive niche.

## What does Kickly predict for sports bettors?

Kickly gives sports bettors probabilities and insights on matches, which the listing says helps them make informed decisions ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/kickly-pronos-ia)). Its value proposition says the AI analyzes data the company calls exclusive and private. The audience is sports bettors, and the market tags are Games, AI and Analytics.

> "Pronostics sportifs." — Kickly company description ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/kickly-pronos-ia))

The page does not publish accuracy figures for the predictions, so how well the model performs is not disclosed.

## How does Kickly find its users?

Kickly lists Instagram, TikTok and X as its marketing channels ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/kickly-pronos-ia)). Sports content travels well on short-form social video, and a freemium model lets followers try free picks before paying. The front end runs on Next.js with Stripe billing. For a single founder, leaning on social platforms instead of paid ads keeps acquisition costs low while the product is still young.

## What lessons can founders take from Kickly?

- **Passionate niches pay early.** Sports bettors gave Kickly $40,623 in five months.
- **Social video fits sports.** Instagram, TikTok and X are its listed channels.
- **Freemium builds trust before payment.** Free picks let users test the model.
- **Accuracy claims need proof.** Prediction accuracy is not disclosed on the listing.
