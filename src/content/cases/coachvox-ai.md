After Jodie Cook sold her social media agency in 2021, her calendar filled up with people who wanted coaching, mentoring, podcast interviews and advice on selling their own companies. She did not want to be busy for the sake of it, and she no longer had a team to deliver. So she asked how she could clone herself. The answer became Coachvox AI, which she says reached six-figure ARR before its public launch ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/jodie-cook)).

## What did selling an agency teach Coachvox AI's founder?

Cook ran her agency as a lifestyle business, so systemised that she could travel the world and compete in powerlifting. When covid hit in March 2020, the company shrank by 25% in a week as travel, hospitality and events clients cancelled. Four intense months of daily emails, twice-weekly webinars and calls to the whole database brought it back to its old size and 20% beyond.

Then she asked herself whether she would start this company from scratch today. The answer was no. A broker introduced 12 potential buyers, six took second meetings and three made offers. Six months later the seven-figure sale was complete, and she had written a book, Ten Year Career, along the way.

## How does an AI coach built from your content work?

Creators, authors, thought leaders and entrepreneurs already have frameworks, books, articles and podcasts. Coachvox AI lets them upload that content and start chatting to an AI version of themselves. When they publish it and share the link, the AI coaches, mentors and answers questions the way they would, and it collects leads and data on its website and in membership groups.

The company began as a platform for business coaching by voice note. In early 2023 it started building AI coaches: Jodie AI as a prototype business coach, and an unofficial Ray Dalio AI so entrepreneurs could be coached on his expertise for free. The team sent Ray the login details.

> "How can I clone myself without building another team?" — Jodie Cook ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/jodie-cook))

## Why does Coachvox AI target creators?

Because they have already done the hard work. Cook's pitch is that creators who have spent years writing and recording can now be everywhere at once without being busier, and earn more from content they already made. They are no longer limited by their own time or energy. It also fits her own philosophy: fill your days only with things you are excited about, and say no to the "seven out of ten" requests.

## What revenue did Coachvox AI make before launch?

Cook reported six-figure ARR before the public launch, with a waitlist being built to open more spaces in September 2023 ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/jodie-cook)). An exact figure, monthly revenue and pricing are not disclosed. The company had two founders and no employees.

## How does Coachvox AI compare with My AskAI and Chatbase?

[Chatbase](/cases/chatbase/) and [My AskAI](/cases/my-askai/) also train AI on a client's own content, but for customer support: the bot answers questions about a product. Coachvox AI trains on a person's ideas and sells the AI as that person, a coach or mentor. The technology is similar; the buyer and the promise are very different.

That difference shapes the business. Support bots sell on cost savings against human agents. Coachvox AI sells on reach and income for creators, which suits a waitlist and a premium, relationship-driven sale more than a self-serve widget.

## What can experienced founders learn from Coachvox AI?

- **Solve the problem your success created.** Too many meeting requests after an exit became the product idea.
- **Ask whether you would start it today.** That question led Cook to sell her agency at the right time.
- **Package what people already made.** Creators' existing books and podcasts are the raw material.
- **A waitlist can be a sales tool.** Six-figure ARR came before the public launch.
