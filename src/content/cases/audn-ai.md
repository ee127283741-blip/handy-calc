Most AI startups sell to people who want their products to do more. Audn.AI sells to people worried about what those products might do by accident. Its agents attack a customer's AI systems, prove each hole in a sandbox and then write the patch. In November 2026, that pitch showed $34,332 in MRR and $112,460 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/audn-ai)), about 15 months after the company's founding date.

## What does Audn.AI test, and for whom?

Audn.AI runs external black-box penetration testing for AI companies and enterprises. Its TrustMRR profile names teams shipping AI, managed security providers and enterprise security teams as the audience ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/audn-ai)). The profile argues that most security models are trained on open-source data, while the highest-risk flaws sit in closed production systems such as binaries, public endpoints and AI behaviour.

> "Patch critical vulnerabilities before hackers exploit" — Audn.AI website ([founder-reported, Audn.AI](https://audn.ai/))

The company also describes an early commercial proof point: a paid pilot with a YC S25 voice AI startup that generated revenue tied to a retailer contract. TrustMRR lists the founder only by the company handle, so the founders' names are not disclosed on the sources we used.

## How does the Audn.AI attack loop work?

Audn.AI pairs a red-team agent that attacks with a blue-team agent that scores and fixes. The homepage lays out eight steps, and a finding only closes when the exploit that proved it stops working ([founder-reported, Audn.AI](https://audn.ai/)).

| Step | What the agent does |
|---|---|
| Recon | Maps endpoints, tools, auth edges and agent delegation paths |
| Learns | Builds a model of the system from its own responses |
| Digs in | Chains partial wins that a scanner would score as noise |
| Exploits | Turns a hypothesis into a working payload |
| Proves in sandbox | Runs against an authorised preview environment |
| Patches | Writes a fix scoped to the proven path |
| Deploys | Ships the patch to preview for human approval |
| Retests | Replays the exploit chain and its variants |

The site says the attackers were trained by 5,000 top-tier hackers over 1.5 years. That is the company's own claim. Findings map to OWASP, NIST and the EU AI Act, which matters to buyers who need paperwork as well as fixes.

## How is Audn.AI different from AI coding startups like Cursor?

Audn.AI is the mirror image of the AI coding wave. [Cursor](/cases/cursor/) and [Windsurf](/cases/windsurf/) help developers write more code faster; Audn.AI sells the check on what that code and those AI features expose. Both sides use agents, but the buyer is different: engineers on one side, security teams on the other.

Our observation: the scale gap is huge, and the logic is the same. More AI-written software means more surface to test. Audn.AI is VC-backed per TrustMRR, though the amount is not disclosed, and its revenue is still a few tens of thousands a month. It is a very early, very small player in a market that grows with the coding tools above it. A small team and $999 flat pricing make it look more like a security product for startups than an enterprise consultancy.

## What are the takeaways from Audn.AI's first year?

- **Sell to the side effect of a boom.** As companies ship AI features, someone has to test them; Audn.AI earned $112,460 doing that in about 15 months ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/audn-ai)).
- **Close the loop, not just the report.** A finding that ends with a patch and a retest is easier to sell than a list of warnings.
- **A headline price is not the average.** A $999 plan next to a much lower per-subscription average shows a ladder of smaller products underneath.
- **Paperwork is part of the product.** Mapping findings to OWASP, NIST and the EU AI Act speaks to buyers who answer to auditors.
