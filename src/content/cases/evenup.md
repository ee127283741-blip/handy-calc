The EvenUp valuation doubled in about a year without the company ever publishing a revenue number. EvenUp raised a $150M Series E in October 2025 at a valuation over $2B, bringing total funding to $385M ([founder-reported, EvenUp blog](https://evenuplaw.com/blog/evenup-2b-valuation)). About a year earlier, its $135M Series D had valued it at over $1B ([media-reported, Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/legal-tech-ai-unicorn-evenup-ai-doubles-valuation/)). EvenUp sells to one niche: personal injury lawyers. It makes their slowest document, the demand letter, in minutes instead of days.

## What does EvenUp do for personal injury lawyers?

EvenUp turns a pile of medical records, bills and case notes into a demand letter, the document a plaintiff's lawyer sends an insurer to start settlement talks. Rami Karabibar, Raymond Mieszaniec and Saam Mashhad founded EvenUp in 2019. Personal injury firms handle many similar cases and are usually paid a share of the settlement, so faster, better-documented demands affect both their workload and their income. EvenUp's newer products cover case preparation beyond the letter itself.

## Does EvenUp disclose its revenue or ARR?

No. EvenUp's revenue and ARR are not disclosed. What it does say is that ARR doubled year over year and that case volume on its platform nearly doubled in six months to about 10,000 cases a week ([founder-reported, EvenUp blog](https://evenuplaw.com/blog/evenup-2b-valuation)). Bessemer Venture Partners led the Series E. EvenUp's pricing and team size are also not disclosed, so outsiders can judge it only by usage, funding and growth rates.

## Is EvenUp heading for an IPO?

EvenUp's CEO has said a public listing is possible but has given no date. In an Axios Pro interview in October 2025, Rami Karabibar said the company could be on a path to the public market as the legal AI industry grows ([founder-reported, Axios Pro](https://www.axios.com/pro/enterprise-software-deals/2025/10/17/evenup-rami-karabibar-ipo-legal-tech)). For a company that does not publish revenue, an IPO would be the first time outsiders see its full numbers.

## How does EvenUp compare with Harvey and Legora?

EvenUp is the narrowest of the legal AI companies in this library. [Harvey](/cases/harvey/) and [Legora](/cases/legora/) sell broad platforms to large corporate law firms and in-house teams, and both disclose ARR: Harvey reported $190M ARR in January 2026 ([founder-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/25/legal-ai-startup-harvey-raises-200-million-at-11-billion-valuation.html)), and Legora said it passed $200M in 2026 ([founder-reported, Max Junestrand on X](https://x.com/MaxJunestrand/status/2102368939896361182)). EvenUp sells one workflow to plaintiff firms, many of them small, which corporate legal AI does not target.

Our reading: EvenUp's niche is its moat and its ceiling. Personal injury is a large, repetitive practice area where documents follow a pattern, which suits AI. But it is one area of law, so EvenUp grows by doing more of each case for the same firms rather than by selling to every lawyer.

## What lessons does EvenUp offer vertical AI founders?

- **Pick the slowest document.** EvenUp started with the demand letter, a task every personal injury case needs and lawyers dislike.
- **Sell where the money follows the outcome.** Firms paid from settlements value anything that improves or speeds up a demand.
- **Usage can stand in for revenue.** EvenUp reports 10,000 cases a week and doubling ARR instead of dollar figures.
- **A niche can still be a unicorn.** One practice area supported a valuation over $2B.
