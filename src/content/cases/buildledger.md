A builder's margin often disappears in paperwork: a supplier invoice that never gets passed on, a variation nobody billed. BuildLedger, an Australian startup founded in November 2025, sells software to catch those costs. A year in, it had 10 paying subscriptions, $1,470 in MRR and $4,357 in lifetime revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/buildledger)). It is a small business, but a clean example of vertical SaaS in a trade that rarely gets software built for it.

## What problem does BuildLedger solve for builders?

BuildLedger puts the whole job in one place: pricing the work, booking the trades, catching every cost, billing the client with proof attached and seeing the margin while there is still time to act. The listing says it cuts admin, captures supplier invoices and gives clients a transparent portal ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/buildledger)).

> "Build more. Manage less." — BuildLedger value proposition on TrustMRR ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/buildledger))

Extra details on the listing: the data is hosted in Australia, the product connects to Xero, and invoices are captured with AI. The backend runs on Laravel with Stripe for billing.

## Why does BuildLedger focus only on Australia?

The audience is defined as Australian builders and construction businesses, and the product leans on local details: Australian hosting and a Xero integration ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/buildledger)). Narrowing to one country lets a tiny team speak the customer's language on tax, invoicing and contracts.

The trade-off is reach. The website, buildledger.com.au, shows an Ahrefs Domain Rating of 0 out of 100 on the listing, so search traffic is not yet doing the selling, and no marketing channels are listed.

## How is BuildLedger different from Calendesk and Plutio?

All three sell operations software to small service businesses, but BuildLedger is the only one built for a single trade.

Our observation: Calendesk and Plutio sell broad tools to many kinds of business, so each customer pays less for a general fit. BuildLedger starts at $150 a month, a high entry price for a ten-customer startup, which only works because it speaks to one trade's specific money leaks.

## What lessons does BuildLedger hold for vertical SaaS founders?

- **Sell the margin, not the features.** The pitch is about seeing your margin while you can still fix it.
- **Local integrations are a moat.** Australian hosting and Xero make the product feel native to its market.
- **Ten customers can justify a real price.** About $147 per subscription shows trades will pay for fewer admin hours.
- **Distribution is the open question.** A Domain Rating of 0 and no listed channels mean growth still depends on direct selling.
