Most founders skip user research because the tools cost more than the idea is worth. Ryan Byrne built ValidateThat to change that, and in its first year it brought in $8,074 in verified revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/validatethat)). The US product, founded in November 2025, had 22 paying subscriptions in November 2026. It is a small business, but a clean example of a research tool priced for indie hackers.

## What does ValidateThat do for an untested business idea?

ValidateThat takes a written idea, returns a market verdict, and then lets the user run studies with real people to prove it. The listing names card sorts, tree tests, prototype tests and surveys, with participants recruited through Prolific, and says studies can now be embedded on the user's own website ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/validatethat)).

> "Don't Guess. Validate That!" — ValidateThat company description ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/validatethat))

The audience is UX teams, indie hackers, educators and students.

## Why did Ryan Byrne build ValidateThat?

The listing gives a one-line origin story: it was built by one person who got tired of overpriced research tools ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/validatethat)). That frustration shapes the pricing, with a free tier and paid plans under fifty dollars a month. Growth channels are SEO and Google Ads, on a React, Redis and Vercel stack with Stripe billing.

## What lessons does ValidateThat hold for indie SaaS founders?

- **Price against bloated incumbents.** A $19 Starter plan targets people priced out of research suites.
- **Borrow supply from a partner.** Prolific provides participants, so ValidateThat need not build a panel.
- **Embedding widens use.** Studies on the user's own site add a reason to stay subscribed.
- **Early numbers are small but real.** 22 subscriptions and $8,074 all time, verified through Stripe.
