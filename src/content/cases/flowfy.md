Flowfy revenue comes from a budgeting app made by someone who used to sell products online, not write software. Alberto Pérez ran a dropshipping store, found Rork through a YouTube video and built a budget app in a couple of days without ever having written code. Rork's case study says Flowfy passed 100,000 downloads, reached #3 in Finance in Spain and made $22K in total at more than $300 a day ([founder-reported, Rork](https://rork.com/blog/alberto-flowfy-case-study)). Growth came without paid ads.

## How did a dropshipper end up building Flowfy?

Alberto Pérez built Flowfy by describing it to Rork, which generated the iPhone app. Rork says he had never written a line of code and published the app to the App Store after a couple of days of building ([Rork](https://rork.com/blog/alberto-flowfy-case-study)). The App Store lists the seller as Alberto Pérez Guijarro and describes Flowfy as a way to track expenses, set budgets and see spending visually ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6754984723)). Team size and subscription prices are not disclosed.

> "a personal finance app designed to help you understand your money in a simple, visual, and intuitive way" — Flowfy App Store listing ([founder-reported, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6754984723))

## How much money does Flowfy earn each day?

Flowfy earns more than $300 a day, with peaks above $600, and had made $22K in total when Rork published its case study ([founder-reported, Rork](https://rork.com/blog/alberto-flowfy-case-study)). At $300 a day, that is about $9K a month (calc). The app is free to download, with in-app purchases ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6754984723)). These are self-reported figures in a vendor's marketing post.

## Why did Flowfy grow in Spain first?

Flowfy grew in Spain because a Spanish-speaking founder could market it there in his own language. Budget apps are a crowded category in English, but local-language markets have fewer polished options. Reaching #3 in Finance in Spain gave Flowfy free visibility in the App Store charts, which brought more organic downloads ([Rork](https://rork.com/blog/alberto-flowfy-case-study)). A dropshipping background helped too: Pérez already knew how to test products and market them online.

## How does Flowfy compare with Wrestle AI and STOPPR?

Flowfy, [Wrestle AI](/cases/wrestle-ai/) and [STOPPR](/cases/stoppr/) are subscription apps built by founders with no coding background. Wrestle AI used Rork for a niche sport; STOPPR cloned an existing app with Cursor. Flowfy entered a crowded category, personal budgeting, and won by focusing on one country and language. All three show that the product can be built in days now; marketing and positioning decide the revenue.

Our reading: an ecommerce seller is well prepared for the app business. Dropshipping teaches fast testing, ad-free organic tactics and reading what buyers respond to. Rork removed the one skill Pérez lacked, so his existing skills could go to work on software.

## What are the takeaways from Flowfy for ecommerce sellers?

- **Ecommerce skills transfer to apps.** Testing and marketing products is the same job in a different store.
- **Local markets are less crowded.** A Spanish-language budget app reached the top three in Spain.
- **Charts are free marketing.** A high category ranking brings organic downloads.
- **Build fast, then market.** Flowfy took days to build; the work after launch made the money.
