Small businesses that run Google Ads often pay for clicks on searches that will never convert, simply because nobody has time to prune the account. NotFair sells an AI agent that does the pruning. Started in April 2026 by Tong Chen, it reached $13,609 in monthly recurring revenue by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/notfair)).

## How does the NotFair agent change a Google Ads account?

NotFair connects to a customer's Google Ads account through Google's official OAuth API, analyses performance, identifies wasted spend and recommends fixes ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/notfair)). With the customer's approval it executes changes itself: adjusting bids, pausing underperforming keywords and adding negative keywords.

The scope has since widened. The listing says NotFair now manages ads across Google, Meta, X, LinkedIn, Reddit and TikTok, and can connect search, analytics, CRM and WordPress data. It also describes the product as open source and supported by Claude and ChatGPT, so the agent can be driven from those assistants.

## Why does NotFair give away 300 operations a month?

NotFair's free plan includes seven days of unlimited access and then 300 free operations every month, while the Growth plan costs about $79 a month billed annually, or $950 a year ([founder-reported, NotFair](https://notfair.co)). The free tier lets an advertiser watch the agent work on a real account before paying.

The numbers suggest the funnel is wide: about 3,000 users and 114 paid subscriptions ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/notfair)), so roughly 4% pay. The average subscription is worth about $119 a month ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/notfair)), above the Growth price, which hints at agencies or monthly billing on some accounts.

## How does NotFair compare with Cometly and Rank Prompt?

NotFair acts on ad accounts, [Cometly](/cases/cometly/) measures which ads drove revenue, and [Rank Prompt](/cases/rank-prompt/) tracks brand visibility in AI answers. All three sell to marketers, but at very different stages.

Our observation: Cometly shows how large the ad-tools market can be for a company that owns one clear job. NotFair is earlier and younger, and its bet is that advertisers will let an agent make changes, not just report on them. Approval before execution is what makes that bet sellable.

## What are the takeaways from NotFair's first seven months?

- **Agents sell when they touch money directly.** NotFair's pitch is cutting wasted ad spend, a cost buyers already see ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/notfair)).
- **Keep a human approval step.** Executing changes only with approval lowers the risk of handing an AI a live budget.
- **Official APIs build trust.** Connecting through Google's OAuth API is part of the product description, not a footnote.
- **Fast does not need huge.** $69,770 in seven months came from 114 subscriptions, verified through Stripe.
