When a small-cap company quietly files to sell new shares, existing holders often find out after the price drops. Dilutracker is built to catch that earlier. Josh May, the solo founder, launched it in January 2026 and had $9,683 in lifetime revenue and $1,507 in MRR by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dilutracker)). He has now put the business up for sale.

## What does Dilutracker read in SEC filings?

Dilutracker turns scattered SEC filings into one source-linked view of warrants, convertibles, shelf capacity, float and cash runway ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dilutracker)). The pitch is simple: see dilution risk before new shares hit the market.

The audience is quant traders, investment teams and banks. According to the founder, the product also includes an API and MCP product, so the data can feed other tools and AI assistants, not just a dashboard.

## Why has Josh May put Dilutracker on the market?

The founder says he wants to focus on other projects, and he frames the business as live and profitable.

> "I’m selling Dilutracker to focus on other projects." — Josh May ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dilutracker))

He lists organic search traffic, automated billing and email, and low operating costs as strengths, and names SEO, partnerships, wider data coverage and paid acquisition as growth paths for a buyer. The stack is small: Next.js, Stripe, Hetzner and Resend.

Our observation: AltIndex sells to a broad retail audience, while Dilutracker targets professionals with a much higher average ticket. Fewer customers can still carry the business, but each one expects data accuracy, which raises the bar for a solo operator.

## What lessons does Dilutracker hold for fintech side projects?

- **Niche data can carry a high price.** Plans start at $59 a month for one specific risk signal.
- **Public filings are a free raw material.** The value is in parsing and linking them, not owning the data.
- **APIs widen the market.** An API and MCP product lets the same data reach other tools.
- **A small, profitable SaaS is sellable.** Low costs and automated billing are what the founder pitches to buyers.
