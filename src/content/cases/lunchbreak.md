Lunchbreak sits in one of the most argued-over corners of AI: tools that make AI-written text read as if a person wrote it. The argument has not stopped it from earning. In November 2026 it showed $31,226 in MRR and $1,484,533 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/lunchbreak)). It is also for sale, and the founder's reason is plain.

## What does Lunchbreak do with AI-written drafts?

Lunchbreak checks a draft against AI detectors and rewrites it to sound human. The homepage lists Turnitin, GPTZero, Originality, Copyleaks, Writer and Sapling as the detectors it checks ([founder-reported, Lunchbreak](https://lunchbreak.ai/)). It pitches the rewrite as a way to turn ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini drafts into writing that sounds like the user.

TrustMRR frames the problem in two parts: AI drafts often sound robotic, and they can be flagged by detectors, including as false positives ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/lunchbreak)). The audience is students, researchers and professional writers. Schools set their own rules on AI use, and a tool like this is only as legitimate as the use it is put to; we report the business, not an endorsement of using it against an assignment's rules.

## How does Lunchbreak turn free checks into paid rewrites?

Lunchbreak gives away detection and charges for rewriting. Checking is free with an account, while Pro costs $10 a month billed $120 annually and adds unlimited essays and 10,000 humanization words a month ([founder-reported, Lunchbreak pricing](https://lunchbreak.ai/pricing)).

| Plan | Price (Nov 2026) | What it includes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | AI detection, 1 essay (about 750 words), 5 Copilot generations a day | [founder-reported, Lunchbreak pricing](https://lunchbreak.ai/pricing) |
| Pro | $10 a month, billed $120 a year | Unlimited essays, 10,000 humanization words a month, sources and citations | [founder-reported, Lunchbreak pricing](https://lunchbreak.ai/pricing) |
| Pro trial | 3 days, card required | 2,000 essay words and 250 humanization words | [founder-reported, Lunchbreak pricing](https://lunchbreak.ai/pricing) |

The funnel is clear: a free detector score creates worry, and the paid rewrite removes it. The homepage leans on that, showing one passage scored at 94% AI by Turnitin before the rewrite and 0% after. That is the company's own demonstration, and we have not reproduced it.

## Why is Lunchbreak's founder selling?

The founder is selling because their attention has moved elsewhere, according to a message on TrustMRR ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/lunchbreak)). The same message describes Lunchbreak as profitable and low-maintenance, needing minimal weekly oversight, with a validated acquisition funnel.

> "I’m selling because my focus has shifted toward larger ventures, and I’m no longer dedicating the time required to fully scale this product." — Lunchbreak founder ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/lunchbreak))

The founder points a buyer toward paid acquisition, partnerships and new distribution channels as upside. No profit figure or asking price appears on the page text we used.

## How does Lunchbreak compare with Textero and Jenni AI?

All three sell AI writing help to students, but they take different positions on how AI should show up in the work.

| Company | Position | Revenue figure | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lunchbreak | Detect AI text and rewrite it to read as human | $31,226 MRR (Nov 2026) | [verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/lunchbreak) |
| [Textero](/cases/textero/) | AI essay and academic writing help | About $20,000 average monthly revenue (Nov 2024) | [founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/textero) |
| [Jenni AI](/cases/jenni-ai/) | AI writing assistant for research papers | ≈$833K a month, implied from $10M+ ARR (Dec 2025) | [media-reported, TMTPost](https://en.tmtpost.com/post/7814928) |

Our observation: Jenni AI reached a very different scale by selling help with writing and citing, a pitch schools can live with. Lunchbreak sells against the detectors themselves, which makes demand steady but ties the business to an arms race it does not control. If detectors change or schools change their rules, the product's core promise changes with them. That may be part of why a profitable owner prefers to sell.

## What lessons does Lunchbreak offer about AI writing tools?

- **Give away the diagnosis, sell the cure.** A free detector score feeds a $10-a-month rewrite plan ([founder-reported, Lunchbreak pricing](https://lunchbreak.ai/pricing)).
- **Flat can still be valuable.** About $35K a month on average for 43 months makes a sellable, low-maintenance asset.
- **Know what your demand depends on.** A product built against detectors rises and falls with them.
- **A sale is an outcome too.** The founder chose to sell rather than scale, and said so openly.
