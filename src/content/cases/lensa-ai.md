In the first days of December 2022, feeds filled with fairy princesses and astronauts wearing the faces of people's friends. The app behind them was not new. Lensa AI had existed since 2018, and Sensor Tower estimated that it had earned almost $29M in its entire life up to that point ([third-party estimate, via TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/01/lensa-ai-climbs-the-app-store-charts-as-its-magic-avatars-go-viral/)). Then the same firm estimated $29.3M in just 12 days ([third-party estimate, via TIME](https://time.com/6240648/lensa-ai-psychology-behind/)). Four years of business, matched in under two weeks.

## How did Prisma Labs get from a 2016 filter app to Lensa AI?

Lensa AI came from a team that had already gone viral once and failed to cash in. NPR reports that five men from Russian tech firms founded Prisma in June 2016: Alexey Moiseenkov, Oleg Poyaganov, Ilya Frolov, Andrey Usoltsev and Aram Hardy. Their nine-person team built an app that made photos resemble famous works of art, and The Verge named it its best app of 2016 ([media-reported, NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2023-01-20/a-rocky-past-haunts-the-mysterious-company-behind-the-lensa-ai-photo-app)).

The first wave ended badly. NPR says Tencent, ByteDance, Facebook, Google, Apple and Snap all showed interest or held talks, and no deal came. A trademark suit from the maker of an app called Prizmia was won in court, but a company source told NPR it cost $600,000 in legal fees, more than the $400,000 settlement the plaintiff had offered ([media-reported, NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2023-01-20/a-rocky-past-haunts-the-mysterious-company-behind-the-lensa-ai-photo-app)).

> "There were some troubles. Visa issues and so on" — Aram Hardy ([founder-reported, NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2023-01-20/a-rocky-past-haunts-the-mysterious-company-behind-the-lensa-ai-photo-app))

The years after were rough too. A business-to-business pivot never took off, Moiseenkov left and Usoltsev became CEO. In March 2022 the company cut 30% of its staff and told employees to move outside Russia, and by NPR's January 2023 account about 90 people worked there, mostly in Cyprus. Yuri Gurski's company Palta, maker of the period tracker Flo, held more than 50% ([media-reported, NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2023-01-20/a-rocky-past-haunts-the-mysterious-company-behind-the-lensa-ai-photo-app)). Lensa AI launched in 2018 as a photo editor in that long, quiet middle period.

## How do Lensa AI Magic Avatars work, and what did they cost?

Magic Avatars turned 10 to 20 selfies into a pack of stylized portraits, using the open-source Stable Diffusion model rather than a model Prisma built from scratch ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/01/lensa-ai-climbs-the-app-store-charts-as-its-magic-avatars-go-viral/)). Prisma told TechCrunch it processes photos on AWS. Processing took about 20 minutes for one CNBC reporter, and styles included Fantasy, Fairy Princess, Pop and Anime ([media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/12/07/lensa-app-turns-selfies-into-avatars-with-artificial-intelligence.html)). The need it met was less practical than emotional. Max Kreminski, a UC Santa Cruz researcher, told NPR the app paired an approachable interface with people's wish to explore other versions of themselves.

The business model stacked two charges. A pop-up pushed a seven-day trial that rolled into an annual plan, and even subscribers paid again for avatars. The press recorded different annual prices within two weeks, which we can't explain from the sources.

| Item | Price reported (Dec 2022) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Annual plan, after a 7-day trial | $39.99 | [media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/01/lensa-ai-climbs-the-app-store-charts-as-its-magic-avatars-go-viral/) |
| Annual plan with unlimited avatars, as described by TIME | $35.99 | [media-reported, TIME](https://time.com/6240648/lensa-ai-psychology-behind/) |
| Annual access range | $14.99 to $49.99 | [media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/12/07/lensa-app-turns-selfies-into-avatars-with-artificial-intelligence.html) |
| Subscription offer on opening the app | $49.99 | [media-reported, TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/ask-these-questions-before-you-make-your-lensa-magic-avatars) |
| Avatar packs at the subscriber price: 50, 100 or 200 images | $3.99, $5.99 or $7.99 | [media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/12/07/lensa-app-turns-selfies-into-avatars-with-artificial-intelligence.html) |

We found no sourced ad budget or marketing plan. The visible channel was the output itself: people posted their portraits and friends followed, and TechCrunch later noted that some users came after seeing TikTok videos about the feature ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/13/the-ai-photo-app-trend-has-already-fizzled-new-data-shows/)). Lensa AI reached No. 1 on the iOS Photo & Video chart, ahead of YouTube and Instagram. Brazil supplied 31% of November downloads, while the US generated 58% of consumer spending ([third-party estimate, via TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/01/lensa-ai-climbs-the-app-store-charts-as-its-magic-avatars-go-viral/)).

## How much revenue did Lensa AI make during the avatar craze?

Nobody outside Prisma Labs knows: monthly revenue and total revenue are not disclosed, and the company would not comment on specific figures to NPR. What exists is a set of estimates that do not agree. Sensor Tower's numbers, reported by TechCrunch and TIME, are the most consistent public record. They are consumer spending estimates, not company accounts.

| Period | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2018 to Dec 1, 2022 | Almost $29M spending; ~22.2M downloads | [third-party estimate, via TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/01/lensa-ai-climbs-the-app-store-charts-as-its-magic-avatars-go-viral/) |
| 2022-11 | 1.6M downloads, up 631% from 219,000 in October | [third-party estimate, via TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/01/lensa-ai-climbs-the-app-store-charts-as-its-magic-avatars-go-viral/) |
| 2022-11 | More than $70M, per an unnamed company official | [unverified, NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2023-01-20/a-rocky-past-haunts-the-mysterious-company-behind-the-lensa-ai-photo-app) |
| Dec 1-12, 2022 | ~$29.3M spending; ~13.5M installs | [third-party estimate, via TIME](https://time.com/6240648/lensa-ai-psychology-behind/) |
| Early Dec 2022 | ~$11.8M peak US weekly revenue | [third-party estimate, Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2022-q4-unified-top-5-photography%20apps-units-us-600a8192241bc16eb890d396) |

The $70M line deserves suspicion. It says one month beat the app's whole estimated lifetime spending by more than double, and the feature launched only in late November. Neither source explains the gap, so we keep the figure labelled unverified. Sensor Tower even disagrees with itself on smaller points: TechCrunch cited 1.6M November downloads and TIME cited 2M, both credited to the firm.

Margins are just as murky. An insider told NPR a pack cost about 50 cents to process against a $3.99 price, which we work out at about 12.5% of the price ([calc, from NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2023-01-20/a-rocky-past-haunts-the-mysterious-company-behind-the-lensa-ai-photo-app)). Yet another insider in the same article estimated that half of Magic Avatars revenue went to computing costs. Both can't describe the same pack economics without some missing explanation, and profit is not disclosed.

## Why did artists and privacy experts push back on Lensa AI?

The backlash arrived within days and came on three fronts. Artists objected first. Digital artist Meg Rae called the product art theft, arguing that it samples work from artists who never consented ([media-reported, Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/culture/2022/12/15/what-is-the-lensa-app-and-why-are-artists-worried-about-it)), and users spotted scrambled signatures in some avatars. Prisma Labs answered in a Twitter thread that its outputs can't be described as exact replicas of any particular artwork ([media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/12/07/lensa-app-turns-selfies-into-avatars-with-artificial-intelligence.html)). That reply addressed copying, while the artists' complaint was about consent.

The second front was how the app depicted people. Female users said it sexualized and slimmed their portraits. A Wired writer reported fully nude results from headshots alone, and some users said it whitened their skin or anglicized their features ([media-reported, PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2022/12/08/users-complain-that-lensa-ai-selfie-generator-is-sexualizing-their-photos/)). Usoltsev said an NSFW filter was being built and placed the bias in the unfiltered internet data behind Stable Diffusion.

> "The creators acknowledge the possibility of societal biases. So do we." — Andrey Usoltsev ([founder-reported, PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2022/12/08/users-complain-that-lensa-ai-selfie-generator-is-sexualizing-their-photos/))

Privacy was the third. The app promised immediate deletion of uploaded photos, but CNBC reported that some privacy experts worried they could be kept, and that Prisma's terms said metadata such as geotags may be attached by default ([media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/12/07/lensa-app-turns-selfies-into-avatars-with-artificial-intelligence.html)). TechRadar found FAQ language saying avatars were stored as long as needed to provide the service ([media-reported, TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/ask-these-questions-before-you-make-your-lensa-magic-avatars)). In December 2022 Prisma updated its privacy policy to say personal data is not used to generally train its other AI products, and added a warning that users may still see content they find inappropriate ([media-reported, Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/privacy-and-data-security/viral-lensa-ai-art-app-revamps-privacy-policy-amid-backlash)).

## Did the Lensa AI boom outlast the winter of 2022?

The wider AI photo wave did not, and it faded within about two months. Apptopia tracked Lensa AI with more than a dozen similar apps for TechCrunch. The group peaked around mid-December at over 4.3M daily downloads and about $1.8M a day in spending. By February 2023 it was down to about 952,000 downloads and about $507,000 a day ([third-party estimate, Apptopia via TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/13/the-ai-photo-app-trend-has-already-fizzled-new-data-shows/)). That is a spending drop of about 72% ([calc, from the same figures](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/13/the-ai-photo-app-trend-has-already-fizzled-new-data-shows/)). These numbers cover the group; we did not find a Lensa-only series we could confirm.

Note another clash between data firms. Sensor Tower's December figure for Lensa AI alone averages about $2.4M a day ([calc, from TIME's figures](https://time.com/6240648/lensa-ai-psychology-behind/)), above Apptopia's peak for the entire group. The direction of the story is clearer than its scale.

TechCrunch offered several reasons for the slide. Commenters shamed people who posted AI portraits, many users wanted the novelty only once, some disliked paying a subscription for a one-off task, and near-identical rivals crowded the charts. At one point in mid-December AI photo apps held the top three US App Store spots. Then attention moved to ChatGPT, released on November 30, 2022 ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/13/the-ai-photo-app-trend-has-already-fizzled-new-data-shows/)). The people around Prisma saw it coming. A longtime investor told NPR everyone might forget the app within four months, as happened in 2016, and sources described brand talks with Disney and Marvel, with no deal imminent ([media-reported, NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2023-01-20/a-rocky-past-haunts-the-mysterious-company-behind-the-lensa-ai-photo-app)).

## How is Lensa AI different from Photo AI, HeadshotPro, BetterPic and Midjourney?

Lensa AI sold a fun moment, while HeadshotPro and BetterPic sell a specific job and Midjourney sells a general creative tool. All of them generate images with AI, but their public numbers tell different stories.

| Company | What it sold | Public figure | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lensa AI | Fun self-portrait packs inside a photo editor | ~$29.3M spending in 12 days (Dec 2022) | [third-party estimate, via TIME](https://time.com/6240648/lensa-ai-psychology-behind/) |
| [Photo AI](/cases/photo-ai/) | AI photos of yourself | $100,000 a month (Sep 2024) | [founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/pieter-levels-just-passed-100-000-a-month-in-revenue-with-photoai-NToMGI3ZjwSBOfTywZnG) |
| [BetterPic](/cases/betterpic/) | AI professional headshots | $20,016 gross revenue (Jul 2024) | [founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/betterpic) |
| [HeadshotPro](/cases/headshotpro/) | Professional headshots only | Revenue not disclosed | [founder-reported, Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/danny-postma-founder-of-headshotpro-interview-series/) |
| [Midjourney](/cases/midjourney/) | General AI image generation | $300M in a year, alleged in a lawsuit | [media-reported, NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/disney-universal-sue-ai-image-company-midjourney-unlicensed-star-wars-rcna212369) |

Our observation: Photo AI and BetterPic report far smaller figures and Midjourney a far larger one, but all three numbers describe a month or a year, while Lensa AI's best-documented figure is a 12-day burst. The comparison is imperfect, because estimated spending and founder-reported revenue measure different things. The quality bets differ as well. Danny Postma told Unite.AI that HeadshotPro goes beyond standard open-source generation with dozens of extra models, and focuses only on headshots. Lensa AI's differentiator was reach and a cheap, shareable result on a public model, which is also what copycats could reproduce. Midjourney shows that the copyright fight follows scale: Disney and Universal's complaint is where its $300M figure surfaced, while Lensa AI's dispute played out in public with artists.

## What lessons does Lensa AI leave for viral AI apps?

- **An app on a public model can earn fast.** Lensa AI used a public model and still drew an estimated $29.3M in 12 days ([third-party estimate, via TIME](https://time.com/6240648/lensa-ai-psychology-behind/)).
- **Shareable output is a growth channel, and so is its backlash.** The portraits spread because people posted them. TechCrunch says commenters shaming those posts likely cooled demand too.
- **One-time wants fit poorly with annual plans.** Some users wanted one set of portraits and resented the subscription, according to TechCrunch ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/13/the-ai-photo-app-trend-has-already-fizzled-new-data-shows/)). Pack pricing suited that demand better.
- **Have consent and privacy answers ready before the spike.** Prisma was rewriting its privacy policy within weeks of launch, while under public scrutiny.
- **Silence leaves your best number disputed.** Because Prisma Labs has published no accounts, a $70M claim and much lower estimates sit side by side ([unverified, NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2023-01-20/a-rocky-past-haunts-the-mysterious-company-behind-the-lensa-ai-photo-app)).
