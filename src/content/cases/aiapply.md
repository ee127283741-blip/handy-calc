AiApply started as a video of a product that did not exist yet. Aidan Cramer posted a demo of a browser extension that would automate job applications, and the response on Twitter was strong enough to find him a co-founder. The extension itself struggled, but the web platform that replaced it reached $25K in monthly revenue, growing 30% month over month, by April 2024 ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/aiapply)).

## What did Aidan Cramer learn before AiApply?

Cramer's first startup, Stride, connected college graduates with startup jobs and ended in a successful exit. He then led product strategy at a UK recruitment firm, which showed him how inefficient job applications are. His next project, PetVet.ai, used large language models to give pet owners veterinary advice, and he sold it on Acquire.com.

PetVet.ai showed him how quickly a consumer product built on LLMs could find users. AiApply combined that lesson with his HR background. Its main product is an application kit: personalised resumes and cover letters, job matching and ways to reach hiring managers, aimed at job seekers who want to apply faster and better.

## How did a demo video turn into AiApply?

The first "product" was a video. Cramer shared a demo of the extension idea on Twitter, and the engagement validated the idea and connected him with Peter Utekal, who had built browser extensions before. Together they shipped the extension, but it was hard to monetise and hard to grow.

So they pivoted to a web platform and kept a very tight loop with users, sometimes shipping updates daily. Cramer admits an early mistake: launching features based on assumptions instead of user insight, which caused unnecessary rework.

> "Starting with MVPs that are essentially just visual content, we've seen firsthand how social media can serve as a powerful tool for product validation." — Aidan Cramer ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/aiapply))

## Which channels grow AiApply?

Viral demo videos on Twitter and TikTok drove the first growth spurt. After that the founders spread their bets across channels so that no single one could sink them:

| Channel | How AiApply uses it |
|---|---|
| Short video | Demo clips on Twitter and TikTok showing the product solving job seekers' pain |
| Paid search and social | PPC on Google and Meta for immediate traffic |
| Influencers | Collaborations with creators who reach job seekers |
| SEO | Long-term, compounding traffic |
| Partnerships | Relationships with HR and tech businesses and influencers |

For retention they add job market tips and updates, and invite users to suggest features or report issues.

## How much does AiApply earn each month?

In April 2024, AiApply had $25K in monthly revenue, growing 30% month over month, and charged users $19 a month ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/aiapply)). The founders said it had been cash flow positive from day one and was profitable with a 98% gross margin. Total revenue is not disclosed. The Starter Story headline cites $15K a month, but the founders' own text gives $25K, so this case uses the higher, more specific figure from the interview body.

The business runs entirely online with two founders and no employees, on a mostly PHP stack with Stripe for payments.

## How does AiApply compare with BetterPic and HeadshotPro?

[BetterPic](/cases/betterpic/) and [HeadshotPro](/cases/headshotpro/) also sell to job seekers, but they sell a single output: a professional photo. AiApply sells the whole application, from resume to cover letter to matching. That makes AiApply a recurring tool for the length of a job search, priced monthly, while headshot products are mostly one-off purchases.

The growth playbooks overlap. All three rely on short demo videos and search, because people looking for a job are actively searching for help. AiApply's extra edge was its founder's recruitment background, which told him where the application process wastes the most time.

[Rezi](/cases/rezi/) is a closer rival on resumes, but it focuses on an ATS-friendly resume builder and its founder said in 2024 that it had stopped running ads.

## What lessons does AiApply offer?

- **Validate with a video before you build.** A demo of an idea found both demand and a co-founder.
- **Pivot when the format fights you.** The browser extension was hard to monetise; the web platform grew.
- **Don't ship features on assumptions.** The founders' own regret was building before listening.
- **Mix fast and slow channels.** Paid ads and influencers bring sales now; SEO compounds later.
