The big language apps teach Spanish, French and Japanese. Silq goes after the languages they skip, from Central Asia and South Asia. That niche was worth $16,385 in monthly recurring revenue and $146,014 in all-time revenue in November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/speak-silq)), only eight months after the app's March 2026 founding.

## Which languages does Silq teach that Duolingo skips?

Silq teaches languages of Central Asia and South Asia through interactive lessons, and it frames the mission as preserving languages that mainstream apps miss ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/speak-silq)). The listing does not give a full language list, so we will not name individual languages.

> "Duolingo for forgotten Central Asian languages." — Silq listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/speak-silq))

The audience is consumers. The most obvious buyers are diaspora families and heritage learners who want a language their parents speak but no major app offers.

## How did Silq earn $146,014 in eight months?

Silq earned $146,014 in all-time revenue between its March 2026 start and November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/speak-silq)). It held 1,412 active subscriptions, $16,385 in MRR, and collected $15,775 in the last 30 days.

## What does a Silq subscription cost?

Silq is free for three days, then starts at $8.25 a month ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/speak-silq)). A price like $8.25 usually reflects an annual plan shown as a monthly rate. MRR per subscription comes to about $11.60 ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/speak-silq)), above that starting price.

## How does Silq compare with Speak and Codedex?

[Speak](/cases/speak/) is a venture-backed AI tutor for widely spoken languages, and [Codedex](/cases/codedex/) gamifies learning to code. Silq shares Codedex's playful lesson format but takes the opposite market bet from Speak: tiny languages with no strong competitor.

Our observation: in a niche the big apps ignore, a small team does not need to beat anyone on features. It only needs to exist and be found by the people who were waiting for it.

## What lessons does Silq offer education founders?

- **Pick the languages incumbents ignore.** Silq's positioning is the gap in mainstream apps.
- **Borrow a familiar frame.** Describing itself as a Duolingo for Central Asian languages explains the product in one line.
- **Trials and annual plans front-load revenue.** $146,014 all time against $16,385 MRR suggests early cash collection ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/speak-silq)).
- **Mission helps marketing.** Language preservation gives heritage learners a reason to share the app.
