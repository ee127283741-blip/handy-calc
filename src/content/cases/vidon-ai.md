Alex Robinson's previous startup lived on content marketing, and it kept getting harder. Search any topic and you find hundreds of near-identical articles. Video worked much better for them, but cameras, editing and time were too much for a small team. Vidon.ai was his answer: press a button and turn a blog post into a video. Five months after launch it had $6K MRR and more than 200 companies using it ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/vidon-ai)).

## Why turn blog posts into videos?

Because companies already own the content. A business with a blog has dozens of articles that could reach more people on video-only platforms like YouTube and TikTok, get better engagement on social media and help with search. Robinson, who has a background in AI and an interest in psychology and marketing, gave himself six months to build and validate the idea. He did not want investment, so he knew he would have to get inventive with marketing too.

## What did Vidon.ai's first version get wrong?

The first version was a single magic button: paste a blog URL, get a video. Content marketers loved it, and also gave plenty of reasons they hated it. The biggest one: there was no way to edit the video afterwards.

After two months of feedback the team went back to the drawing board and spent a long time prototyping in Figma. Version 2 became a document editor that looks like Google Docs or Notion. Write text and it is spoken aloud, add a title and it becomes an animated title section, drop in an image and it appears in the video. Writers did not have to learn a video tool at all.

> "That's why I feel persistence, patience, and consistency are key for any entrepreneur." — Alex Robinson ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/vidon-ai))

## How did cold email sell an AI video tool?

Vidon.ai skipped a big launch on purpose. The small team wanted a steady trickle of early adopters while it tuned a new kind of editor, alternating one week of building with one week of marketing.

The first customers came from targeted cold emails to content marketing agencies, which produce lots of content and are open to tools that save time. The best version of the email was the product itself: the team took a prospect's recent blog post, turned it into a video and sent it over to use however they liked. Adding video to cold emails lifted engagement.

They also ran programmatic SEO, publishing content for niche blog authors on a learning centre and using their own API to add a video to every page, since video helps posts rank. A slimmed-down demo editor on the homepage turned visitors into users by getting them to the "this is cool" moment fast.

## How much revenue does Vidon AI make?

Five months after launch, Vidon.ai had $6K MRR from more than 200 companies ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/vidon-ai)). The bulk came from about half a dozen content marketing agencies on big contracts, and the goal was to diversify so the business relied less on a few large customers. ## How does Vidon.ai compare with OpusClip and aiCarousels?

[OpusClip](/cases/opus-clip/) also repurposes content into video, but starts from long videos and cuts them into shorts. Vidon.ai starts from text, which suits companies that write a lot and film little. OpusClip grew into a large consumer and creator business; Vidon.ai stayed a small B2B tool sold to agencies.

[aiCarousels](/cases/aicarousels/) repurposes ideas into another social format, slide carousels, and sells to individuals through SEO. Vidon.ai's cold email to agencies brought bigger accounts faster but concentrated revenue in a few customers. The two show the trade-off between many small buyers and a handful of large ones.

## What can B2B founders learn from Vidon.ai?

- **Start from content customers already have.** Turning existing blog posts into video sold time saved, not a new workflow.
- **Ship one button, then listen.** The missing edit feature only surfaced after real use.
- **Make the cold email the demo.** A free video of the prospect's own post opened conversations.
- **Watch customer concentration.** Six agencies paying most of the bills is a risk to fix early.
