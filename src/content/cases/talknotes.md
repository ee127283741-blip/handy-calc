In 2022 Nicolas Jeanne's dropshipping store was struggling and his main freelance client was leaving. He had one year of savings. So he locked himself in a hotel room for two months, learned to code, and then shipped 17 apps in a year, testing them like products in an online store. One sold for $65,000. Another, Talknotes, became a profitable AI business with about $5,000 in monthly revenue by April 2024 ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/talknotes-the-ai-voice-notes-app)).

## Why did a dropshipper learn to code?

Jeanne dropped out of college after a month and spent years in e-commerce and freelancing. Dropshipping taught him one thing he wanted to avoid: a business your supplier can destroy with one mistake. Software looked like something he could control.

He kept the dropshipping mindset, though. Instead of one big bet he tested lots of products quickly and moved on from the ones that did not stick. The sale of one app for $65,000 bought him room to keep experimenting ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/talknotes-the-ai-voice-notes-app)).

## How did a lazy tweet become the Talknotes app?

He wanted to write a Twitter post but was too lazy to type it, so he tried speaking it into Google Docs. The transcription was bad. He knew OpenAI's Whisper could do better, built a quick prototype, and liked the results.

Talknotes records a voice note and uses AI to turn it into formats like blog posts and bullet lists. Its customers are mainly content creators and entrepreneurs with lots of ideas. The core took three or four days to build, the rest of the week went into a landing page and polish, and the whole thing cost under $10 in OpenAI calls. He aims for a maximum of one week to build any new app ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/talknotes-the-ai-voice-notes-app)).

> "Anyone can code an app, but what matters is getting users." — Nicolas Jeanne ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/talknotes-the-ai-voice-notes-app))

## Which channel actually worked for Talknotes?

He launched on directories and Twitter and got over 1,000 free users and 7 yearly subscriptions in a week. The first "subscription" was a one-time payment for a year of access, because many people dislike subscriptions. He then added friction step by step: asking users to pay upfront, then introducing real subscriptions. Two months later a Product Hunt launch made Talknotes product of the day and took it to $1,000 MRR ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/talknotes-the-ai-voice-notes-app)).

| Channel | Result for Talknotes |

## How much revenue does the Talknotes app make?

In April 2024, Talknotes made about $5,000 a month in total revenue: $4,000 MRR plus yearly subscriptions that bring in cash upfront ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/talknotes-the-ai-voice-notes-app)). It had 5,000 users, more than 900 paying customers, monthly churn under 10% and MRR growing about 10% a month. Total revenue is not disclosed.

The ad maths is simple. Each customer costs about $50 to acquire, which he earns back within a couple of months because churn is low. A big discount on yearly plans gives him cash to reinvest in ads, which is how he reached break-even on advertising. Someone had already offered $150,000 in cash for Talknotes when it was at $3,000 MRR ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/talknotes-the-ai-voice-notes-app)).

## How does Talknotes compare with AudioPen and Formula Bot?

[AudioPen](/cases/audiopen/) sells nearly the same product, voice notes turned into clear text, and also launched in 2023. Its founder relies on word of mouth and affiliates and spends nothing on ads. Jeanne went the other way and built Talknotes on paid acquisition. Both are profitable one-person businesses, so the channel choice matters less than knowing your numbers.

[Formula Bot](/cases/formula-bot/) shares the scrappy origin: a founder with no traditional engineering background shipping an AI wrapper fast. Jeanne himself warns that the window for simple AI apps is closing, because the novelty is wearing off and many similar apps launch every week.

For voice-to-notes applied to medical visits, see [Freed](/cases/freed/).

## What should app builders take from Talknotes' lessons?

- **Test products like a store tests products.** Seventeen apps in a year found one sale and one business.
- **Cap every build at a week.** Getting users matters more than polishing code.
- **Paid ads can work if churn is low.** A $50 customer pays back in a couple of months at under 10% churn ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/talknotes-the-ai-voice-notes-app)).
- **Use yearly discounts to fund growth.** Upfront cash paid for the ads that brought the next customers.
