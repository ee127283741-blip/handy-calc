Six months. That is how long Maor Shlomo had been running Base44, a bootstrapped AI app builder he started alone as a side project, before Wix announced it was buying the company for $80M, with possible earn-out payments through 2029 ([media-reported, TechRadar](https://techradar.com/pro/website-building/wix-jumps-on-the-vibe-coding-bandwagon-with-a-new-acquisition)). Less than a year later, Base44 was one of Wix's fastest-growing products, with annual recurring revenue reported at about $100M in March 2026.

## What gap did Maor Shlomo see in AI app builders?

Shlomo, a repeat founder, wanted non-developers to build working internal tools and apps by describing them, with the database, auth and hosting already included so there is nothing to wire up. The pain is the gap between generating code and running an app: most non-developers get stuck on databases, users and deployment.

## Why does Base44 include the backend?

Large language models generate the app, running on a built-in backend: data, users, permissions and hosting. Because the backend is part of the platform, generated apps work immediately. The user describes what they want and gets a running app, not a code project.

He built it alone as a side project, launched in early 2025, without outside funding. Building alone kept decisions fast and costs low, and bundling the backend meant there was one integrated product to maintain rather than a code generator plus a list of third-party services. Team size is not disclosed; Shlomo started solo and hired a small team before the sale.

## How did a solo side project find users?

He grew it through word of mouth and posts on X and LinkedIn, user showcases, and partnerships with communities of non-technical builders.

Pricing is freemium with monthly plans based on message and usage credits. The free tier lets non-technical builders try the product before paying, and credits scale revenue with how much they build.

## What did selling to Wix change for Base44?

A solo founder in a hot category faces well-funded competitors, including [Lovable](/cases/lovable/) and [Bolt.new](/cases/bolt-new/). Selling to Wix in June 2025 traded independence for resources. Reports on the price also differ. Wix announced $80M plus earn-outs at the time ([media-reported, TechRadar](https://techradar.com/pro/website-building/wix-jumps-on-the-vibe-coding-bandwagon-with-a-new-acquisition)), while Globes later put the total at about $92M, with $18M in cash near signing and the rest contingent on targets that Base44 went on to meet ([media-reported, Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001542919)).

> "It has the potential to replace entire software categories by enabling people to create software instead of buying it." — Maor Shlomo ([founder-reported, TechRadar](https://techradar.com/pro/website-building/wix-jumps-on-the-vibe-coding-bandwagon-with-a-new-acquisition))

## How much ARR does Base44 make under Wix?

Wix described about $100M ARR on its March 2026 earnings call, which implies about $8.3M a month ([media-reported, MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2026-3-4-wixcom-ltd-stock/)). ARR at the end of 2025 was about $59M ([media-reported, MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2026-3-4-wixcom-ltd-stock/)). Wix paid $38M in earn-out payments in Wix shares in Q1 2026 ([media-reported, Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001542919)). Total revenue is not disclosed.

Growth kept accelerating under Wix. Globes reported ARR approaching $150M by mid-May 2026, and noted that Wix spent $90M on advertising in the first quarter of 2026, excluding its Super Bowl campaign, to capitalise on demand for Base44 ([media-reported, Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001542919)). Total revenue is not disclosed.

## How does Base44 compare with Lovable and Bolt.new?

[Lovable](/cases/lovable/) and [Bolt.new](/cases/bolt-new/) generate code and connect it to services like Supabase and Netlify. Base44 bundles the backend itself: data, users, permissions and hosting are part of the platform, so a generated app runs without any wiring. That makes Base44 the most "batteries included" of the three, which suits non-developers building internal tools.

The paths diverged too. Lovable and Bolt.new stayed independent and grew with venture or existing company money. Base44 took a strategic buyer early. Wix brought distribution and a large marketing budget, and Globes' figures suggest that combination grew Base44 faster after the sale than before it.

## What are the lessons from the Base44 acquisition?

- **One founder can build a category product.** Shlomo started Base44 alone as a side project, with no outside funding.
- **Bundle the hard parts.** Including database, auth and hosting removes the step where non-developers get stuck.
- **Strategic buyers pay for momentum.** Wix bought six months of fast growth, not years of steady revenue.
- **Earn-outs can be worth a lot.** Hitting the targets set at closing brought another $38M in Wix shares.
