FaceKit turns an iPhone's Face ID camera into a scoring tool for your own face. Less than a year after its December 2025 founding date, the app had $26,340 in MRR, 5,949 active subscriptions and $279,425 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/facekit)). It sits in looksmaxxing, a consumer niche that has drawn both fast money and public criticism.

## What does the FaceKit app actually measure?

FaceKit uses Apple's TrueDepth sensor to build a 3D model of the face and score it. The homepage shows a 3D structure score, a "potential" score, scores for traits such as redness, and a daily plan of skincare steps and AR exercise routines like "Jawline & Chin" and "Cheek Tone" ([founder-reported, FaceKit homepage](https://facekitapp.com/)). Daily scans are stitched into a weekly progress video.

The app also recommends skincare products tied to a skin scan, and it runs an affiliate program. The homepage claims 180k+ users, while the TrustMRR listing estimates about 120,000, so the count depends on the page.

> "Highly profitable & scalable consumer app in the looksmaxxing / face analysis niche." — FaceKit listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/facekit))

## How fast did FaceKit reach $26K in MRR?

FaceKit reached $26,340 in MRR within about 11 months of its listed December 2025 founding ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/facekit)). Revenue in the last 30 days was $26,681, close to MRR, which suggests most income is recurring.

That is a low figure, consistent with weekly or discounted annual plans, but FaceKit's prices are not disclosed on the pages we checked. Team size is not disclosed either. TrustMRR verifies the figures through Superwall, the paywall tool the app uses.

## Is FaceKit for sale, and what does the listing claim?

FaceKit's TrustMRR description reads like a sale pitch: it calls the app highly profitable and scalable and says the backend holds "very valuable data", to be explained later ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/facekit)). The page appears among startups for sale, but an asking price is not disclosed for FaceKit itself.

The build is lean. The tech stack lists SwiftUI for the app, with Supabase and Superwall behind it. The site's domain rating is 7 out of 100, which tells us search traffic is unlikely to be the main channel, although the listing does not name its marketing channels.

The low revenue per subscription also hints at a cheaper price point than the bigger apps, though we can't confirm their prices here.

## What are the takeaways from FaceKit's first year?

- **Niches move fast.** A face analysis app reached $26,340 in MRR within its first year ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/facekit)).
- **Hardware can be a moat.** Building on TrueDepth sets FaceKit apart from photo-based raters.
- **Retention needs a routine.** Daily plans and weekly progress videos give users a reason to stay subscribed.
- **Volume over price.** About $4.43 of MRR per subscription means FaceKit depends on thousands of payers, not a few big ones.
