The first emails sent by Lindy's AI agent gave the whole trick away. Asked to recruit engineers, the agent wrote to 50 strangers and told them, more or less, "the user wants me to send an email to 50 software engineers" ([founder-reported, The SaaS Podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/lindy-flo-crivello-450/)). Founder Flo Crivello was mortified and offered refunds. Customers waved them away. Those forgiving early users, drawn from a 70,000-person waitlist, kept the company going until the product caught up with the pitch. By July 2025, Crivello put revenue at high seven figures.

## Why did Flo Crivello abandon Teamflow to build Lindy?

Crivello abandoned Teamflow because its market disappeared. Crivello, a former Uber software engineer, built the virtual office after a prototype of a "spatial Zoom" took off during the pandemic ([founder-reported, EO](https://www.eomag.io/article/lindy-flo-crivello)). On the podcast, Crivello said the waves of COVID could be overlaid on Teamflow's growth chart. When people went back to offices, growth went flat. Rivals with strong teams and plenty of funding weren't taking off either, which convinced Crivello there was no market left.

The replacement idea came from inside the company. Teamflow had a meeting recorder that summarized calls with GPT-3, and its sales team asked whether it could update Salesforce too. Crivello's team generalized that step by step: any Salesforce field, then any CRM, then other tools ([founder-reported, The SaaS Podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/lindy-flo-crivello-450/)). The realization was that language models could take actions, not only write text. On January 1, 2023, the company committed to a full pivot ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/pivoting-to-a-6-figure-mrr-ai-business-kR3HL8woQelc5BD8iDcU)).

> "Don't build a mine until you've found gold." — Flo Crivello ([founder-reported, EO](https://www.eomag.io/article/lindy-flo-crivello))

The cost was personal. From a team of 50-plus, Crivello let go of two-thirds, including the entire go-to-market team, and called it one of the most painful parts of a founder's career. The money, oddly, was the easy part. Lindy is the same legal entity as Teamflow, and Crivello confirmed $52M raised in total, saying the vast majority was still in the bank at the time of the interview ([founder-reported, The SaaS Podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/lindy-flo-crivello-450/)).

## How did one demo video fill the Lindy waitlist?

One video worked because the audience already existed. In March 2023, Crivello posted a straightforward talk-to-camera demo on Twitter, built partly on Figma mockups and partly on working prototypes, and it produced a waitlist of 70,000 people ([founder-reported, The SaaS Podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/lindy-flo-crivello-450/)). The reach came from long practice. Crivello said a script on a server once sent a daily email counting tweets, against a goal of 20 a day, and advised going after quantity over quality.

Then the invites went out, and the product failed. It did not retain users, and the first version, little more than a prompt, often failed ([founder-reported, EO](https://www.eomag.io/article/lindy-flo-crivello)). What saved it was who had signed up. Crivello describes self-selected pioneers, some of whom paid a couple of hundred dollars a month just to watch the vision develop. That patience lasted roughly a year and a half to two years, in Crivello's estimate.

## What changed when Lindy became the "Zapier of AI"?

The fix was a new position and a new architecture, shipped together. "AI employee" was an appealing promise, but the product fell too far short of it. So Lindy 2.0 was pitched as the "Zapier of AI", under the tagline "if Zapier and ChatGPT had a baby", and its interface of boxes and arrows looked like a workflow tool even though it was an agent builder underneath ([founder-reported, The SaaS Podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/lindy-flo-crivello-450/)). Crivello calls this the "Notion head fake": look familiar first, and reveal the bigger vision later. The tagline itself was eventually retired, because many visitors had never heard of Zapier and the line was advertising a rival.

The rebuild took five or six months and began at around $100K MRR, a number the host raised and Crivello confirmed. A big prospect's list of exact use cases had shown that the old paradigm could not deliver what customers needed. Crivello's reasoning was that nearly all of Lindy's revenue lay in the future, so current revenue was worth risking ([founder-reported, The SaaS Podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/lindy-flo-crivello-450/)). Lindy shipped version 2.0 quietly, which Crivello now calls a mistake. Five days later, the YouTuber MattVidPro, a paying customer Crivello doesn't know personally, posted a rave review, and Crivello says growth inflected and kept accelerating. The servers started breaking too.

The product moved again after that. Notes for a later episode of Get Paid with Manny Medina say Crivello decided Lindy was stuck between product and platform, chose end users over developers, and rebuilt around autonomous agents once models improved ([founder-reported, Get Paid with Manny Medina](https://pocketcasts.com/podcast/get-paid-with-manny-medina/f7f2d380-250c-013e-e07d-0e1ab590d6db/he-built-ai-agents-before-anyone-knew-what-to-call-them-flo-crivello-get-paid-with-manny-medina/a16ef3b9-c930-487c-a9db-5f6890f61006)). Users also pulled it into places nobody planned. Clinicians asked for HIPAA compliance so they could use it to write patient notes ([founder-reported, EO](https://www.eomag.io/article/lindy-flo-crivello)).

## What revenue has Lindy AI actually reported?

Lindy AI has reported revenue only as a range: high seven figures as of July 2025, with no exact number. Asked about the size of the business, Crivello said "high seven figures of revenue", with 35 employees plus five or eight contractors ([founder-reported, The SaaS Podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/lindy-flo-crivello-450/)). Crivello named no time period, while the episode's show notes call the same number ARR. We keep it as a range and don't convert it to a monthly figure. ## What does Lindy charge for its AI agents today?

Lindy charges by the seat and by the credit. Its pricing page, checked in November 2026, starts new users with $50 in free credits that last 7 days. The Team plan runs from $49.99 per active user a month for 5,000 credits, shared across the workspace, and extra credits cost $10 per 1,000 ([founder-reported, Lindy pricing](https://www.lindy.ai/pricing)). Enterprise is custom and adds HIPAA compliance with a signed BAA, the demand that clinicians first surfaced. An everyday ask uses 2-250 credits, and a big build uses 1,000-2,500.

The page's wording is worth noticing. It calls the product a teammate and invites buyers to "hire" Lindy for roles such as bookkeeping and recruiting. In our reading, the "AI employee" pitch that failed in 2023 has returned now that the models can better support it.

[Clay](/cases/clay/) sells sales teams data and workflows rather than a replacement rep.

Our observation: Lindy's story is a useful counterpoint to 11x. TechCrunch's sources said 11x didn't separate trial periods from long-term customers when reporting ARR, so a bold AI worker pitch came with disputed numbers. Lindy made the same promise, found the product couldn't keep it, and retreated to a modest comparison until it could. Neither n8n nor Lindy gives an exact revenue figure, which makes both harder to rank than Clay or Artisan.

## What can founders learn from the Lindy pivot?

- **Read a market's death from your competitors.** Crivello concluded Teamflow had no market once well-funded rivals were failing to take off as well ([founder-reported, EO](https://www.eomag.io/article/lindy-flo-crivello)).
- **Position against what people already know.** "AI employee" overpromised, and "Zapier of AI" gave buyers an immediate mental model, even though the tagline itself later had to go.
- **An audience can buy you time, not retention.** Years of tweeting produced 70,000 signups, but the product still failed to keep users until the 2.0 rebuild.
- **Be willing to rebuild while revenue is small.** Lindy risked about $100K MRR on a five-to-six-month rewrite, and Crivello's logic was that the future revenue mattered far more ([founder-reported, The SaaS Podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/lindy-flo-crivello-450/)).
- **Don't wait for models to catch up before shipping.** Crivello's advice is to build slightly ahead of what today's models can do, and accept that early versions will embarrass you.

> "In AI, I would go even further and say that if your first version works, you're not thinking big enough." — Flo Crivello ([founder-reported, EO](https://www.eomag.io/article/lindy-flo-crivello))
