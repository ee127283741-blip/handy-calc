Habit apps are a crowded corner of the App Store, and most never pay for themselves. Zenit - Wellness Habits, a mental health app founded in Spain in March 2026, collected $6,375 in the last 30 days and held $4,029 MRR across 707 active subscriptions by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/zenit-wellness-habits)). Its founder says none of that growth was paid for.

## What is the Zenit wellness habits app?

Zenit is a mobile app for mental health and wellness habits, listed under Health & Fitness and Mobile Apps on TrustMRR ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/zenit-wellness-habits)). The listing is short: it does not describe individual features, pricing or team size, so those are not disclosed here. Subscriptions run through RevenueCat, the in-app purchase layer many small app teams use. That means the subscription and revenue figures below are read directly from billing data, not typed in by the founder.

## Why does Zenit lean on TikTok?

TikTok is the only marketing channel the listing names ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/zenit-wellness-habits)). Wellness content suits short video: routines, moods and small daily wins are easy to show in a few seconds. Pedro Pérez frames the result plainly.

> "Growth 100% organically. 85% profit margin" — Pedro Pérez ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/zenit-wellness-habits))

With no ad spend, the main costs are app store fees and tools, which is consistent with the margin he reports.

## Which lessons from Zenit carry over to other wellness apps?

- **One channel can be enough.** TikTok alone carried Zenit to 707 subscriptions.
- **Low prices need volume.** At under $6 per subscription, growth depends on reach.
- **Organic growth protects margin.** No paid ads is what makes an 85% margin plausible.
- **Verified numbers beat long pitches.** The listing says little, but RevenueCat data speaks for it.
