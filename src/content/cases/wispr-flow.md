The Wispr Flow valuation nearly tripled in nine months. Wispr raised a $280M Series B at a $2B valuation in August 2026, led by Menlo Ventures, bringing total funding to $361M ([media-reported, Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/17/wispr-2-billion-valuation-dictations-only-the-beginning/)). In November 2025, Menlo had led a round at $700M ([media-reported, Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/wispr-raises-280m-at-2b-valuation-from-menlo-ventures-to-build-voice-layer-beneath-every-app/)). Wispr's product is simple to describe: you talk, and Wispr Flow types for you, in whatever app you are using.

## What is Wispr Flow, and who built it?

Wispr Flow is a dictation app that turns speech into clean text across a computer or phone. It removes filler words, fixes punctuation and formats the result for the app it is typing into, such as email or chat. Wispr was co-founded by Tanay Kothari, its CEO, and Sahaj Garg ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wispr_Flow)). Fortune reported that the company sees dictation as a first step toward voice as the main way people control software ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/17/wispr-2-billion-valuation-dictations-only-the-beginning/)). Its founding date, plan prices and team size are not disclosed.

## Does Wispr disclose Wispr Flow revenue?

No. Wispr's revenue and ARR are not disclosed. Its founders told Fortune that revenue grew more than 150% a quarter for four consecutive quarters, and that Wispr Flow is used by millions of consumers and 100,000 businesses ([founder-reported, Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/17/wispr-2-billion-valuation-dictations-only-the-beginning/)). Growth of 150% a quarter means revenue rises to 2.5 times its level each quarter; four quarters in a row would be about 39 times in a year (calc), although the company gave no starting figure.

## Who pays for Wispr Flow?

Both individuals and companies pay for Wispr Flow, and the business side is large. Menlo Ventures wrote that Flow is used in 162 countries and more than 100 languages by tens of thousands of paying businesses ([media-reported, Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/the-keyboard-is-dying-on-schedule-the-text-box-is-next/)). Fortune's figure of 100,000 businesses is higher; the two likely count different things, such as all business users versus paying accounts. Kothari also pointed to people who use voice because typing is hard for them.

> "There's this whole class of people who are using Wispr to do things they wouldn't have been able to do otherwise." — Tanay Kothari ([founder-reported, Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/17/wispr-2-billion-valuation-dictations-only-the-beginning/))

## How does Wispr compare with AudioPen and Talknotes?

Wispr and two small voice-note apps in this library start from the same habit, talking instead of typing, but sell different things. [AudioPen](/cases/audiopen/) and [Talknotes](/cases/talknotes/) turn voice recordings into cleaned-up notes, and both were built by solo or tiny teams on modest revenue. Wispr Flow works live inside every other app, so it competes with the keyboard itself rather than with note-taking apps. [Otter.ai](/cases/otter-ai/) transcribes meetings; Wispr transcribes the user.

Our reading: Wispr's bet is that voice becomes an input layer for all software, and investors priced it that way at $2B. The open question is the one its own numbers leave out: how much revenue that growth adds up to. Until Wispr publishes a dollar figure, the valuation rests on growth rates and usage.

## What lessons does Wispr offer consumer AI founders?

- **Work inside other apps.** Wispr Flow types wherever the cursor is, so users do not have to change tools.
- **Polish is the product.** Removing filler words and formatting text is what separates Flow from raw speech-to-text.
- **Sell to people and to companies.** Flow has millions of consumer users, and tens of thousands of businesses pay for it.
- **Growth rates are not revenue.** Four quarters of 150%+ growth is striking, but without a base number outsiders cannot size the business.
