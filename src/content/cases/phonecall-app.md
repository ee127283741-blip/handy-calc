Phonecall App has 40,000 users and only $8 in monthly recurring revenue. That is not a failure: the browser calling tool charges by the second, so almost none of its money is recurring. In the 30 days before November 10, 2026 it collected $1,078, and $2,684 since it started in January ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/phonecall-app)). It shows why MRR alone can mislead when a product is priced by usage.

## How does Phonecall App work?

Phonecall App places VoIP calls to landline and mobile numbers straight from a browser. The listing says it works in Chrome, Safari and Firefox, encrypts calls and can be installed as a progressive web app, so there is no SIM, contract or app store download ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/phonecall-app)).

> "Call any number, worldwide. For a fraction of carrier rates." — Phonecall App value proposition on TrustMRR ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/phonecall-app))

The audience is travelers and remote workers who need to reach a phone number abroad. The stack is Angular and TypeScript on the front end, with Firebase, Google Cloud, Node.js and Stripe behind it; OpenAI and Anthropic are also listed.

## Why does pay-per-second pricing suit Phonecall App?

Pay-per-second pricing matches how its users behave. A traveler who needs one call to a hotel or bank does not want a monthly plan, so the product charges only for minutes used, with no contract ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/phonecall-app)).

The cost of that choice is predictability. Each month's revenue has to be earned again from new calls, and the 40,000 users figure says nothing about how many actually top up. Exact per-minute rates are not disclosed on the listing.

## How does Phonecall App differ from Voicy and DownConvert?

All three are small utility products with Stripe-verified revenue, but Phonecall App is the only one that sells usage instead of a subscription.

Our observation: on an MRR leaderboard Phonecall App looks like it barely exists, yet its 30-day takings equal about a quarter of DownConvert's MRR. Both are based in the United Arab Emirates. Any comparison of usage-priced tools has to use revenue collected, not MRR.

## What lessons does Phonecall App hold for calling apps?

- **MRR hides usage businesses.** $8 MRR next to $1,078 in 30-day revenue shows the metric does not fit pay-as-you-go products.
- **Remove every step you can.** No SIM, contract or download is the core of the pitch.
- **Free users are not customers yet.** About 40,000 users produced $2,684 so far, so conversion is the lever to pull.
- **Recent growth matters most.** Roughly 40% of lifetime revenue came in the latest 30 days.
