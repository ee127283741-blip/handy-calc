Most software companies take a decade to reach $100M in annual recurring revenue. Lovable was reported to have done it eight months after reaching its first $1M ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/14/loveable-projects-1b-in-arr-within-next-12-months)). The company did not start as a startup at all, but as an open-source project, GPT Engineer, that showed a language model could scaffold a whole codebase from a prompt. Lovable is the story of turning a popular experiment into a product non-engineers pay for.

## Why did Anton Osika and Fabian Hedin start Lovable?

Osika's open-source GPT Engineer showed that a model could scaffold a whole codebase from a prompt. The commercial question was whether non-engineers would pay to build real web apps by chatting. The pain is access: many people have ideas for software and no ability or budget to hire developers.

## How does Lovable work?

A user describes an app in chat. Frontier LLMs generate a React front end, with Supabase integration for databases and auth, GitHub sync, and one-click deploys. The user keeps chatting to change the app. The result is real code the user owns, not a locked-in page builder.

## How was Lovable built?

The company started in 2023 from GPT Engineer. The team turned the project into a hosted product, rebranded as Lovable and launched publicly in late 2024. It is venture-backed, not bootstrapped. Team size is not disclosed with a dated primary source.

## How did Lovable market and price its product?

Marketing came from viral demos on X and LinkedIn, an active community of "vibe coders", and templates people share. The open-source project had already built awareness among developers.

Pricing is freemium with monthly credit-based plans and team and business tiers. Credits tie revenue to model usage, which is the main cost.

## What challenges does Lovable face?

Model costs are the structural challenge for any AI app builder: every message to the builder costs compute. Credit-based pricing aligns cost with heavy model usage. When every user action costs compute, price in units that track that cost, and keep a free tier small enough to afford.

Competition is the other: [Bolt.new](/cases/bolt-new/) and [Base44](/cases/base44/) launched similar products in the same period. Lovable's later push, reported by TechCrunch in November 2025, was toward corporate employees.

## How much revenue does Lovable make?

Lovable reported $100M ARR in July 2025, which implies about $8.3M a month ([media-reported, The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/vibe-coding-platform-lovable-becomes-historys-fastest-growing-software-startup)). TechCrunch reported it went from $1M to $100M ARR in 8 months ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/14/loveable-projects-1b-in-arr-within-next-12-months)). In November 2025 the company said it was nearing 8M users ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/10/lovable-says-its-nearing-8-million-users-as-the-year-old-ai-coding-startup-eyes-more-corporate-employees)). Total revenue is not disclosed.

The timeline: company started from GPT Engineer in 2023; public launch as Lovable in late 2024; $100M ARR reported in July 2025; nearly 8M users in November 2025.

## Why did Lovable work?

Open source was the top of the funnel for a paid product. GPT Engineer gave Lovable credibility and an audience before it sold anything.

Letting non-developers ship apps unlocked a huge new market. The buyer is no longer only engineers but anyone with an idea.

Credit-based pricing aligned cost with heavy model usage, so growth did not destroy margins.

For another fast-growing AI company, this one selling to law firms and corporate legal teams, see [Harvey](/cases/harvey/).

## What can founders learn from Lovable?

Open source can be the top of the funnel for a paid product.

Letting non-developers ship apps unlocked a huge new market.

Credit-based pricing aligns cost with heavy model usage. When every user action costs compute, price in units that track that cost, and keep a free tier small enough to afford.
