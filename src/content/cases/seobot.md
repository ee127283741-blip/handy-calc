SEObot is a rare AI startup whose all-time number is far more striking than its monthly one. In November 2026 it showed $32,116 in MRR, but $1,929,728 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/seobot)). That ratio says something about how the product has been sold since John Rush launched it in September 2023.

## What does SEObot do for busy founders?

SEObot runs a founder's blog SEO end to end. The homepage lists keyword research, Google scraping, articles that average 3,000 words, internal and external links, images, related YouTube videos, fact checking and monthly re-linking ([founder-reported, SEObot](https://seobotai.com/)). Newer features include programmatic SEO, news posts, interactive mini-tools and backlinks, and articles sync to CMSs such as WordPress, Webflow, Ghost, Framer and Wix.

> "SEObot takes 100% of SEO work out of your way so that you can focus on building your product." — SEObot website ([founder-reported, SEObot](https://seobotai.com/))

The site claims SEObot has created over 200k articles, driving 1.2 billion impressions and 30 million clicks. Those are the company's figures, and we could not check them. TrustMRR describes the audience as "project-busy founders" and calls SEObot the world's first AI agent for blog SEO, a claim we have not verified.

## Why is SEObot's all-time revenue so high next to its MRR?

SEObot's all-time revenue is about 60 times its current MRR ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/seobot)). A company that had always sat near $32K a month would need about five years to reach $1.93M, yet SEObot is about three years old. The likely explanation is that monthly revenue was higher at some point and has since come down, or that a lot of revenue came from one-off sales. TrustMRR does not show the history, so we cannot say which.

The per-subscription average is above the $49 entry price ([founder-reported, SEObot](https://seobotai.com/)), so many customers pay for bigger plans. Team size and profit are not disclosed.

## How does John Rush sell SEObot?

John Rush sells SEObot mainly through his own audience. TrustMRR lists 116.6k followers on X ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/seobot)). The homepage invites questions by direct message to Rush on LinkedIn, X or email, which keeps the founder as the face of the product.

The site also cross-sells a sister product, ListingBott, to boost domain rating. SEObot's own domain rating on TrustMRR is 65 out of 100, high for a three-year-old site and a useful proof point for an SEO tool. The homepage's testimonials lean on concrete results: one customer reports 500 clicks a day and a $6500 client closed from the traffic ([founder-reported, SEObot](https://seobotai.com/)).

SEObot sits at similar MRR to two other AI marketing tools on this site, but with far more lifetime revenue. [ChatSEO](/cases/chatseo/) and [Leadmore AI](/cases/leadmore-ai/) both reported about $30K MRR.

Our observation: SEObot's figures are Stripe-verified while the other two are founder-reported, so like-for-like comparisons are loose. The bigger difference is distribution. ChatSEO also built audience-first, but SEObot rides a founder account with six-figure reach, and that kind of channel can produce strong launch spikes that later settle down.

## What lessons does SEObot teach about AI content tools?

- **Lifetime revenue tells a different story from MRR.** $1,929,728 all time against $32,116 MRR points to a bigger past or one-off sales ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/seobot)).
- **Use the product on yourself.** A domain rating of 65 is the best ad an SEO tool can run.
- **A founder audience is a channel.** 116.6k X followers give SEObot reach that paid ads would struggle to match.
- **Results beat features in testimonials.** Clicks a day and a closed client say more than a list of article lengths.
