Replit revenue was almost nothing for most of the company's first eight years. Then it jumped. In about a year, annualized revenue went from $2.8M to $150M, TechCrunch reported in September 2025 ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/replit-hits-3b-valuation-on-150m-annualized-revenue)). The browser coding tool had been around since 2016. What changed was the customer: Replit stopped selling mainly to programmers and started selling an AI agent to people who had never written code.

## Why did Replit take nine years to find its market?

Replit started as an online editor for students and hobbyist programmers, and it took a pivot to non-programmers to find paying demand. TechCrunch says Amjad Masad, Faris Masad and designer Haya Odeh founded it in 2016 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/replit-hits-3b-valuation-on-150m-annualized-revenue)). Masad told TechCrunch it took nine years, and a controversial shift away from professional developers, to get there ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/11/replit-snags-9b-valuation-6-months-after-hitting-3b)).

The turn was painful. Forbes Australia reports that just before its first coding agent launched in 2024, Replit had laid off 30 people, more left after that, and only half the staff remained ([Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/replit-just-raised-400-million-to-beat-claude-at-vibe-coding/)).

> "I'm willing to throw away code, throw away work, and restart from scratch" — Amjad Masad ([founder-reported, Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/replit-just-raised-400-million-to-beat-claude-at-vibe-coding/))

## How much revenue does Replit make now?

The last public figure is $150M in annualized revenue in September 2025, which is about $12.5M a month on our arithmetic (calc). Masad had posted a $100M figure in June 2025 ([founder-reported via TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/replit-hits-3b-valuation-on-150m-annualized-revenue)). When Replit raised again in March 2026, it gave no updated ARR ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/11/replit-snags-9b-valuation-6-months-after-hitting-3b)). It only told Forbes it was on track for $1B in annual recurring revenue by the end of 2026 ([founder-reported, Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/replit-just-raised-400-million-to-beat-claude-at-vibe-coding/)). Current ARR and total revenue are not disclosed.

## What does Replit charge for its AI agent?

Replit sells monthly plans that bundle usage of its agent, with a free tier at the bottom. In November 2026 its pricing page listed Core at $20 a month ($18 billed yearly) and Pro at $100 a month ($90 billed yearly), plus custom Enterprise pricing ([founder-reported, Replit pricing](https://replit.com/pricing)). The page also shows higher monthly amounts for buying more usage on top of the base plans. Replit has not said how many people pay or how revenue splits between individuals and companies.

## How did investors price Replit's growth?

Investors tripled Replit's price in six months, before seeing a new revenue number. In 2023 it raised about $100M at a $1.16B valuation. In September 2025 Prysm Capital led a $250M round at $3B, and PitchBook estimated total funding at about $478M ([third-party estimate via TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/replit-hits-3b-valuation-on-150m-annualized-revenue)). In March 2026 Georgian Partners led a $400M Series D at $9B ([founder-reported via TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/11/replit-snags-9b-valuation-6-months-after-hitting-3b)). On the last public revenue figure, $9B is 60 times $150M (calc).

## How does Replit compare with Cursor, Lovable and Bolt.new?

Replit, [Lovable](/cases/lovable/) and [Bolt.new](/cases/bolt-new/) sell app building to non-coders, while [Cursor](/cases/cursor/) sells an editor to working developers. Cursor passed $100M in recurring revenue in January 2025 ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/series-b)). Lovable reached $100M ARR in July 2025 ([media-reported, TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/vibe-coding-platform-lovable-becomes-historys-fastest-growing-software-startup)). Bolt.new hit $4M ARR about four weeks after launch ([media-reported, Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/bolt-new-from-stackblitz-how-they-surfed-the-ai-wave-with-no-wipeouts)). Replit crossed the same $100M line in June 2025, a month before Lovable.

In our reading, the difference is the starting point. Lovable and Bolt.new were new products. Replit already had hosting, a browser editor and a large free user base when it added the agent, so the agent could take an app all the way to a live URL. That old infrastructure was a cost for eight years and became the advantage in the ninth. [Windsurf](/cases/windsurf/) shows the other path: a coding tool for developers that sold itself in pieces rather than raising again.

[n8n](/cases/n8n/) followed a similar path in automation, with years of free developer adoption before AI agents made its revenue jump.

Replit's agent has also let non-coders start companies, such as [GenAIPI](/cases/genaipi/), an AI training business, and [WhisperAI](/cases/whisperai/), a transcription app built by a former real estate investor.

## What can founders learn from Replit's pivot?

- **The customer can be the pivot.** Replit kept most of its product and changed who it was for, and revenue followed within a year.
- **Old infrastructure can pay off late.** Hosting and a browser editor let Replit's agent ship finished apps, which newer rivals had to build.
- **Pivots cost people.** Replit lost half its staff before the agent launched, by Forbes Australia's account.
- **Big targets replace disclosures.** Since September 2025 Replit has given a $1B goal instead of new revenue numbers, so outsiders cannot track progress.
