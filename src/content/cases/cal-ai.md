## Origin of the idea
Logging every meal by hand is the reason most people quit calorie counting. Vision models made it possible to estimate a meal from a single photo, removing most of the effort.

## How they started
Yadegari, who had already built and sold a small web business as a teenager, started Cal AI with a schoolmate while both were still in high school, and shipped an iPhone app first.

## Tools & tech stack
A native mobile app that sends meal photos to vision-capable AI models and combines the result with nutrition data, plus in-app subscriptions.

## Marketing
Heavy use of influencer and creator content on TikTok and Instagram, paid user acquisition, and tight paywall/onboarding experiments.

## Sales & pricing
Free download with in-app subscriptions, the usual model for consumer mobile apps; exact prices are not cited here.

## Monthly revenue
About **$4.2M/month**, implied from the $50M+ ARR the founder cited at acquisition (March 2026).

## Total revenue
Not disclosed.

## Founding date & timeline
- **2024:** App launched.
- **May 2025:** $35M ARR (founder interview).
- **Mar 2026:** Acquired by MyFitnessPal at $50M+ ARR (founder-reported).

## Team size
30+ people at acquisition (founder-reported).

## Key takeaways
- Remove the most painful step in an existing habit.
- Consumer AI apps win on distribution and paywall design.
- Incumbents buy fast-growing challengers.
