The Fireflies AI valuation passed $1B in June 2025 without the company raising a dollar of new money. Fireflies.ai reached the mark through its first tender offer, in which investors bought shares from early employees ([founder-reported, Fireflies.ai release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/fireflies-reaches-1-billion-valuation-150000434.html)). The AI note taker says it has been profitable since 2023 and has not raised primary capital since 2021. Inc42 puts its total funding at about $19M ([media-reported, Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/fireflies-ai-unicorn-india-strategy-acquisition-enterprises/)). That is one of the smallest amounts of capital behind any AI unicorn in this library.

## How did Krish Ramineni and Sam Udotong start Fireflies.ai?

Fireflies.ai began in 2016 in San Francisco, growing out of a 2015 side project, with Krish Ramineni as CEO and Sam Udotong as co-founder ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/fireflies-ai-unicorn-india-strategy-acquisition-enterprises/)). The product is a bot that joins video calls, records and transcribes them, writes summaries and lets teams search past meetings. TechCrunch reported in 2021 that it launched the meeting product in early 2020, just as remote work spread ([TechCrunch](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/fireflies-ai-raises-14m-meeting-161452951.html)). Inc42 says the early team was in India, and about 120 people now work remotely across more than 20 countries.

## How much money did Fireflies.ai raise, and when did it stop?

Fireflies raised about $19M in total and stopped after its 2021 Series A. Before that round it had about $5M, including a $4.9M seed in late 2019. In May 2021 Khosla Ventures led a $14M Series A ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/fireflies-ai-raises-14m-meeting-161452951.html)). Ramineni told TechCrunch at the time that revenue had grown 300% in the previous six or seven months ([founder-reported via TechCrunch](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/fireflies-ai-raises-14m-meeting-161452951.html)), and that more than 10,000 teams used the product.

## Does Fireflies.ai disclose its revenue?

No. Fireflies.ai has not published revenue, ARR or profit figures, so its revenue is not disclosed. What it does say is that it has been profitable since 2023 while growing at triple-digit rates year over year ([founder-reported, Fireflies.ai release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/fireflies-reaches-1-billion-valuation-150000434.html)). It also claims more than 20 million users at over 500,000 organizations, people at 75% of the Fortune 500 and more than 2 billion meeting minutes processed. Inc42 adds that India makes up about 10–15% of revenue. Several data sites publish revenue estimates, but we found none that cites the company, so we leave them out.

> "Reaching this significant valuation milestone while remaining profitable validates our approach" — Krish Ramineni ([founder-reported, Fireflies.ai release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/fireflies-reaches-1-billion-valuation-150000434.html))

## What does Fireflies.ai charge?

Fireflies uses a free plan to bring people in and charges for more transcription credits and team features. Its pricing page lists Free, Pro, Business and Enterprise plans, with Business adding team-pooled limits, analytics and video recording, and Enterprise aimed at groups of more than 50 ([founder-reported, Fireflies.ai pricing](https://fireflies.ai/pricing)). In 2021 TechCrunch described the model as a mix of SaaS and usage-based pricing, and Ramineni said the company would keep SaaS-like gross margins.

## How does Fireflies.ai compare with Otter.ai and Bluedot?

Fireflies and [Otter.ai](/cases/otter-ai/) are both meeting-notes companies founded in 2016, and both grew without large venture rounds. Otter.ai disclosed $100M+ ARR in March 2025 with fewer than 200 staff ([founder-reported, Otter.ai release](https://salestechstar.com/price-optimization-revenue-management/otter-ai-breaks-100m-arr-barrier-and-transforms-business-meetings-launching-industry-first-ai-meeting-agent-suite/)), after raising over $63M by 2021 ([media-reported, VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/otter-ai-raises-50-million-for-its-ai-transcription-service)). Fireflies raised less, about $19M, and shares no revenue. [Bluedot](/cases/bluedot/), a newer Chrome extension that records Google Meet without a bot, reported $1.5K MRR two months after launch ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/bluedot-0891acdc-ecac-4222-89da-e5d0ac06d882)).

In our reading, Otter and Fireflies made opposite disclosure choices with similar strategies. Otter announced a round revenue number. Fireflies announced a valuation and profitability instead. Both kept teams of a few hundred people or fewer and relied on free plans that spread inside companies, as one employee invites the note taker to a call and others see it.

For a meeting notes app that works without a bot joining the call, see [Granola](/cases/granola/).

## What can capital-light founders learn from Fireflies.ai?

- **Profit can replace new rounds.** Fireflies says it has been profitable since 2023, so it did not need to raise after its 2021 Series A.
- **A tender offer can set a valuation.** Investors buying employee shares gave Fireflies a $1B price without diluting the company.
- **The product markets itself.** A bot that joins calls is seen by every other participant, which helps a free plan spread.
- **Silence on revenue limits comparisons.** Without an ARR figure, outsiders can judge Fireflies only by users, staff and valuation.
