Most SaaS companies at $28,000 a month have hundreds or thousands of customers. Tribe Social has eight. In November 2026 the community app platform showed $28,271 in monthly recurring revenue from 8 active subscriptions, and $402,288 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tribe-social)). The economics look more like an agency than a typical self-serve tool.

## What does Tribe Social build for communities?

Tribe Social builds custom community apps that combine courses, live calls, conversations and payments. The company-written TrustMRR listing describes the audience as communities and says the platform powers communities that reach 500,000+ people every month ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tribe-social)).

The pitch is focus: one branded place for a community's content and events instead of a mix of chat apps, video calls and course hosts.

> "Bring your community, courses, live calls, and conversations into one focused place built around your people." — Tribe Social listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tribe-social))

## How does Tribe Social make $28K a month from 8 subscriptions?

Tribe Social had $28,271 in MRR across 8 active subscriptions in November 2026, with $402,288 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tribe-social)). TrustMRR checks these numbers through a Stripe API key.

| All-time revenue | $402,288 | [verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tribe-social) |
| Active subscriptions | 8 | [verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tribe-social) |

MRR divided by subscriptions is about $3,533.88 per subscription ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tribe-social)). That is an average, so one or two large clients may account for much of it. Pricing is not disclosed on the listing, and team size and profit are not disclosed either.

## Who pays for a Tribe Social community app?

The paying customer is the community owner, not the members. The listing marks the business as B2C and gives about 500,000 users, which most likely counts members inside client apps rather than paying accounts ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tribe-social)). With only 8 subscriptions, each client is a large account.

Founder Bruce van Zyl has 850 followers on X according to the listing, and no marketing channels are named. That points to sales built on relationships with a few community owners rather than broad self-serve sign-ups, although the listing does not say so directly.

## How does Tribe Social compare with Postiz and Simple Analytics?

Tribe Social reaches similar MRR to some self-serve tools on the site, but with a tiny fraction of the customers.

| Tribe Social | Custom community apps, 8 subscriptions | $28,271 | [verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tribe-social) |

Our observation: Simple Analytics and Postiz spread revenue across many small accounts, so losing one customer barely registers. At Tribe Social, losing one of eight could cut MRR sharply. The trade is higher revenue per account for higher concentration risk.

## What takeaways does Tribe Social offer B2B platform founders?

- **High-ticket SaaS needs few customers.** Eight subscriptions produce $28,271 in MRR at Tribe Social.
- **Concentration is the main risk.** Each client carries thousands of dollars a month on average, so churn is lumpy.
- **Member reach is not paying users.** A 500,000-person reach sits on top of only eight paying accounts.
- **Undisclosed pricing fits custom deals.** With pricing not disclosed, Tribe Social appears to price per client rather than through a public plan table.
