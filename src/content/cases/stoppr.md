STOPPR revenue started with a copy. David Attias spent eight years as a quant trader, could not code, and noticed a habit-quitting app growing fast. So he cloned it with AI. He told Starter Story he made $5,000 in the first month and about $12K a month after five months ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-vibe-coded-a-12k-month-mobile-app-stoppr-breakdown)). STOPPR is a clear example of the "clone and change one thing" playbook that AI coding tools have made cheap.

## How did David Attias build STOPPR without coding?

David Attias built STOPPR by copying screens, not writing code. He took screenshots of the app he admired, refined the designs in Figma and had Cursor, an AI code editor, build the features, finishing the first version in about a month ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-vibe-coded-a-12k-month-mobile-app-stoppr-breakdown)). The app he studied was Quittr, a quit-porn app he said was making $200,000 a month three months after launch. Team size is not disclosed.

> "I clone a very successful app and first month of revenue I made $5,000." — David Attias ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-vibe-coded-a-12k-month-mobile-app-stoppr-breakdown))

## How much revenue does STOPPR earn?

STOPPR earns about $12K a month, five months after launch, according to Attias ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-vibe-coded-a-12k-month-mobile-app-stoppr-breakdown)). It made $5,000 in its first month. A summary of the interview put his profit margin at about 35% after five months, which reflects heavy spending on promotion. Total revenue and prices are not disclosed.

## What does STOPPR actually help people quit?

Sources describe STOPPR differently. In the Starter Story interview, Attias described his twist as aiming the cloned app at Gen Z women who want to quit sugar cravings ([Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-vibe-coded-a-12k-month-mobile-app-stoppr-breakdown)). The current App Store listing, titled "STOPPR: Quit Porn & Stay Sober," describes help quitting porn, smoking, vaping and drinking ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6757722256)). The app's focus appears to have widened since the interview.

## How does STOPPR compare with Cal AI and Umax?

STOPPR, [Cal AI](/cases/cal-ai/) and [Umax](/cases/umax/) are all consumer self-improvement apps aimed at young users and sold on mobile subscriptions. Cal AI and Umax were built by young founders who could code, and both report far larger revenue. STOPPR is far smaller at about $12K a month, but its founder could not code at all. The comparison shows how the barrier to entry has dropped: copying a proven app format no longer needs a developer, which also means more competitors can copy STOPPR.

Our reading: cloning a winning app is a fast way to first revenue, because the demand is already proven. It is a weak long-term moat. STOPPR's change of audience was its only real difference from the original, so its growth will depend on marketing, not on the product being hard to copy.

## What lessons does STOPPR hold for app cloners?

- **Proven demand lowers risk.** Copying an app that already sells removes the guesswork about whether anyone wants it.
- **Change one thing.** STOPPR's twist was a different audience, not a new product.
- **AI tools make copying cheap for everyone.** If Attias could clone an app in a month, others can clone STOPPR.
- **Margins matter more than revenue.** About 35% margin at $12K a month shows how much consumer apps spend to grow.
