Most creator courses spike at launch and fade. DataExpert has done the opposite. Zach Wilson's data engineering school, started in March 2023, had collected $4,829,130 in lifetime revenue by November 2026, and it still brought in $100,640 over the last 30 days ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dataexpert-techcreator)). It ranks number 31 on TrustMRR, near the top of the whole database.

## What is DataExpert.io and who is it for?

DataExpert.io is a paid education company for working data engineers who want to move into AI and senior roles. The listing describes structured, hands-on programmes and free cloud access to Databricks, AWS and Snowflake, so students practise on the same tools employers use ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dataexpert-techcreator)).

> "Master data engineering and AI like a big tech engineer" — DataExpert value proposition on TrustMRR ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dataexpert-techcreator))

The listing appears as "DataExpert / TechCreator" because the company also wants to help data engineers become content creators themselves. The audience is B2C: individual engineers paying out of pocket.

## How much revenue does DataExpert bring in from 827 subscribers?

DataExpert made $52,708 in MRR in November 2026, from 827 active subscriptions, with $100,640 collected in the last 30 days and $4,829,130 all time ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dataexpert-techcreator)).

Last-30-day revenue was about 1.9 times MRR ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dataexpert-techcreator)). That gap means roughly half of recent cash came from non-recurring sales, such as one-off course or bootcamp payments. All-time revenue equals about 92 months of current MRR, far more than the 44 months the company has existed, which points the same way.

## Why does Zach Wilson's audience matter to DataExpert?

Zach Wilson's audience is the main distribution asset: TrustMRR shows 51.3K followers on X for the founder ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dataexpert-techcreator)). The listing does not name marketing channels, but a technical course sold by a known engineer usually depends on that personal reach.

The average subscription is worth about $63.73 a month (calc: $52,708 / 827, [from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dataexpert-techcreator)), below the listed $119 price. Discounts, annual plans or older prices could explain that, but the split is not disclosed. Team size is also not disclosed.

DataExpert sells advanced, career-focused training to working professionals, while the other two education cases on this site sell to earlier-stage learners.

Our observation: Codédex has higher MRR, but DataExpert's lifetime total is the striking number. A narrow, high-salary niche lets a founder charge more per learner and sell big one-off programmes on top of a subscription.

## What can course founders learn from DataExpert?

- **A senior niche supports premium prices.** DataExpert lists $119 a month, aimed at engineers who expect a career payoff.
- **One-off sales can outweigh recurring revenue.** Last-30-day revenue of $100,640 was almost double the $52,708 MRR.
- **Hands-on infrastructure is part of the product.** Free cloud access to Databricks, AWS and Snowflake makes the course harder to copy with free videos.
- **A founder audience is a moat.** 51.3K followers on X give the company a channel it does not have to rent.
