REKALAB's numbers do not line up the way most do. The Malaysian video tool had $9,276 in monthly recurring revenue in November 2026, yet only $2,923 actually arrived in the last 30 days ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rekalab)). A gap that size usually means subscriptions were added recently and have not all billed yet, or that some are discounted or paused. Either way, it makes REKALAB a case about reading verified data carefully.

## What does REKALAB promise video creators?

REKALAB promises a finished video from one line of text. Its TrustMRR description says a user can generate a 60-second video with one prompt, and the company pitches it as the simplest video tool there is.

> "Type what you want. Get a finished video. That's the entire workflow." — REKALAB company description ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rekalab))

The problem it names is that making videos feels slow, complex and frustrating. The audience is content creators, both businesses and individuals.

## What revenue has REKALAB collected so far?

REKALAB had $9,276 in MRR from 214 subscriptions, $2,923 in the last 30 days and $23,865 all time in November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rekalab)). The team is 2–5 people and bootstrapped ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rekalab)).

## Why is REKALAB MRR three times its 30-day revenue?

TrustMRR does not explain the gap, so the cause is not disclosed. MRR counts the monthly value of active subscriptions; 30-day revenue counts cash collected. When MRR is about 3.2 times cash (calc: $9,276 / $2,923, [from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rekalab)), common reasons include new subscribers on free trials, recent sign-ups not yet billed, or failed payments still counted as active.

The useful reading: REKALAB's MRR shows demand, and next month's cash will show how much of it converts.

## How is REKALAB different from AutoClip and Vid.AI?

REKALAB, [AutoClip](/cases/autoclip/) and [Vid.AI](/cases/vid-ai/) all make short videos with AI, but they start from different inputs.

| Company | Input | MRR (Nov 2026) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| REKALAB | One text prompt | $9,276 | [verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rekalab) |
| [AutoClip](/cases/autoclip/) | A long video to cut into shorts | $11,773 | [verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/autoclip) |
| [Vid.AI](/cases/vid-ai/) | Faceless video generation | $51,121 | [verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/vidai-llc) |

Our observation: AutoClip needs footage the creator already has, so its users are established. REKALAB and Vid.AI generate from nothing, which widens the market. Vid.AI shows how far that market can go; REKALAB's lower price list suggests it is chasing volume.

## What takeaways does REKALAB leave for video tool builders?

- **One-step workflows sell.** The entire pitch is one prompt in, one video out.
- **Check MRR against cash.** A 3.2x gap between MRR and 30-day revenue needs explaining.
- **Two plans are enough.** Creator and Business keep the choice simple.
- **Small teams can ship video AI.** A bootstrapped team of 2–5 runs it on Next.js and Rails.
