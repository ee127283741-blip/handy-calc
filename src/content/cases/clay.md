Clay revenue grew more than 3.5x in 2025, and the company barely used the money it raised to get there. Clay reached $100M in ARR in December 2025 and was cash-flow positive for parts of the year ([founder-reported, Clay](https://clay.com/blog/tender-offer-2026)). Weeks later it let employees sell shares at a $5B valuation. CEO Kareem Amin told Crunchbase News the company had not touched the cash from its last funding round ([founder-reported, Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/liquidity/sales-automation-unicorn-clay-tender-offers-qa-amin/)). For a sales tool, a market full of cheaper rivals, that is an unusual position.

## What does Clay do for sales teams?

Clay pulls data about companies and people from many sources into one spreadsheet-like workspace, then uses AI to research and contact prospects. Crunchbase News says it connects to more than 150 data sources and counts OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor, Canva, Intercom and Rippling as customers ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/ai-powered-gtm-startup-clay-valuation-doubles-capitalg/)). Clay has also promoted a new job title, the "GTM engineer", for the people who build these workflows. It sells a free plan, paid Launch and Growth plans and custom Enterprise contracts ([founder-reported, Clay pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)).

BetaKit reports that Clay was founded in 2017 by Kareem Amin and Nicolae Rusan, who has since left, with Varun Anand as a co-founder and head of operations ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/clay-lands-115-million-usd-series-d-at-7-billion-valuation/)). Amin told Crunchbase News the team grew from about 80–90 people to about 300 in a year ([founder-reported, Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/liquidity/sales-automation-unicorn-clay-tender-offers-qa-amin/)).

## How did Clay's revenue reach $100M ARR?

Clay's revenue more than tripled in 2025 after years of slower growth. In August 2025 the company said revenue was on track to more than triple that year ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/ai-powered-gtm-startup-clay-valuation-doubles-capitalg/)). In January 2026 it reported the result: revenue up more than 3.5x and $100M in ARR in December 2025 ([founder-reported, Clay](https://clay.com/blog/tender-offer-2026)). That is about $8.3M a month (calc). Clay also said enterprise net revenue retention was above 200%, so existing large customers more than doubled their spending in a year. Clay has not published a revenue figure for 2026, and total revenue is not disclosed.

## Why does Clay keep doing tender offers?

Clay uses tender offers to pay employees without going public, and it says it does not need the cash. The January 2026 tender let staff sell up to $55M of shares at $5B, led by DST Global ([founder-reported, Clay](https://clay.com/blog/tender-offer-2026)). Amin told Crunchbase News that Clay had raised $206M in equity since 2017 and had not spent the money from its last round ([founder-reported, Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/liquidity/sales-automation-unicorn-clay-tender-offers-qa-amin/)).

> "We don't actually need more primary capital. We haven't touched the primary capital." — Kareem Amin ([founder-reported, Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/liquidity/sales-automation-unicorn-clay-tender-offers-qa-amin/))

In our reading, this is a hiring tool. Clay more than tripled its headcount in a year, and frequent tenders let it promise new hires that their equity can turn into cash before an IPO.

## What did Clay's $7.1B Series D buy?

In September 2026 Wellington led a $115M Series D at $7.1B, with a16z, CapitalG, Meritech and Sequoia also investing ([media-reported, BetaKit](https://betakit.com/clay-lands-115-million-usd-series-d-at-7-billion-valuation/)). Clay had more than 17,000 customers by then, up from 10,000 a year earlier. Amin described the next step as agents that grow a company on its own. On the last public ARR of $100M, the new valuation is 71 times revenue (calc), a premium that assumes 2026 growth has continued.

## How does Clay compare with Instantly and Gojiberry AI?

All three help companies find and contact prospects, but Clay sells the data layer while [Instantly](/cases/instantly/) and [Gojiberry AI](/cases/gojiberry-ai/) sell outreach. Instantly, a bootstrapped cold email tool, was at $2.4M ARR in October 2022 ([founder-reported via Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instantly-ai/interviews/instantly-ai-raul-kaevand-2022)). Gojiberry AI, which finds LinkedIn prospects showing buying signals, reported $27K MRR in December 2025 ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/validating-via-services-before-building-a-27k-mrr-product-7FrT6FXChPOiUK4PbXFp)). In the same month Clay was at $100M ARR, roughly 300 times Gojiberry's monthly rate on our arithmetic (calc).

In our view, Clay won by sitting upstream. Email and LinkedIn tools compete on price and deliverability, and buyers switch easily. Clay holds the research, the enrichment and the workflows that feed those tools, which is harder to rip out. That is also why its customer list reads like the AI industry itself.

[Lindy](/cases/lindy/) also automates prospecting and lead qualification, but through no-code AI agents, and reported high seven figures of revenue in July 2025 ([founder-reported, The SaaS Podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/lindy-flo-crivello-450/)).

## What are the lessons from Clay's growth?

- **Own the workflow, not the channel.** Clay sits before the email or LinkedIn tool, where switching costs are higher.
- **Name the job you create.** Promoting the "GTM engineer" gave Clay's power users a job title and a reason to stay loyal to the tool.
- **Expansion beats new logos.** Enterprise net revenue retention above 200% means existing customers did much of the growth.
- **Use tenders to keep staff.** Two tenders in nine months let a fast-hiring team turn equity into cash without an IPO.
