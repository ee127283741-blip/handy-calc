Sveta Bay and her co-founder had a ceiling. Their first indie business sold digital marketing products and stalled at $5,000 a month. Then they added a little AI: a paid pack of 40 ChatGPT prompts for marketing, and sales poured in. That told them founders want tools, not lessons. Their next tool, FounderPal's Marketing Strategy Generator, earned $60,000 in six months ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/founderpal)).

## What do solopreneurs actually want from AI marketing?

Strategy. Bay noticed that many solopreneurs badly want to outsource marketing, but most AI products only generate cheesy email subject lines and spammy SEO posts. She also saw that people prefer clicking buttons to typing into ChatGPT, and that she and her co-founder knew how to write strong prompts.

> "Solopreneurs need a strategic CMO, not a content marketing intern." — Sveta Bay ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/founderpal))

Their first MVP for this audience failed. They kept the same audience and built a second one, the Marketing Strategy Generator.

## How does a $150 no-code MVP earn $60,000?

They chose speed over learning to code: Bubble at $32 a month instead of three months of programming. The first version took three weeks and cost $150 in its first month, including OpenAI ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/founderpal)). The flow was enough to prove the idea: describe your business in a paragraph, pick from 10 audience segments and 10 value propositions, wait five minutes, and get a persona, positioning, 10 distribution ideas and 10 conversion ideas.

Each strategy cost them about $1 because of GPT-4 pricing and very long prompts. They accepted lower margins for quality, reasoning that loyal fans beat angry customers asking for refunds. Pricing is one-time: buy one strategy or unlimited strategies, because nobody needs a new marketing strategy every week ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/founderpal)).

## How did FounderPal launch and keep growing?

The launch had three steps: a Product Hunt launch on a Monday, when competition is lighter; a plain, single-call-to-action email to a 13,000-person list; and a founder video on Twitter. Launch day brought $1,249, and the next two months added $18,709 ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/founderpal)).

Growth since then comes from what Bay calls side-project marketing:

| Step | How FounderPal does it |
|---|---|
| Find a keyword | An overlooked search term related to the product, such as "user persona generator" |
| Build a free tool | A two-screen AI wrapper in Bubble: input and result |
| Give value first | The first result is free, with no email required |
| Ask for an email | A second, deeper result in exchange for an email |
| Show the paid product | Offered naturally after the free flow |
| Nurture | Automatic email sequences with more value and product stories |

She calls it "boring marketing": SEO and email, done consistently, beat flashier tactics. The list later grew to 25,000.

## How much money does FounderPal AI bring in?

The Marketing Strategy Generator brings in about $10,000 a month through one-time payments and earned $60,000 in its first six months ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/founderpal)). FounderPal has two founders and no employees. Because the revenue is one-time rather than recurring, the monthly figure depends on steady new buyers from search and email.

## How does FounderPal compare with aiCarousels and Leadmore AI?

[aiCarousels](/cases/aicarousels/) sells to the same solopreneur audience and uses the same engine: free tools that rank in search and lead to a paid product, plus build-in-public posts. FounderPal goes one level higher, selling the strategy, while aiCarousels sells one piece of content.

[Leadmore AI](/cases/leadmore-ai/) also sells marketing to small businesses but charges per action with refundable credits. FounderPal's one-time pricing fits a product people use occasionally; Leadmore AI's credits fit a product people use continuously. Matching the pricing model to how often customers need the result is the common lesson.

## What should founders learn from FounderPal?

- **Founders prefer tools to education.** The jump from courses to an AI tool broke the $5,000 ceiling ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/founderpal)).
- **Keep the audience, change the product.** The first MVP failed; the second, for the same people, worked.
- **Price for how often people need it.** One-time payments suit a strategy you buy once.
- **Free tools are distribution.** Small keyword-driven tools feed emails and sales for the main product.
