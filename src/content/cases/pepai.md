PepAI revenue reached about $11K MRR seven weeks after launch, built by a business student, not a computer science student. Cedric Roberge, 22, studies business at the University of Oregon ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/cedric)). He built PepAI, an app for tracking peptide and GLP-1 doses, in about two weeks with Replit and Claude. He told Starter Story it had about 2,000 active subscriptions and $51K in total revenue seven weeks in ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/i-built-a-30k-month-in-35-days)).

## How did Cedric Roberge build PepAI?

Cedric Roberge built PepAI with Replit as his development environment, Claude as his coding partner and Firebase for the backend ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/i-built-a-30k-month-in-35-days)). The idea came from peptides and GLP-1 drugs trending on social media and a gap in the App Store for a tracker. PepAI logs doses, scans meals, tracks side effects and answers questions with an AI assistant. Team size and founding date are not disclosed.

> "My Mobile App Made $4,000 in 2 Weeks" — Cedric Roberge ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/pep-ai-stay-on-track-with-your-peptides))

## What MRR do PepAI subscriptions bring in?

PepAI makes about $11K in MRR from about 2,000 active subscriptions, and made about $33,000 in its latest 28 days, Roberge said ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/i-built-a-30k-month-in-35-days)). Total revenue was about $51K seven weeks after launch. Plans cost $10 a month or $45 a year, with a three-day free trial. The 28-day figure is higher than MRR, likely because annual plans are paid upfront.

## Why did Apple keep rejecting PepAI?

Apple rejected PepAI several times because its reviewers saw the app's features as too close to giving medical advice ([founder-reported, Stork](https://www.stork.ai/blog/he-built-a-50k-app-with-ai)). Health-adjacent apps that discuss doses face strict App Store rules, and Roberge had to adjust how PepAI presented information before it was approved. He also said Apple's expedited review helped speed later approvals ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/i-built-a-30k-month-in-35-days)). For a non-technical founder, the App Store review was a bigger obstacle than writing the code.

## How does PepAI compare with Cal AI and Wrestle AI?

PepAI, [Cal AI](/cases/cal-ai/) and [Wrestle AI](/cases/wrestle-ai/) are all mobile health apps built by young founders. Cal AI tracks calories from photos and grew far larger; its founders could code. Wrestle AI's 18-year-old founder used Rork and reached about $17K a month ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/how-i-grew-my-mobile-app-to-17k-per-month)). PepAI is closest to Wrestle AI in size and story: a student, an AI builder, a niche and a fast start. Its niche is regulated in a way wrestling is not, which explains the App Store fights.

Our reading: PepAI rode a trend at the right moment, with GLP-1 and peptide use exploding and no dominant tracker. Trends bring fast users but can fade, and health topics bring platform risk. Building in two weeks let Roberge test the idea before either risk mattered.

## What can student founders learn from PepAI?

- **Follow what your feed shows you.** Peptides trending on social media pointed Roberge to an empty App Store niche.
- **AI can be the senior developer.** A business student shipped a working app with Replit and Claude.
- **Health apps face extra review.** Anything near medical advice should expect App Store rejections.
- **Annual plans boost early cash.** Upfront yearly payments pushed 28-day revenue well above MRR.
