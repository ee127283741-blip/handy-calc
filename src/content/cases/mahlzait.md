Typing every ingredient into a food diary is why most people quit calorie counting. Mahlzait, a German app, lets users snap a photo instead. Launched in January 2026 by a founder named Ruslan, it had 803 active subscriptions and $2,629 MRR by November 2026, with $29,449 in lifetime revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/mahlzait)). The app is a quiet example of a crowded category still having room.

## How does Mahlzait log a meal?

Mahlzait logs meals four ways: photo, barcode, voice or text, then tracks calories, macros and nutrition facts in one place ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/mahlzait)). AI photo recognition does the heavy lifting, aimed at people who want a faster way to count calories without typing every ingredient.

> "Calorie Tracker with AI" — Mahlzait description ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/mahlzait))

The app is built with Flutter, and RevenueCat handles subscriptions. Because revenue is verified through RevenueCat rather than reported by hand, the subscription and revenue figures below come straight from the billing system.

## What takeaways does Mahlzait offer health app makers?

- **Remove the typing.** Photo, barcode and voice input attack the main reason users quit.
- **Local markets still have room.** A German app reached 803 subscriptions in ten months.
- **Low prices mean volume matters.** A low average revenue per subscription needs many users, so reach is the main growth lever for an app at this price point.
- **Ship constantly.** 1,410 contributions in a year show sustained work behind the numbers.
