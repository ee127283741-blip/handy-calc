Creator Hunter revenue came from a founder who prompted his way through every line of code. Paulius Masalskas, who had worked door to door to pay for his studies, built an influencer database for startups without knowing how to program. He told Starter Story it had made about $30K with more than 350 paying customers ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-built-an-app-with-cursor-made-30k-and-quit-my-job)). Much of it was built on his train commute, and the revenue let him quit his job.

## How did Paulius Masalskas build Creator Hunter without coding?

Paulius Masalskas built Creator Hunter by chaining AI tools. He used Perplexity to plan each step, Bolt.new to generate the first version, Cursor to make it production-ready with logins and a backend, and Framer for the marketing site ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-built-an-app-with-cursor-made-30k-and-quit-my-job)). Slovak outlet Startitup.sk reported that he began selling door to door at 18 and left his job at the end of 2024 to work on Creator Hunter full-time ([Startitup.sk](https://www.startitup.sk/?p=1074340)). Team size and prices are not disclosed.

> "Nevedel som napísať riadok kódu, no zarobil som 27 600 eur." — Paulius Masalskas ([founder-reported, Startitup.sk](https://www.startitup.sk/?p=1074340))

In English: "I couldn't write a line of code, but I earned €27,600."

## What revenue has Creator Hunter reported?

Creator Hunter's reported revenue grew from $17K in its first 90 days ([founder-reported, No-Code Exits](https://nocodeexits.substack.com/p/how-paulius-built-a-directory-of)) to about $30K with 350+ paying customers ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-built-an-app-with-cursor-made-30k-and-quit-my-job)). In Slovakia he put the figure at €27,600 ([founder-reported, Startitup.sk](https://www.startitup.sk/?p=1074340)). These are cumulative totals at different dates, not monthly revenue; MRR is not disclosed.

## Why does Creator Hunter sell a creator database?

Startups that market on TikTok need creators who fit their niche, and finding them by scrolling is slow. Creator Hunter sells a curated list so a founder can find and contact relevant creators quickly ([No-Code Exits](https://nocodeexits.substack.com/p/how-paulius-built-a-directory-of)). A database is a good first product for a non-coder: the value is in the data and curation, and the software around it is simple enough for AI tools to build.

## How does Creator Hunter compare with Payout and TrendFeed?

Creator Hunter, [Payout](/cases/payout/) and [TrendFeed](/cases/trendfeed/) all sell into the creator economy, built by founders who rely on AI to write code. Payout uses creators to market a consumer app; TrendFeed sells to creators; Creator Hunter sells access to creators, to startups. Of the three, Creator Hunter has the most B2B model: 350+ paying customers rather than thousands of app subscribers.

Our reading: Masalskas's sales background mattered as much as the tools. Selling door to door teaches the same thing a directory business needs, which is finding customers one at a time. The AI stack let him skip hiring a developer, but he still had to sell.

## What are the takeaways from Creator Hunter for non-coders?

- **Chain tools by job.** Perplexity planned, Bolt drafted, Cursor finished, Framer marketed.
- **Data products suit non-coders.** The value is curation; the software can stay simple.
- **Commute time is build time.** Much of Creator Hunter was built on a train.
- **Sales skills transfer.** A door-to-door seller knew how to win the first 350 customers.
