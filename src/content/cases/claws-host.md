Every popular open-source tool creates a second market: people who want it running but do not want to manage a server. Claws.host sells into that market for OpenClaw. Founded in the United States in January 2026, it had $8,223 in lifetime revenue and $1,672 in MRR by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claws-host)). It is a small, clean example of the managed-hosting play.

## What does Claws.host actually host?

Claws.host runs managed instances of OpenClaw. Customers skip the server setup: they subscribe, provision and configure an instance in the browser ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claws-host)).

> "Run OpenClaw in 5 minutes, not 5 hours." — Claws.host value proposition ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claws-host))

The audience is listed as B2B. The only tool named in its stack is Stripe for billing, and the founder, listed as Eric, has a small following on X.

## What does Claws.host earn per customer?

Claws.host had $1,672 MRR from 38 active subscriptions in November 2026, with $1,540 collected in the last 30 days and $8,223 all time ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claws-host)). That works out to about $44 per subscription per month (calc: $1,672 / 38, [from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claws-host)), between the two lower plans.

## Why do people pay to skip an OpenClaw server setup?

The product sells time, not software. OpenClaw itself is the thing customers want; Claws.host charges for the hours it would take to provision and maintain a server. The page frames the gap as minutes versus hours, which is an easy trade for a business user.

Managed hosting also earns recurring revenue by default. An instance keeps running each month, so customers stay subscribed as long as they use the tool.

## What should builders learn from Claws.host?

- **Host what people already want.** A popular open-source tool brings its own demand.
- **Sell the saved hours.** "5 minutes, not 5 hours" is the whole pitch.
- **Tiered plans fit hosting.** Three price points map to different instance needs.
- **Platform risk is real.** Revenue depends on one upstream project staying popular.
