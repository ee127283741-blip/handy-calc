A grey sky can make a good house look unsellable. Immopix, a German startup founded in January 2026, sells real estate agents a one-minute fix: AI that swaps the sky, corrects the light and removes bins and cars from listing photos. By November 2026 it had taken $17,088 in total, $3,311 of it in the last 30 days ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/immopix)).

## What does Immopix change in a listing photo?

Immopix changes the parts of a photo that hurt a listing and leaves the property itself alone. The TrustMRR listing says it corrects weather and lighting and removes distracting elements, while keeping fixed elements unchanged and ensuring legal compliance.

> "Immopix makes your photo ready for listing in one minute" — Immopix company description ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/immopix))

The German-language website lists more services: sky replacement, virtual furnishing, object removal, interior enhancement, redrawn and 3D floor plans, and property videos with title, logo and music. It also offers an API and an MCP connector ([founder-reported, Immopix](https://immopix.ai)). Oddly, the headline description on TrustMRR describes a different product, imghero, a blog image generator; the rest of the listing is about Immopix.

## Why do credits fit real estate agents?

Agents work in bursts: a new listing means a batch of photos, then nothing for a while. Credit packs match that rhythm better than a monthly fee an agent might cancel between listings. Larger packs lower the price per credit, from about €1 at 10 credits to about €0.60 at 500 (calc, [from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/immopix)), which rewards agencies that buy in bulk.

The cost is visibility: on a site that ranks by MRR, Immopix looks far smaller than its cash flow.

## What lessons can proptech founders take from Immopix?

- **Credits match bursty demand.** Agents buy when they list, and Immopix prices for that.
- **MRR undercounts pay-per-use tools.** $292 MRR hides $3,311 in monthly sales.
- **Local language is a moat.** A German site and compliance promise target a market English tools skip.
- **Keep listings tidy.** A headline describing another product can confuse buyers and readers.
