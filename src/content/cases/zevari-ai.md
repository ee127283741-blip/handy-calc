Outbound sales tools usually promise full automation. zevari.ai promises the opposite safeguard: an AI team does the work, but a human approves every message. The Canadian company, founded in April 2026 by John Peslar, had $28,125 in all-time revenue and $5,042 in MRR by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/zevari-ai)). That is roughly seven months of selling.

## How does the zevari.ai outbound team work?

zevari.ai splits outbound into six AI "specialists" that take a prospect from first touch to a qualified reply over LinkedIn and email. The user stays in the loop as the final approver ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/zevari-ai)).

> "Your AI SDR team for LinkedIn and email outbound." — zevari.ai company description ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/zevari-ai))

The listing positions it as "a full outbound department" rather than a single tool. Pricing and team size are not disclosed. The company runs payments through Stripe, and founder John Peslar is listed with 643 followers on X. The listing does not name any marketing channels.

## Why does zevari.ai make users approve every send?

Approval is the product's trust feature. Automated LinkedIn and email outreach can damage a sender's reputation when it goes wrong, so keeping a human gate lets buyers adopt AI prospecting without handing over their brand. The design trades some speed for control, which suits founders and small sales teams.

## What lessons does zevari.ai offer outbound founders?

- **Sell a department, not a feature.** Framing the product as a team makes the value easier to grasp.
- **Human approval lowers adoption risk.** Buyers keep control of every message.
- **Many small subscriptions add up.** 68 subscribers produce the MRR, so no single customer dominates.
- **Crowded channels need a clear angle.** zevari.ai competes on workflow design, not on reaching LinkedIn first.
