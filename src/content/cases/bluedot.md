Ruslan Halilov and his co-founder Dima spent six months building a product, then could not sell it to anyone. People on their research calls had been friendly, and the founders mistook friendliness for demand. Their second attempt was smaller: a Chrome extension that writes Google Meet notes without a bot joining the call. Two months after launch, Bluedot had 500 daily active users and $1.5K MRR growing 50% a month ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/bluedot-0891acdc-ecac-4222-89da-e5d0ac06d882)).

## What pain did a remote staffing agency reveal?

Halilov came from recruitment and sales; Dima was an experienced product manager. Together they ran a staffing agency with a team of 15 spread around the world, making about 30K a month at 20% margins. It was unstable: some months brought lots of hires, others none.

Agencies run on calls, and with a remote team they struggled to document decisions and processes. When people left, their knowledge went with them. Their first idea was a central hub for all company meeting recordings.

## Why did Bluedot's first product fail to sell?

They built the hub while holding five to ten research calls a week. The first version was a Figma page shown to engineering leaders, with the goal of inviting 50 of them to calls and getting them onto a waiting list. The build used React and Node.js, with freelancers from Upwork, and cost about $10K in total ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/bluedot-0891acdc-ecac-4222-89da-e5d0ac06d882)).

After almost six months, nobody would roll it out in their company.

> "Looking back now I can see that I was very bad at research and misrepresented people being nice with real interest in the product." — Ruslan Halilov ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/bluedot-0891acdc-ecac-4222-89da-e5d0ac06d882))

## How did a product-led pivot rescue Bluedot?

They decided users needed to try the product themselves, without a sales process. So they built a Chrome extension that records meetings in a few clicks, which took about three months. Beta users adopted it much faster. Bluedot then became an AI note taker for Google Meet that captures the call through the extension rather than a bot, and generates notes adapted to what the user needs.

The team kept design standards high, since Dima studied design at Central Saint Martins in London, and recorded personalised videos inviting people from the waiting list. The official Product Hunt launch came three weeks before the interview.

## What MRR did Bluedot AI reach after launch?

Two months after launch, Bluedot reported 500 daily active users and $1.5K MRR, with revenue growing 50% a month ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/bluedot-0891acdc-ecac-4222-89da-e5d0ac06d882)). The team was three founders and two employees. Total revenue and pricing are not disclosed; the Starter Story headline cites $2K MRR, while the founder's text gives $1.5K.

## How does Bluedot compare with Recall and Talknotes?

[Recall](/cases/recall/) is another small AI browser extension that raised a modest round and nearly died on a first version people did not keep using. Both found their footing by making the product easier to try and by learning that polite feedback is not proof of demand.

[Talknotes](/cases/talknotes/) also turns spoken words into notes, but from voice memos rather than meetings, and grew on paid ads as a one-person business. Bluedot's angle is privacy and convenience in calls: no bot in the meeting. In a crowded AI note-taker market, that one difference is its whole pitch.

For an AI scribe built for one profession, clinicians, see [Freed](/cases/freed/).

For a venture-backed take on real-time meeting help, and a retracted revenue claim, see [Cluely](/cases/cluely/).

For the two longest-running AI meeting note takers, see [Otter.ai](/cases/otter-ai/) and [Fireflies.ai](/cases/fireflies-ai/).

## What lessons does Bluedot teach about research?

- **Friendly calls are not demand.** Six months of building followed research that mistook politeness for interest.
- **Let users try before they buy.** A self-serve extension spread where a sales-led hub did not.
- **One sharp difference can carry a product.** "No bot joins your call" set Bluedot apart.
- **Grants count as funding.** A Google grant for Ukrainian startups extended the runway without equity.
