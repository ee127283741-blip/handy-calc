The Sana acquisition is one of Europe's largest AI exits so far. Workday agreed in September 2025 to buy Sana, a Stockholm AI company, for about $1.1B for all its shares ([founder-reported, Workday via Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/09/16/workday-to-acquire-sana-for-11-billion/)). Joel Hellermark had founded Sana in 2016, years before ChatGPT, as an AI learning company. By the time Workday came calling, Sana also sold AI agents for everyday knowledge work, and coverage of the deal reported over 1 million business users.

## What did Joel Hellermark build at Sana?

Joel Hellermark founded Sana in Stockholm in 2016 to use AI for workplace learning ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/09/16/workday-to-acquire-sana-for-11-billion/)). Its first product, a learning platform, helps companies create and personalize training for employees. Later Sana added AI agents that search a company's documents and tools, answer questions and handle routine tasks. Over 1 million business users used its products by September 2025 ([media-reported, IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/workday-acquires-sana-for-usd-1-1-billion-to-boost-ai-at-work)). Team size and prices are not disclosed.

> "Our focus has always been on creating intuitive AI tools that improve how people learn and work," — Joel Hellermark ([founder-reported, IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/workday-acquires-sana-for-usd-1-1-billion-to-boost-ai-at-work))

## Why did Workday buy Sana for $1.1B?

Workday bought Sana to put an AI layer in front of its HR and finance software. Workday described the plan as a new "front door for work": employees would ask Sana's agents for what they need instead of navigating Workday screens ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/09/16/workday-to-acquire-sana-for-11-billion/)). The deal valued Sana at about $1.1B and closed on November 4, 2025 ([founder-reported, Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2025-11-04-Workday-Completes-Acquisition-of-Sana)). Hellermark stayed on to lead the team inside Workday.

## Did Sana disclose its revenue?

No. Neither Sana nor Workday disclosed Sana's revenue or ARR, so both are not disclosed here. The public numbers are the price, about $1.1B, and the user count, over 1 million business users ([media-reported, IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/workday-acquires-sana-for-usd-1-1-billion-to-boost-ai-at-work)). Workday is a public company, so Sana's results now sit inside Workday's reports rather than being broken out.

## How does Sana compare with Glean and Harvey?

Sana sold the same promise as [Glean](/cases/glean/), an AI assistant that searches everything inside a company, but it sold itself to a software giant instead of staying independent. Glean raised large rounds and remained a standalone company in this library. [Harvey](/cases/harvey/) shows the vertical version of workplace AI: one profession, law, served deeply. Sana combined a horizontal agent with a learning product, which fit Workday's HR focus.

Our reading: Sana's exit shows how incumbents buy AI instead of building it. Workday already had millions of employees logging in for pay slips and time off. Sana gave it an AI interface those users could talk to, and Sana got Workday's distribution in exchange for its independence.

## What are the lessons from the Sana acquisition?

- **Start early, then ride the wave.** Sana was nine years old when generative AI made its products urgent.
- **Incumbents pay for interfaces.** Workday bought an AI front door rather than building one.
- **A niche can lead to a broader product.** Learning software grew into general workplace agents.
- **Exits hide the numbers.** With revenue undisclosed, the $1.1B price is the only financial signal outsiders get.
