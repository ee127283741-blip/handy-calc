ClaudeKit's monthly recurring revenue is just $28. Its lifetime revenue is $83,068 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claudekit)). That gap tells the whole story: ClaudeKit is a product people buy once, not a subscription they rent. The kit, founded in Vietnam in October 2025, packages Claude Code agents for developers who are tired of rebuilding their AI workflow on every project.

## What does ClaudeKit give Claude Code users?

ClaudeKit gives Claude Code users a ready-made team of specialized agents for planning, coding, testing, reviewing and deployment. The listing says it works with any tech stack, with zero lock-in, full source code ownership and instant license-key access ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claudekit)).

> "Keep what works. Change what doesn't. Make it yours." — ClaudeKit company description on TrustMRR ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claudekit))

The problem it targets is repetition: reinventing context, conventions and workflows for each project is slow and inconsistent. The audience is broad, from software engineers and full-stack builders to growth teams, founders and marketers.

## Why is ClaudeKit's MRR so low next to its total?

The low MRR comes from the pricing, not weak demand. The core product is $99 one-time kit access, and the only recurring items are Desktop App licenses at $49 or $99 a year and an AgentKit Bundle at $149 ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claudekit)). One-time sales never show up as MRR, so the 8 subscriptions only reflect the small recurring layer.

The trade-off is clear. A one-time price is easy to say yes to for developers wary of another subscription, but each month starts close to zero and depends on new buyers.

## How does ClaudeKit compare with Conductor and SoldComps?

All three sell to technical buyers, but ClaudeKit is the only one built on one-time sales.

Our observation: Conductor and SoldComps charge for ongoing access to data or infrastructure, so customers keep paying while they use it. ClaudeKit sells files and workflows the buyer owns forever. That makes it faster to sell but harder to compound, which is why its total looks healthy while MRR barely registers.

## What are the takeaways from ClaudeKit's model?

- **Ride a platform wave.** Building on Claude Code put the kit in front of developers already adopting that tool.
- **One-time pricing lowers friction.** A single $99 payment avoids subscription fatigue among developers.
- **MRR can hide real revenue.** $28 MRR sits beside $83,068 all time ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claudekit)); judge one-time products by total and 30-day sales.
- **Ownership is a selling point.** Full source code and zero lock-in answer the buyer's main worry about adopting someone else's workflow.
