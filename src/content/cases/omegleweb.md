Random chat with strangers is an old internet habit, and it still pays. OmegleWeb, founded in the United States in February 2026, turned that leftover demand into $415,771 of revenue in about nine months ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/omegleweb)). By November 2026 it was earning $61,878 in MRR from 1,949 active subscriptions, which makes it one of the larger consumer sites in this batch.

## What is OmegleWeb and how does random chat work there?

OmegleWeb is a random chat site where users talk to strangers by video or text. The listing describes interest-based matching and active moderation as its answer to the safety problems of older random chat platforms ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/omegleweb)). The audience is adults aged 18 and above.

> "OmegleWeb is a free Omegle alternative to talk to strangers with random video chat or text chat." — OmegleWeb company description ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/omegleweb))

The listing also notes that the site is not affiliated with Omegle.com. The founder is not disclosed; the founder field shows only the company's own X account.

## Why does a free chat site have nearly 2,000 subscribers?

The listing calls the product free, yet Stripe shows 1,949 active subscriptions. The likely reading is a free core experience with paid extras on top, but the paid plans themselves are not disclosed on the page. What is clear is that a well-known brand gap, the Omegle name, gave the site a ready-made search term and audience from day one.

## Which lessons from OmegleWeb apply to chat platforms?

- **A famous name leaves a search term behind.** "Omegle alternative" gave OmegleWeb instant demand.
- **Safety is a selling point.** Moderation and interest matching are the stated differentiators.
- **Free at the front, paid behind.** The listing says free, yet 1,949 subscriptions pay.
- **Be clear about affiliation.** The site states it is not tied to Omegle.com.
