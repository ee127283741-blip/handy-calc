Alex Rainey's first startup did everything right and still died. Pluto, a travel-insurance company he founded after six years at Accenture, climbed to second place on Trustpilot just before the pandemic shut down travel. His second attempt took the opposite route: no investors, a team of two, and a product that went from idea to first version in three weeks. My AskAI now answers more than 75,000 support chats a month and makes about $40K in monthly recurring revenue ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/bootstrapping-to-40k-mrr-after-his-vc-backed-startup-failed-LF1CwRs1vL3oVLcuoIoE)).

## Why did Alex Rainey and Mike Heap start My AskAI?

The spark was technical. When OpenAI released its new embedding models, Rainey realised software could search a large body of content and answer a specific question from it, for example by digging the right answer out of a help centre with more than 100 pages. Customer support was the obvious place to apply that. The timing was also personal: Rainey had just become a father, and a profitable, flexible bootstrapped business looked better than chasing venture money again or going back to a large company. He founded My AskAI with cofounder Mike Heap in early 2023, after building and selling a few small SaaS experiments.

## How does My AskAI work?

My AskAI lets a company create an AI customer support agent from its own content and launch it inside the support tools it already uses, such as Intercom and Zendesk. The agent answers customer questions from the company's help articles and documents. The first version was deliberately simple: upload content, ask a question, get an answer. That narrow loop is still the core of the product, and the integrations mean a support team does not have to switch help desks to use it.

## How was My AskAI built?

The founders shipped the first working version in three weeks and spent under $1K on Bubble and OpenAI to get it running, paid for with cash from earlier projects ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/bootstrapping-to-40k-mrr-after-his-vc-backed-startup-failed-LF1CwRs1vL3oVLcuoIoE)). Rainey handled the product and Heap ran customer interviews and growth tests, while a few indie-hacker friends tested the beta. About 80% of the core app still runs on the no-code tool Bubble, with a Python backend and mainly OpenAI models. The team moved to Bubble's Enterprise tier once daily traffic reached 50,000 requests, and is gradually moving more infrastructure onto AWS. Rainey says no-code still surprises him with its speed, even compared with coding using AI tools like Cursor.

## How does My AskAI market and price its product?

Most of My AskAI's customers find it through Google. The founders did not rely on clever growth tactics. Instead they kept the copy on their site and in their docs specific and consistent about what makes them different, for example the claim that they are 5x cheaper than Intercom's Fin or Zendesk's AI. Rainey says that wording helps people who are searching for alternatives find them, on Google and, increasingly, through ChatGPT. Small tests with paid ads and targeted outreach have not shown a good enough return to justify more spend.

Pricing is a pure SaaS model: tiered plans plus usage costs based on conversation volume. Tying part of the bill to conversations means revenue grows as customers handle more support through the product. The founders also hold onboarding calls with every serious potential customer and ask new users to say how they heard about My AskAI.

## What challenges did My AskAI face?

The biggest early mistake was focus. In the first year, every new AI model pulled the founders into experiments that were fun but did not move the business. They also started too broad, as a "chat with anything" tool, and only narrowed to customer support after 12 months. Rainey says he would niche down much sooner next time.

Scaling on Bubble caused several large outages before the team upgraded to the Enterprise tier, and Rainey wishes he had stayed closer to those infrastructure issues and upgraded earlier. Attribution is the other hard problem: customers take two to four weeks after signing up to start paying, which makes it difficult to know which marketing actually works.

## How much revenue does My AskAI make?

My AskAI reached $10K MRR in its fourth month, stalled, pivoted to customer support, and then climbed to $40K MRR six months later ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/bootstrapping-to-40k-mrr-after-his-vc-backed-startup-failed-LF1CwRs1vL3oVLcuoIoE)). In July 2025 Rainey put revenue at about $40K MRR, or roughly $500K ARR, with a team of just two people. Monthly churn dropped from 9% to 3% after better onboarding and analytics, and gross margin sits around 82% with AI costs included. Total revenue is not disclosed.

The timeline: founded in early 2023; first version shipped in three weeks; $10K MRR in month four; a pivot to customer support after about a year; around $40K MRR by July 2025. The stated goal at that point was $1M ARR within the year while staying a two-person team, which Rainey himself called a stretch ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/bootstrapping-to-40k-mrr-after-his-vc-backed-startup-failed-LF1CwRs1vL3oVLcuoIoE)).

## Why did My AskAI work?

The integrations removed the hardest objection. A support team does not have to replace Intercom or Zendesk to try My AskAI, so the purchase is an add-on, not a migration. That makes a small team's sales job much easier.

Specific copy turned search into a sales channel. Instead of vague AI claims, My AskAI states a concrete comparison against the tools buyers already know. People looking for a cheaper alternative to Intercom's Fin or Zendesk's AI are exactly the customers it wants, and that kind of plain, comparable statement is also what answer engines pick up when users ask ChatGPT for alternatives.

Low costs gave the founders time. Building on Bubble for under $1K and keeping the team at two meant the business could survive a stall and a pivot. A venture-backed company with the same early numbers would have faced much more pressure. For two other customer-support chatbots built on the same idea, see [Chatbase](/cases/chatbase/) and [SiteGPT](/cases/sitegpt/).

## What can founders learn from My AskAI?

Pick the use case early. My AskAI lost about a year as a general "chat with anything" tool before it found its market in customer support, and growth followed the narrower positioning.

Integrate with the tools your buyers already pay for. Plugging into an existing help desk lets a customer try your product without a risky switch.

Write the comparison your customers are searching for. A clear, honest line about how you differ from the market leader can drive search traffic for years.

Treat churn as a product problem. The drop from 9% to 3% came from better onboarding and analytics, not from new features. Rainey's own advice adds that every support ticket is a marketing opportunity, so answer it as if the whole internet were reading.
