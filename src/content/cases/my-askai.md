Alex Rainey's first startup did everything right and still died. Pluto, the travel-insurance company he founded after six years at Accenture, climbed to second place on Trustpilot just before the pandemic shut down travel. His second attempt took the opposite route: no investors, a team of two, and a first version shipped in three weeks for under $1K. My AskAI now answers more than 75,000 support chats a month and makes about $40K in monthly recurring revenue ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/bootstrapping-to-40k-mrr-after-his-vc-backed-startup-failed-LF1CwRs1vL3oVLcuoIoE)).

## What problem does My AskAI solve for support teams?

Support teams answer the same questions again and again, even when the answer already sits somewhere in a help centre with hundreds of pages. Rainey's light-bulb moment came when OpenAI released new embedding models: software could now search a large body of content and pull out the answer to one specific question. Customer support was the obvious place to use that.

My AskAI turns a company's help articles and documents into an AI support agent. The key decision was where that agent lives. Instead of asking companies to switch help desks, it plugs into the tools they already pay for, such as Intercom and Zendesk. Rainey founded it with cofounder Mike Heap in early 2023, shortly after he had become a father and decided a profitable, flexible business beat chasing venture money again.

## How did two founders build it on Bubble for under $1K?

The first version took three weeks and cost under $1K in Bubble and OpenAI fees, paid for with cash from earlier projects ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/bootstrapping-to-40k-mrr-after-his-vc-backed-startup-failed-LF1CwRs1vL3oVLcuoIoE)). The MVP did one thing: upload content, ask a question, get an answer. Rainey built the product, Heap ran customer interviews and growth tests, and a few indie-hacker friends tested the beta.

| Layer | What My AskAI uses |
|---|---|
| Core app | About 80% Bubble (front end and workflows), on Bubble Enterprise since daily traffic hit 50,000 requests |
| Backend | Python |
| AI models | Mainly OpenAI, with others for specific use cases |
| Hosting | Moving more infrastructure onto AWS |
| Team tools | Notion, Slack, Cursor, ChatGPT |

Rainey says no-code still surprises him with its speed, even compared with coding using AI tools like Cursor. The price came later: several large outages while scaling on Bubble, before the team upgraded to the Enterprise tier. He says he should have stayed closer to those infrastructure issues and upgraded earlier.

## Where do My AskAI's customers come from?

Mostly from Google. There was no clever growth playbook. Rainey's view is blunt:

> "IMHO, growth is the hardest part about running a startup." — Alex Rainey ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/bootstrapping-to-40k-mrr-after-his-vc-backed-startup-failed-LF1CwRs1vL3oVLcuoIoE))

What worked was being specific and consistent in every line of copy on the site and in the docs, including a direct comparison buyers can check: 5x cheaper than Intercom's Fin or Zendesk's AI. That wording helps people who are searching for an alternative find My AskAI, on Google and, Rainey says, more and more often through ChatGPT. Small tests with paid ads and targeted outreach have not shown a good enough return to scale.

Attribution is the hard part. Customers take two to four weeks after signing up before they pay, so it is difficult to know which channel deserves more time. The founders now hold onboarding calls with every serious prospect and ask new users how they heard about the product. Pricing is pure SaaS: tiered plans plus usage costs based on conversation volume.

## How much revenue does My AskAI make?

My AskAI hit $10K MRR in its fourth month, stalled, pivoted, and then grew to $40K MRR six months later ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/bootstrapping-to-40k-mrr-after-his-vc-backed-startup-failed-LF1CwRs1vL3oVLcuoIoE)). In July 2025 Rainey put it at about $40K MRR, roughly $500K ARR, with the same two-person team. Total revenue is not disclosed.

| When | Milestone |
|---|---|
| Early 2023 | Founded by Alex Rainey and Mike Heap |
| Three weeks in | First working version, built for under $1K |
| Month four | $10K MRR, then a stall |
| About 12 months in | Pivot from "chat with anything" to customer support |
| July 2025 | About $40K MRR (~$500K ARR), 75,000+ chats a month, team of two |

Two numbers explain why the revenue is healthy. Monthly churn fell from 9% to 3% after better onboarding and analytics, and gross margin is around 82% with AI costs included ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/bootstrapping-to-40k-mrr-after-his-vc-backed-startup-failed-LF1CwRs1vL3oVLcuoIoE)). The founders were aiming for $1M ARR within the year while staying at two people, a goal Rainey himself called a stretch.

## How does My AskAI compare with Chatbase and SiteGPT?

All three started from the same idea in 2023: train a chatbot on a company's own content. They split on where the bot lives and who buys it. [Chatbase](/cases/chatbase/) and [SiteGPT](/cases/sitegpt/) began as chatbots a company adds to its own website. My AskAI bet on the help desk instead, so its buyer is a support team that already runs Intercom or Zendesk and wants to cut ticket volume without switching tools.

That changes the sales conversation. A website widget competes with every other chatbot. An add-on for an existing help desk competes mainly with the help desk's own AI, which is why My AskAI's copy leads with a price comparison against Intercom's Fin and Zendesk's AI. The trade-off is reach: My AskAI depends on those platforms and their integrations, while a standalone widget can sell to any website. Like [Instantly](/cases/instantly/), it also shows how far a bootstrapped team can get with a narrow, well-defined buyer.

For venture-funded AI support agents sold to large companies, see [Sierra](/cases/sierra/) and [Decagon](/cases/decagon/).

## What are the key takeaways from My AskAI?

- **Niche down early.** My AskAI spent about a year as a general "chat with anything" tool, and growth came only after it focused on customer support.
- **Plug into the tools buyers already pay for.** Living inside Intercom and Zendesk turns a risky switch into an easy add-on.
- **Write the comparison your buyers search for.** A concrete line against the market leader keeps bringing in search and ChatGPT traffic.
- **Fix churn with onboarding, not features.** The drop from 9% to 3% came from better onboarding and analytics.
- **Spend little so you can survive a stall.** Under $1K to build and a team of two meant the pivot was affordable.
