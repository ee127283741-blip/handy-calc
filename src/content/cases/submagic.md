Submagic revenue reached $1M ARR on August 1, 2023, three months after the first customer paid. Co-founder and CEO David Zitoun gave those dates in a June 2025 interview, when the company was at $8M ARR with 13 people and $0 in outside funding ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/submagic-david-zitoun-2025)). The product does one job: it turns video into ready-to-post shorts. That focus, an affiliate army and heavy internal use of AI got it there without a single investor.

## What does Submagic do for short-form creators?

Submagic is a tool for creating viral shorts quickly from short or long videos, and it was adding an AI caption tool at the time of the June 2025 interview. The timing helped. Latka's interview notes credit the rise of ChatGPT and the short-form trend around creators such as Alex Hormozi as an accelerant ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/submagic-david-zitoun-2025)). Zitoun said the company started with two people. He stayed close to users from the start, with WhatsApp groups for early customers, a Discord server of about 7,600 members and five or six user calls a day. His advice on this was blunt: "Talk to them. Be close to your customers."

## How fast did Submagic's revenue grow to $8M ARR?

Submagic's revenue grew from $0 to $1M ARR in three months, and to $8M ARR in about two years. Zitoun told Latka the first customer came in May 2023 and the company passed $1M ARR on August 1, 2023 ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/submagic-david-zitoun-2025)). By June 2025 it was at $8M ARR, roughly $667K a month ([calc, from Latka's $8M](https://getlatka.com/interviews/submagic-david-zitoun-2025)). With 13 people, that is about $615K of ARR per person (calc). Profit and total revenue are not disclosed.

## Why does Submagic pay affiliates 30% for life?

Submagic pays 30% for life because affiliates who make videos about the product are also its best advertising. The program launched about 30 days after the first customer. By June 2025 it had more than 10,000 affiliates, and Zitoun said it directly brought in about 20% of ARR ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/submagic-david-zitoun-2025)). Zitoun also taught affiliates how to make viral short-form content about Submagic. The product makes short videos, and affiliates promote it with short videos made in it. In our reading, that loop is what made a lifetime commission affordable. Paid ads add to it, at about $50,000 a month across Facebook and Google, and the team was testing where to place the paywall. Plans are priced by monthly video volume. In November 2026 the pricing page listed Starter at $19, Pro at $39 and Business at $69 a month ([founder-reported, Submagic pricing page](https://www.submagic.co/pricing)).

## How does Submagic run on 13 people?

Submagic runs on 13 people because the team uses AI for its own work as heavily as its customers do. Zitoun put it this way:

> "Without AI, we should have been maybe 40 in the company or 50 people to do what we do." — David Zitoun ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/submagic-david-zitoun-2025))

The team is fully remote and split into product and engineering, marketing, and customer experience. About 8 of the 13 were Submagic users before they were hired. The weak spot is retention. Zitoun said gross logo churn was about 15% a month and called it "very high month over month." Signups were 5,000 to 10,000 a day, and about 2,500 new paying customers came in the month before the interview. A free-tools SEO strategy once drew up to 1,000,000 visitors a month but converted poorly.

## How does Submagic compare with OpusClip, Vidon.ai and neural frames?

Submagic is the bootstrapped answer to [OpusClip](/cases/opus-clip/), which also serves short-form video creators. OpusClip took $20M from SoftBank Vision Fund 2 in March 2025 ([media-reported, Net Influencer](https://netinfluencer.com/opusclip-secures-20m-usd-from-softbank-vision-fund-2-launches-ai-video-search-tool)) and reported nearly $20M ARR, a figure we label unverified ([unverified, ARR Club](https://www.arr.club/signal/opusclip-20m-arr-softbank-mar-2025)). The products overlap, since Submagic also works from long-form video and sells an add-on called Magic Clips. The bigger difference is money. Submagic reached $8M ARR with no outside money ([founder-reported, Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/submagic-david-zitoun-2025)).

[Vidon.ai](/cases/vidon-ai/) turned blog posts into videos and reached $6K MRR in five months by selling to about six agencies over cold email ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/vidon-ai)). [neural frames](/cases/neural-frames/) built music videos for musicians and reached $17K MRR by the end of October 2023 ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/neural-frames)). Both stayed small and sold to narrow buyers. Submagic aimed at the very large pool of everyday creators and built distribution through those creators.

## What are the takeaways from Submagic for bootstrapped founders?

- **Let customers sell the product in its own format.** Submagic's affiliates promote a short-video tool with short videos, and they bring in about 20% of ARR.
- **A lifetime commission can pay for itself.** 30% for life sounds expensive, but it bought 10,000+ affiliates without venture money.
- **Use AI inside the company, not just in the product.** Zitoun estimates the same work would take 40 to 50 people without it.
- **Fast growth can hide churn.** About 15% monthly logo churn means Submagic has to replace much of its customer base all the time.
- **Hire from your users.** Most of the 13-person team used Submagic before joining, which keeps the team close to the customer.
