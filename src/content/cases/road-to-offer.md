Consulting firms like McKinsey, BCG and Bain hire through case interviews, and candidates have long practised them with friends and printed casebooks. Road to offer turned that grind into a subscription. Ten months after a January 2026 founding in France, it showed $4,199 in monthly recurring revenue and $21,246 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/road-to-offer)), and its founder says he runs it alongside a full-time job.

## What does Road to offer train candidates for?

Road to offer trains candidates for consulting case interviews. It offers practice cases, drills and AI feedback for interviews at McKinsey, BCG, Bain and other firms, and sells to individual students and young professionals rather than universities ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/road-to-offer)). The listing notes that it is independent and not affiliated with any of those firms.

The pitch is a single promise: everything a candidate needs to earn a consulting offer. The stack listed is Next.js and React Native on the front end, with Supabase, Stripe, Sentry and Railway behind it.

## Why does Road to offer sell a 90-day sprint?

Case prep is seasonal and has an end date: once a candidate lands an offer, they stop. A one-time $120 sprint matches that window, while the $249 annual plan suits people preparing over a longer recruiting cycle ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/road-to-offer)). Pricing around the job hunt, not around a forever subscription, is a sensible fit for the use case.

The founder describes the growth engine in his message on the listing.

> "SEO driven automatic marketing, DR:26, number 1 LLM ranking" — Road to offer founder message ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/road-to-offer))

## How does Road to offer compare with ApplyAll and Hirevire?

All three sell into hiring, but from different sides of the table.

| [Hirevire](/cases/hirevire/) | Employers screening candidates | $13,766 | [verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/hirevire) |

Our observation: Road to offer and ApplyAll both charge individuals during a stressful, temporary search, so churn is built in. Hirevire sells to employers who hire all year, which is a steadier base. Road to offer's narrow focus on one interview format lets it charge $49 a month, a premium for a single niche ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/road-to-offer)).

## What can interview prep founders learn from Road to offer?

- **A narrow exam is a market.** Case interviews are one format at a handful of firms, and that was enough for 94 subscriptions.
- **Match pricing to the deadline.** A one-time sprint fits a goal that ends when the offer arrives.
- **SEO can run without the founder.** The founder credits SEO-driven marketing while working full time.
- **Side projects can be sold.** The founder says he lacks the time to grow it, which makes a verified revenue record useful to a buyer.
