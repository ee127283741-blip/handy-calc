HeyGen revenue passed $200M ARR in June 2026, and the company got there on less money than many startups spend on one funding round. CEO Joshua Xu told Upstarts that HeyGen had raised $74M in total and burned only about $25M of it ([founder-reported, Upstarts](https://www.upstartsmedia.com/p/exclusive-heygen-200m-arr-without-burn)). The AI avatar video company says it makes about $2.70 of ARR for every dollar of equity raised ([founder-reported, HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/blog/heygen-surpasses-200m-arr)). In a market where rivals raise hundreds of millions, HeyGen grew mostly on its own revenue.

## What does HeyGen make, and who uses it?

HeyGen lets anyone create a video presenter from their own face and voice. Its latest avatar model needs a 15-second clip to build a digital twin, which can then present a script in 175+ languages and dialects ([HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/blog/heygen-surpasses-200m-arr)). The company says it has more than 30 million users in 196 countries, who have made over 118 million videos, and that 85% of the Fortune 100 use it ([founder-reported, HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/blog/heygen-surpasses-200m-arr)).

Xu's pitch is about trust, not spectacle. Upstarts reports that HeyGen focuses on a person's identity and message rather than cinematic effects, which is why many of its users are creators and companies presenting to camera.

> "People don't want more AI slop. They want to communicate with trust, clarity, and presence" — Joshua Xu ([founder-reported, HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/blog/heygen-surpasses-200m-arr))

## How did HeyGen get to $1M ARR in under six months?

HeyGen shipped fast and charged early. In a blog post, Xu wrote that the company launched on July 29, 2022, reached $1M ARR after 178 days and became ramen-profitable after 217 days ([founder-reported, HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/blog/0-1m-arr-in-7-months)). The first paying customer paid $5. The team was about 30 people across three continents, released new features every week, and built more than 200 templates to help new users get started. Xu also wrote that the company was founded in December 2020, so the product launch came about 19 months after the start.

## How much ARR does HeyGen make now?

HeyGen's ARR passed $200M in June 2026, up from $100M about eight months earlier ([founder-reported, Upstarts](https://www.upstartsmedia.com/p/exclusive-heygen-200m-arr-without-burn)). That is about $16.7M a month on our arithmetic (calc). Xu told Upstarts the company had been cash-flow break-even so far in 2026 and had burned little cash since it passed $10M ARR three years earlier. He also cited about $1.5M in revenue per full-time employee. Total revenue, headcount and valuation are not disclosed.

## What does HeyGen charge?

HeyGen uses a self-serve subscription ladder with credits for heavier use. In November 2026 its pricing page listed a free plan, Creator at $29 a month ($24 billed yearly), Pro at $49, Business at $149 a month plus $20 for each extra seat, and custom Enterprise contracts ([founder-reported, HeyGen pricing](https://www.heygen.com/pricing)). Upstarts describes the model as usage-based and credit-driven. In our reading, the low entry price is what brings in millions of individual users, and the Business and Enterprise tiers are where the Fortune 100 accounts pay.

## How does HeyGen compare with Synthesia, ElevenLabs and Arcads?

HeyGen and [Synthesia](/cases/synthesia/) both sell AI avatar video, and HeyGen has moved ahead on revenue. Synthesia passed $100M ARR in April 2025 ([founder-reported, Synthesia](https://www.synthesia.io/post/100-million-revenue-adobe-investment)) and its CFO told CNBC it reached $150M by January 2026 ([founder-reported via CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/26/nvidia-alphabet-vc-arms-back-synthesia.html)), the month it raised at a $4B valuation. [ElevenLabs](/cases/elevenlabs/), which sells AI voices rather than faces, closed 2025 above $330M ARR ([founder-reported, ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/series-d)). [Arcads](/cases/arcads/) uses AI actors for ad videos and told Latka it had $10M ARR with a team of 8 in November 2025 ([founder-reported via Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/arcads-romain-torres-2025)).

The biggest difference is capital. Synthesia and ElevenLabs raised large venture rounds. HeyGen says it raised $74M in total and burned about a third of it. In our view, a low-priced self-serve product that brings in revenue from day one let HeyGen grow without depending on new funding.

## What lessons does HeyGen hold for capital-efficient founders?

- **Charge from the first week.** HeyGen charged its very first customer and was ramen-profitable within 217 days of launch.
- **Efficiency is a selling point.** Burning about $25M to reach $200M ARR gave HeyGen a story rivals with bigger rounds cannot tell.
- **Self-serve can reach the enterprise.** A $29 plan brought in millions of users, and 85% of the Fortune 100 followed.
- **Pick a clear position.** HeyGen sells trustworthy presenters, not cinematic effects, which sets it apart from general video generators.
