Fitted app revenue reached $150K, and the AI closet app passed 600K downloads, without its CEO writing traditional code. The Superwall Podcast describes how Reid Moncada built Fitted, an AI app that digitizes your closet and styles outfits, as a no-code build, and says it has generated $150K in revenue ([founder-reported, The Superwall Podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2522779/episodes/18264040-reid-moncada-he-went-from-0-to-150k-by-building-an-ai-fashion-app-no-code)). Growth started with a $20 video. The real asset, he argues, is what the app learns: data about what people actually own.

## What does the Fitted app do?

Fitted turns a phone into a digital wardrobe. Its App Store listing describes it this way:

> "AI-powered wardrobe app that organizes your clothes, creates outfits, and lets you virtually try on looks with your digital twin" — Fitted's App Store listing ([founder-reported, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6596771952))

Superwall adds that Fitted helps users style outfits and taps into resale markets, and that users have uploaded over 2 million clothing items ([founder-reported, Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/how-reid-moncada-built-a-viral-ai-fashion-app-that-hit-600k)). Moncada's thesis is that retailers know what you buy, but no one knows what your closet holds. Pricing is not disclosed.

## How did Fitted start before it was an app?

Fitted began closer to a service. A Playkit newsletter described a laundry service that would also digitize your closet in the process ([Playkit](https://playkit.beehiiv.com/p/fitted-laundry)). Moncada and Max Birch later presented Fitted at the Fashion Institute of Technology's entrepreneurship lecture series ([FIT](https://events.fitnyc.edu/event/entrepreneurship-lecture-series-max-birch-and-reid-moncada-of-fitted/)). Then a web version struggled: Superwall says his audience was mobile-first and the web experience was not resonating, so Fitted pivoted to a native mobile app.

## How much revenue has Fitted made?

Fitted has generated $150K in revenue, according to The Superwall Podcast ([founder-reported, The Superwall Podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2522779/episodes/18264040-reid-moncada-he-went-from-0-to-150k-by-building-an-ai-fashion-app-no-code)). Monthly revenue is not disclosed, so we cannot say whether the $150K is a run rate or a lifetime total; we treat it as total revenue. Superwall's November 2025 write-up puts downloads at 600K ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/how-reid-moncada-built-a-viral-ai-fashion-app-that-hit-600k)).

## How did a $20 video become Fitted's growth engine?

Fitted built distribution before the product was finished. Moncada paid a creator $20 per video, and the episode describes a "crawl, walk, run" partnership that turned the deal into a co-founder relationship ([The Superwall Podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2522779/episodes/18264040-reid-moncada-he-went-from-0-to-150k-by-building-an-ai-fashion-app-no-code)). Our reading: a small, repeatable payment that grows into a partnership gives the creator a stake in the app's success, which a one-off sponsorship does not.

## How is Fitted different from Lumoo and Glam Up?

[Lumoo](/cases/lumoo/) also applies AI to fashion, but it sells to brands, which pay for product images and try-ons. Fitted sells to consumers and collects data about what they own. [Glam Up](/cases/glam-up/) is the closest consumer peer: a founder without an engineering background and a viral beauty app. Glam Up reports far higher monthly revenue; Fitted's pitch rests more on its closet data than on subscriptions.

Our reading: consumer AI apps with modest revenue can still be valuable if usage creates a dataset nobody else has. Fitted's 2 million uploaded items are that bet.

## What are the lessons from Fitted for consumer app founders?

- **Follow your audience's device.** Fitted moved to mobile because the web version did not fit its users.
- **Pay creators small, then partner.** A $20 video deal grew into a co-founder relationship.
- **Data can be the product.** Two million uploaded items tell Fitted what people own, not just what they buy.
- **Be precise about revenue.** The $150K figure is not described as monthly.
