Anyone can download the code behind Postiz from GitHub and run it for free. Yet TrustMRR showed the company at $373,501 MRR through Stripe in November 2026, with $1,748,934 in all-time Stripe revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/postiz)). Even that undercounts it. The same page says a significant portion of recurring revenue goes through Whop and sits outside both numbers. Founder Nevo David gives away the software and sells the job of running it.

## How did Nevo David get from a failed launch tool to Postiz?

Nevo David reached Postiz by reposting a rejected product under a clearer name. David spent about seven years as a full-stack engineer at Israeli startups and went through failing startups. In 2022 David joined the open source startup Novu as a growth marketer and took it to 30,000 GitHub stars in two years ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/growing-an-open-source-product-to-1-3m-arr-in-two-years-hbMiXIoZsueV9D3L58DP)). Consulting for open source companies followed. Indie Hackers puts that income at $3,000-$4,000 a month, while the Trends.vc podcast says about twelve thousand dollars a month, and we can't reconcile the two.

The first product, Gitroom, helped open source projects prepare launches. David worked on it for about six months and concluded the market was too small. When it went up on Reddit's self-hosting forum, commenters tore it apart. David then renamed it Postiz and posted it again as a free alternative to Buffer and Hootsuite, and people asked how to install it ([media-reported, Trends.vc](https://podcast.trends.vc/episodes/this-free-scheduler-earns-326k-month-from-ai-agents/transcript)). In our reading, the pain was the bill for tools like Buffer and Hootsuite, not launch planning. TrustMRR dates the company to July 2024 and lists it in Hong Kong SAR China.

## Should you self-host the Postiz open source social scheduler or pay for the cloud?

Self-host if you are happy to run a server, and pay if you are not: it is the same scheduler, and the choice is who runs it. Postiz schedules posts across more than 30 platforms and has been open source since day one ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/growing-an-open-source-product-to-1-3m-arr-in-two-years-hbMiXIoZsueV9D3L58DP)). The code is licensed under AGPL-3.0 and had 40,978 GitHub stars as of 2026-11 ([verified, GitHub](https://github.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app)). Stars show interest, not income.

| | Self-hosted open source | Paid Postiz cloud | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| License or price | AGPL-3.0, free to run | $29 to $99 a month | [verified, GitHub](https://github.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app); [founder-reported, Postiz pricing](https://postiz.com/pricing) |
| Server | You rent one | Postiz runs it | [media-reported, Trends.vc](https://podcast.trends.vc/episodes/this-free-scheduler-earns-326k-month-from-ai-agents/transcript) |
| Updates and broken posts | You install and fix them | Postiz handles them | [media-reported, Trends.vc](https://podcast.trends.vc/episodes/this-free-scheduler-earns-326k-month-from-ai-agents/transcript) |

David has stated the rule in two lines: "Everything must be open-sourced" and "Charge only for cloud costs, never force developers to pay" ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-did-it-my-open-source-company-now-makes-14-2k-monthly-as-a-single-developer-f2fec088a4)).

The build priorities followed that model. Late in 2025, David paused new features for a month or two and rebuilt each part of the app to post faster and fail less, because a dropped post can lose a customer. Then the interface stopped being the center of the product. On the Trends.vc podcast, David's advice is summed up as building for AI agents through a CLI and MCP, a connector that lets an assistant such as Claude operate an app. Every plan now includes an MCP server, and official Claude and ChatGPT connectors arrived in October 2026 ([media-reported, Trends.vc](https://podcast.trends.vc/episodes/this-free-scheduler-earns-326k-month-from-ai-agents/transcript)).

## How did Postiz find customers without paying for reach?

Postiz found customers through its free version first. On the first Reddit post, people asked for Docker, and David's earlier Indie Hackers post counts 4.79M Docker downloads. The same post lists the other channels: an official n8n node, a public API, an automation focus, cold outreach to Skool communities, affiliates and SEO ([founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-did-it-my-open-source-company-now-makes-14-2k-monthly-as-a-single-developer-f2fec088a4)). The podcast calls the free version the advertisement.

> "Postiz makes money because it's open-source." — Nevo David ([founder-reported, Trends.vc](https://podcast.trends.vc/episodes/this-free-scheduler-earns-326k-month-from-ai-agents/transcript))

David even turned competitors into marketing. In late October 2026 David asked people to build and launch Postiz alternatives, because each launch tags David and sends more people to Postiz, according to the podcast.

## What does Postiz charge, and what has gone wrong?

Postiz charges $29, $39, $49 or $99 a month for its Standard, Team, Pro and Ultimate cloud plans, each with a 7-day free trial ([founder-reported, Postiz pricing](https://postiz.com/pricing)). The podcast's example buyer is a creator with five accounts who pays $29 a month to avoid running servers.

The biggest test of that promise came on October 21, 2026, when a breach hit Railway, which hosts Postiz's servers. David took the service offline and rotated 140 keys overnight, and Postiz was down for 17 hours. Churn had been the quieter problem. David told Mixergy that people who post by hand and get discouraged cancel, while automation keeps them posting ([founder-reported, Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/revenue-jumped-when-he-sold-to-ai-agents/)). On Starter Story, David said churn fell to 13.7 from 26 and 28 after the agentic pivot; the transcript gives no unit ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/i-grew-my-saas-from-17k-to-143k-mrr-in-4-months)).

## How does Postiz compare with Tweet Hunter, Creator Buddy and ReplyDaddy?

Postiz differs from all three in where its customers first came from: a free download rather than a founder's X following or a Reddit reply. [Tweet Hunter](/cases/tweet-hunter/) and [Creator Buddy](/cases/creator-buddy/) sold to audiences their founders had built on X. [ReplyDaddy](/cases/replydaddy/) helps others win customers inside Reddit threads, the same site where Postiz got its second chance.

| Company | Channel | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Postiz | Free open source code, self-hosting forums | $373,501 MRR, Stripe only (Nov 2026) | [verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/postiz) |
| Tweet Hunter | Founder's X audience | $150K ARR, four months after MVP | [founder-reported, Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-tweet-hunters-mvp-in-2-weeks-4-months-later-it-grew-to-150k-arr-ama-a6ae1ee850) |

| ReplyDaddy | Selling Reddit lead generation | About $5,000 MRR | [founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/replydaddy/founders) |

Our observation: Tweet Hunter and Creator Buddy are tied to X, while Postiz spreads across more than 30 platforms. That doesn't remove platform risk. It moves the work of keeping up with network rule changes onto the cloud team, and that work is part of what customers pay to avoid. Postiz's edge is also its exposure: anyone can take the code, whereas a creator's audience can't be downloaded.

## What can founders learn from the Postiz model?

- **Sell the chore, not the code.** Postiz gives the software away and charges people who would rather not rent servers or fix broken posts.
- **Positioning can rescue the same code.** Gitroom was rejected on Reddit, and the same code under a new name drew install requests ([media-reported, Trends.vc](https://podcast.trends.vc/episodes/this-free-scheduler-earns-326k-month-from-ai-agents/transcript)).
- **Reliability is a growth feature for a hosted product.** David paused new features to rebuild for fewer failures, since the cloud's value depends on posts going out.
- **Automation can address churn.** David's reasoning is that scheduled, automated posting survives the moment a user gets discouraged, and reported churn fell after the agentic pivot.
- **Read payment-tracker figures with their limits.** Postiz's verified $373,501 covers Stripe only, so the Whop share remains unknown.
