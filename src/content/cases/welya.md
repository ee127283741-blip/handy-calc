Webinar software usually measures who showed up. Welya measures who is ready to buy. The platform records every chat message, survey answer and click on an offer during a live session, and it sells that data to the people running sales webinars. In November 2026, Welya showed $31,652 in MRR and $106,128 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/welya-inc)), about 17 months after its founding date.

## Who is Welya built for?

Welya is built for the people behind sales webinars, not for casual meetings. Its TrustMRR profile names freelancers, agencies and marketers who run webinars for large infopreneurs, meaning creators who sell courses and coaching to big audiences ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/welya-inc)). The website is written in French first, with an English version, which points to a French-speaking core market.

> "Welya is more than just a broadcasting tool; it's a conversion tool that tracks every message, survey response, and click on offers to identify future sales opportunities." — Welya, company profile ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/welya-inc))

Marvin Hannachi is listed as the founder of Welya, Inc., and TrustMRR shows a team of 6–10 people. That is a larger team than most products at this revenue level on TrustMRR, which suggests Welya is investing ahead of revenue or carrying service work.

## Which Welya features target webinar conversion?

Welya's features all aim at the moment a viewer decides to buy. The homepage lists chat gamification, A/B tests of offers, conversion notifications, exit pop-ups during the live and an end-of-live screen ([founder-reported, Welya](https://welya.io/)). Social proof gets a push notification and a special status in the chat, so other viewers see when someone buys.

The site's argument is blunt: without a closing room, a webinar stops and the host can lose up to 20% of conversion. That figure is Welya's marketing claim, not a measured result we can check. TrustMRR adds that Welya uses AI to optimise interactions, though neither source spells out which features use it.

Welya earns about $30K a month. TrustMRR showed $31,652 MRR and $29,478 in the last 30 days in November 2026, with 174 active subscriptions ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/welya-inc)).

An average of about $182 per subscription sits between the Launch and Grow prices once currency is converted, so a real share of customers are on the bigger plan. Profit is not disclosed.

Our observation: Welya's market is narrow and high value. Course creators who sell through webinars can measure each live session in sales, so a tool that claims to lift conversion can charge hundreds of euros a month. Poppy AI shows how large a creator tool can get, and Fathom AI shows another narrow sales niche reaching a similar monthly range. Welya is betting on one specific, measurable moment: the offer at the end of a live.

## What are the takeaways from Welya's webinar bet?

- **Sell to the moment money changes hands.** Welya focuses on the offer, not the broadcast, and reached $31,652 MRR in about 17 months ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/welya-inc)).
- **Charge by audience size.** Attendee caps of 500 and 2,000 tie price to the customer's own revenue potential.
- **A local market can be enough.** A French-first site still built a six-figure lifetime business.
- **Late growth hides in averages.** Lifetime revenue that lags far behind MRR is a sign of a steep recent climb.
