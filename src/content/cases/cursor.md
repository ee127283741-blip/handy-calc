Cursor revenue went from a startup number to a public-company number in about 13 months. In January 2025, Anysphere said its AI code editor had passed $100M in recurring revenue ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/series-b)). In November 2025 it said it had crossed $1B in annualized revenue ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/series-d)). By February 2026, Bloomberg put the figure above $2B ([media-reported, Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-02/cursor-recurring-revenue-doubles-in-three-months-to-2-billion)). Then the company stopped raising money and sold itself to SpaceX.

## What did four MIT graduates set out to build with Cursor?

They set out to build an editor where AI writes most of the code. Michael Truell, Sualeh Asif, Aman Sanger and Arvid Lunnemark, all MIT alumni, co-founded Anysphere to build Cursor, an "AI-native" development environment ([MIT News, citing TechCrunch](https://news.mit.edu/news-clip/techcrunch-211)). The company's own pitch was ambitious from the start. In August 2024 it wrote that the aim was "a magical tool that will one day write all the world's software," and said it had grown to over 40,000 customers ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/series-a)). That same post announced a $60M Series A from Andreessen Horowitz and Thrive Capital.

## How fast did Cursor's revenue grow from $100M to $2B?

Cursor's revenue grew about 20x in 13 months, using its own and Bloomberg's figures. Each funding post doubled as a revenue update. The January 2025 Series B post said Cursor had "exceeded $100m in recurring revenue" ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/series-b)). Five months later, the Series C post said over $500M in ARR and use by over half of the Fortune 500 ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/series-c)). The Series D post in November 2025 said $1B+ in annualized revenue and a team of over 300 ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/series-d)).

The $2B figure works out to roughly $167M a month ([calc, from Bloomberg's $2B](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-02/cursor-recurring-revenue-doubles-in-three-months-to-2-billion)). The February 2026 row comes from a single unnamed source, with no audited accounts behind it. Total revenue and profit are not disclosed.

## Why did Cursor build its own models?

Cursor built its own models because it was paying other labs for most of the intelligence it sold. Training in-house gave it a product edge and, over time, control over cost. By January 2025 the company said its proprietary models generated "more code than almost any LLMs in the world" and edited over a billion characters a day ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/series-b)). It repeated the claim in November 2025, when NVIDIA and Google joined the Series D ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/series-d)). In our reading, this is the thread that leads to the SpaceX deal. A company that trains models needs compute, and compute was what SpaceX could offer.

## Why did SpaceX pay $60 billion for Cursor?

SpaceX paid for a coding product with paying customers and a team that trains models, and Cursor got access to a very large GPU fleet. In April 2026 SpaceX announced a deal under which it would either acquire Cursor or pay $10 billion and part ways ([media-reported, Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/spacex-acquires-cursor-60b-20260616)). Bloomberg reported it as a right to acquire Cursor for $60 billion ([media-reported, Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-21/spacex-says-has-agreement-to-acquire-cursor-for-60-billion)). In June 2026 SpaceX said it planned to buy Anysphere in an all-stock deal worth $60 billion, expected to close in the third quarter.

Cursor announced the close on August 14, 2026. Its post said the company "will have access to the largest fleet of GPUs in the world" and pointed to Grok 4.6, released that week, as an early result ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/joining-spacex)). The change shows on the pricing page. In November 2026 it listed a free plan and plans at $8, $30, $100 and $200 a month, plus Teams from $30 a user. It also noted that only Grok models are available on the $8 Plus plan ([founder-reported, Cursor pricing page](https://cursor.com/pricing)).

## How does Cursor compare with Lovable, Bolt.new and Base44?

Cursor is an editor for people who already write code, while the other three are AI app builders that let you describe an app in plain language. In our reading, that difference in buyer explains much of the gap in scale. [Lovable](/cases/lovable/) reached $100M ARR in July 2025, eight months after its first $1M ([media-reported, The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/vibe-coding-platform-lovable-becomes-historys-fastest-growing-software-startup)). Cursor was past $500M ARR the month before ([founder-reported, Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/series-c)). [Bolt.new](/cases/bolt-new/) reached $4M ARR about four weeks after launch ([media-reported, Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/bolt-new-from-stackblitz-how-they-surfed-the-ai-wave-with-no-wipeouts)).

The exits differ by about three orders of magnitude. [Base44](/cases/base44/) sold to Wix for $80M plus possible earn-outs about six months after Maor Shlomo started it alone ([media-reported, TechRadar](https://techradar.com/pro/website-building/wix-jumps-on-the-vibe-coding-bandwagon-with-a-new-acquisition)). Cursor sold for $60B in stock ([media-reported, Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/spacex-acquires-cursor-60b-20260616)). Cursor also shows up inside other cases. [Launch Fast](/cases/launch-fast/) founder Hasaam Bhatti had never written code and built his first version in 48 hours with Cursor. For many small founders, Cursor is the tool behind their product.

For two other AI coding stories, see [Replit](/cases/replit/), which pivoted to non-coders, and [Windsurf](/cases/windsurf/), which was split between Google and Cognition.

## What can founders learn from Cursor's run?

- **Sell to the buyer who already pays.** Developers and their employers already paid for tools, so Cursor could reach over half of the Fortune 500 within about three years of founding.
- **Publish milestones in one place.** Every funding post carried a revenue number, which made Cursor's own blog the primary source for its growth curve.
- **Own the model, not just the interface.** In-house models were Cursor's answer to depending on other labs, and they made compute the thing it needed most.
- **Know which numbers are yours.** Cursor's last self-reported figure is $1B+ annualized; the $2B figure rests on one unnamed source, and total revenue is not disclosed.
- **Fast growth can end in a sale.** Cursor raised at $29.3B in November 2025 and sold for $60B in stock about nine months later.
