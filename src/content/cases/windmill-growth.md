Most AI marketing businesses on TrustMRR sell software for a few dollars a month. Windmill Growth sells people. It writes LinkedIn posts for B2B founders, and in November 2026 that service carried $119,696 in monthly recurring revenue from just 74 active subscriptions, with $2,335,651 collected all time ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/windmill-growth-llc)). Its homepage pitch is a direct shot at AI-written content.

## What does Windmill Growth do for B2B founders on LinkedIn?

Windmill Growth runs a founder's LinkedIn presence for them. The homepage says the founder talks for 30 minutes, and the team writes the posts, publishes them, engages with the ideal customer profile and tries to fill the founder's calendar ([founder-reported, Windmill Growth homepage](https://windmillgrowth.com/)). The TrustMRR listing describes the value proposition as personal branding for B2B founders in SaaS and marketing services.

> "Your LinkedIn brand. Without the AI accent." — Windmill Growth homepage ([founder-reported, Windmill Growth](https://windmillgrowth.com/))

The positioning matters. While most cases on this site use AI to produce more content, Windmill sells the promise that its content does not read like AI.

## Why does Windmill Growth keep only 30 founders on the books?

The homepage says Windmill has worked with 185+ founders to date and keeps 30 on the books at a time ([founder-reported, Windmill Growth homepage](https://windmillgrowth.com/)). That cap reads as a capacity choice for a service business, where each client needs a writer who has learned their voice.

The same page lists client results, such as follower growth and inbound deals traced to LinkedIn. These are the company's own case studies, not audited numbers. TrustMRR lists LinkedIn and X as marketing channels, says the business is bootstrapped and shows Ben Buaron with about 1,288 followers on X, so the founder's own audience is small next to the revenue.

## How is Windmill Growth different from Poppy AI and Creator Buddy?

Windmill Growth is the service version of a problem that software tools on this site also attack: helping people post better content on social platforms.

| Company | Model | What the customer does |
|---|---|---|
| Windmill Growth | Done-for-you ghostwriting, about $3,999 a month list price ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/windmill-growth-llc)) | Talks for 30 minutes |
| [Poppy AI](/cases/poppy-ai/) | AI software for content creation | Writes with AI help |
| [Creator Buddy](/cases/creator-buddy/) | AI software for X creators | Writes with AI help |

Our observation: software tools win on price and volume, while Windmill wins on removing the work entirely. Its 74 subscriptions produce more MRR than many self-serve tools with thousands of users, which shows how high-ticket service pricing changes the math.

## What can AI-era agencies learn from Windmill Growth?

- **Anti-AI positioning can sell.** Windmill's headline promises a brand "without the AI accent", turning the flood of AI content into its reason to exist.
- **Few clients, high price.** 74 active subscriptions carry $119,696 of MRR ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/windmill-growth-llc)).
- **Capacity is the ceiling.** A 30-client cap protects quality but limits growth, a trade-off software businesses do not face.
- **Proof comes from clients.** The homepage leans on named client results rather than the founder's own following.
