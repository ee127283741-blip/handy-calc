Emergent ARR doubled from $50M to $100M in a single month, according to the company. Emergent told TechCrunch in February 2026 that it had passed $100M ARR eight months after launch ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/17/emergent-hits-100m-arr-eight-months-after-launch-rolls-out-mobile-app/)). The vibe-coding platform was started by twin brothers Mukund and Madhav Jha. Most of its revenue comes from outside India, and most of its users have never written code. By July 2026 it was a unicorn.

## Who built Emergent, and what does it do?

Emergent was built by Mukund Jha, who previously co-founded the Indian delivery app Dunzo, and his twin brother Madhav Jha, and it launched in 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/15/indian-ai-coding-startup-emergent-becomes-a-unicorn-just-over-a-year-after-launch/)). Users describe an app in plain language, and Emergent's agents write the code, test it, deploy it and host it. In February 2026 the company added a mobile app, so users can hand a task to the agents and come back later to review progress ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/17/emergent-hits-100m-arr-eight-months-after-launch-rolls-out-mobile-app/)).

## How fast did Emergent ARR grow?

Emergent ARR went from $50M to a $120M run rate in about six months, by the company's own count. TechCrunch reported $50M ARR and more than 5 million users when Emergent raised a $70M Series B at a $300M valuation in January 2026 ([media-reported, TechCrunch via AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/vibe-coding-startup-emergent-raised-110001031.html)). A month later it claimed over $100M ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/17/emergent-hits-100m-arr-eight-months-after-launch-rolls-out-mobile-app/)). By July 2026 it reported a $120M run rate and more than 200,000 paying customers ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/15/indian-ai-coding-startup-emergent-becomes-a-unicorn-just-over-a-year-after-launch/)), about $10M a month (calc).

## Who pays for Emergent?

Small businesses and non-developers in rich markets pay for most of Emergent. Mukund Jha told TechCrunch that nearly 40% of users are small businesses, about 70% have no prior coding experience, and the US and Europe account for roughly 70% of revenue ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/17/emergent-hits-100m-arr-eight-months-after-launch-rolls-out-mobile-app/)). Revenue comes from subscriptions, usage-based pricing, and deployment and hosting fees. India is its next-largest and fastest-growing market. Plan prices, team size and total revenue are not disclosed.

## How does Emergent compare with Lovable and Base44?

Emergent and [Lovable](/cases/lovable/) both reported $100M ARR about eight months after a public launch. [Base44](/cases/base44/) took a different path: its solo founder sold it to Wix about six months in, and the growth to nine figures happened inside Wix. Emergent's twist is geography and billing: an Indian team selling mostly to the US and Europe, with hosting fees on top of subscriptions. The hosting fee matters, because an app that lives on Emergent keeps paying after the build is done.

Our reading: the "ARR" numbers in this category are mostly monthly revenue times 12, and they can swing with promotions and usage. Emergent's own figures show a 2x jump in one month, which is a run rate claim, not a contract base. The paying-customer count, 200,000 by July 2026, is the steadier signal.

[Rocket.new](/cases/rocket-new/), from Surat, is a smaller Indian vibe-coding company selling to users in 180 countries.

## What should app-builder founders learn from Emergent?

- **Build in one market, sell to another.** Emergent's team is in India, but about 70% of its revenue comes from the US and Europe.
- **Charge for hosting, not only building.** Deployment and hosting fees keep revenue coming after the app is finished.
- **Non-coders are the market.** About 70% of users had no coding experience, so the product competes with agencies, not IDEs.
- **Treat one-month jumps with care.** A doubling in a month is a run rate; paying customers show whether it lasts.
