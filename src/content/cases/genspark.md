Genspark ARR went from zero to $36M in 45 days. That is what Genspark told OpenAI about its Super Agent, launched in April 2025, adding that a 20-person team did it without paid advertising ([founder-reported, OpenAI customer story](https://openai.com/index/genspark)). Genspark had started as an AI search engine. Its founders, Eric Jing and Kay Zhu, moved the company from answering questions to doing the work: an agent that produces slides, spreadsheets, documents and research reports from a single prompt.

## How did Genspark go from AI search to an agent workspace?

Genspark changed what it sells: finished work instead of search results. Its Super Agent takes a task, plans the steps, calls tools and returns a deliverable. OpenAI's customer story describes Genspark building that agent on several models and growing entirely through word of mouth and product virality ([founder-reported, OpenAI customer story](https://openai.com/index/genspark)). In January 2026 the company released AI Workspace 2.0, and it said more than 1,000 organizations had started using Genspark for Business since that plan launched in late November 2025 ([founder-reported, Genspark release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/genspark-launches-ai-workspace-2-150000379.html)).

## How fast did Genspark ARR grow after launch?

Genspark ARR grew faster than almost any case in this library, and every milestone comes from the company. It reported $36M ARR 45 days after launch, more than $100M nine months after launch, and $250M in June 2026, about 14 months in ([founder-reported, The Founder Nation](https://www.thefoundernation.com/genspark-closes-100m-series-b-extension-at-2-6b-valuation-as-arr-hits-250m/)). At $250M ARR that is about $20.8M a month (calc).

## How much has Genspark raised, and at what valuation?

Genspark raised a $300M Series B in January 2026 ([founder-reported, Genspark release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/genspark-launches-ai-workspace-2-150000379.html)). In June 2026 it added a $100M extension at a $2.6B post-money valuation, which brought the Series B to $485M ([media-reported, FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2026/06/genspark-ai-extends-series-b-to-485m-at-2-6-billion-valuation.html)). Reports on the size and price of the steps between those two dates differ, so we only list the start and end points. Genspark's founding date and its total revenue are not disclosed. We could not confirm its current plan prices on the official pricing page, so pricing is listed as not disclosed.

## How does Genspark compare with Perplexity and Manus?

Genspark sits between an answer engine and an autonomous agent. [Perplexity](/cases/perplexity/) stayed an AI search company and sells answers with citations. [Manus](/cases/manus/) launched a general agent one month before Genspark's Super Agent and reported crossing $100M ARR eight months after launch ([founder-reported, Manus on X](https://x.com/ManusAI/status/2001291341343641680)). Genspark announced the same $100M mark at nine months. [Gamma](/cases/gamma/) is the closest single-product rival, since slides are one of Genspark's main outputs.

## What can founders learn from Genspark's growth?

- **A pivot can beat a slow climb.** Genspark left a working search product to bet on agents, and the new product passed its old one within weeks.
- **Sell the deliverable.** Users pay for a finished deck or report more readily than for a better answer.
- **Word of mouth still works at scale.** OpenAI says the first $36M ARR came without paid ads, from users sharing what the agent made.
- **Add a business plan quickly.** Genspark for Business launched about seven months after Super Agent and brought in more than 1,000 organizations in two months.
