Turbo AI revenue reached eight figures a year while its founders were still 20. Rudy Arora and Sarthak Dhawan, two college dropouts, told TechCrunch in October 2025 that their AI study app had 5 million users and eight-figure ARR ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/23/20-year-old-dropouts-built-ai-notetaker-turbo-ai-to-5-million-users)). Most of that growth came in six months, from 1 million to 5 million users. They did it on a $750K seed round and say they have been profitable the whole time.

## What is Turbo AI, and who uses it?

Turbo AI turns class material into study material. Students record a lecture or upload a PDF or video, and the app produces notes, flashcards and quizzes. It started as Turbolearn, a study app for students, before renaming to Turbo AI and adding professional users ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/23/20-year-old-dropouts-built-ai-notetaker-turbo-ai-to-5-million-users)). The founders built it for their own studies, then left college to run it. Their team size and founding date are not disclosed.

## How much revenue does Turbo AI earn from students?

Turbo AI earns eight-figure ARR, at least $10M a year, according to its founders ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/23/20-year-old-dropouts-built-ai-notetaker-turbo-ai-to-5-million-users)). They gave no exact number, so monthly and total revenue are not disclosed. Students pay about $20 a month, and the founders said they were testing other prices ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/23/20-year-old-dropouts-built-ai-notetaker-turbo-ai-to-5-million-users)). At $20 a month, $10M ARR would take roughly 42,000 paying subscribers (calc), under 1% of 5 million users.

## Why haven't Turbo AI's founders raised more money?

They say they do not need it. Turbo AI raised a $750K seed round and has stayed cash-flow positive since ([media-reported, Dig Watch](https://dig.watch/updates/two-founders-turn-note-taking-into-an-ai-success)). The founders said they were taking their time on outside funding despite strong investor interest. Profitability on a small seed means they keep control and can choose investors later, when they need them.

> "profitable our entire time as a company" — Rudy Arora ([founder-reported, Dig Watch](https://dig.watch/updates/two-founders-turn-note-taking-into-an-ai-success))

## How does Turbo AI compare with Jenni AI and Speak?

Turbo AI is one of three consumer education businesses in this library with eight- or nine-figure revenue. [Jenni AI](/cases/jenni-ai/) sells academic writing help and reported $10M+ ARR in a single media report. [Speak](/cases/speak/) sells language practice and passed $100M in annualized revenue after a $1B valuation ([media-reported, Forbes via Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/topics/25/11/48975908/this-1-billion-ai-startup-backed-by-openai-khosla-ventures-just-hit-100m-revenue-racing-to-beat-duolingo)). Turbo AI got to eight figures far faster and with far less capital than Speak, by selling one job students do every week: turning a lecture into something they can study from.

Our reading: student apps spread through classrooms and group chats, which is why Turbo AI could add 4 million users in six months without much funding. The weakness is the calendar. Students study hardest before exams and leave in summer, so subscription revenue can swing with the school year.

## What can student-app founders learn from Turbo AI?

- **Build for your own class.** Turbo AI's founders were students solving their own studying problem.
- **One clear job sells.** Lecture-to-flashcards is easy to explain and easy to show a friend.
- **Profit buys independence.** A $750K seed and steady profits let the founders decline to raise.
- **Watch the school calendar.** Exam seasons and summer breaks can make student revenue uneven.
