GoTall sells a number many teenagers care about: how tall they will end up. The iOS app predicts adult height and turns sleep, food and stretching into daily tasks. In November 2026 it showed $55,876 in MRR, 17,221 active subscriptions and $917,193 in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/gotall)), about 16 months after its founding date. Its founder's note on the same page says the team is breaking up.

## What does the GoTall app promise its users?

GoTall promises a predicted adult height and a plan of habits that may support growth. Users enter their parents' heights, their current height, age and ethnicity, and the app returns a projection plus a daily routine covering nutrition, sleep and stretching, according to [its website](https://gotall.app).

The site says sleep tracking focuses on when growth hormone peaks and that guidance draws on data from health organizations including the CDC, while stating that GoTall is not affiliated with or endorsed by the CDC. The page cites no specific studies. The app's own wording, habits that "support healthy development", stops short of promising extra centimeters.

## Why is GoTall's MRR so high per dollar collected?

GoTall's MRR is nearly three times its 30-day revenue, $55,876 against $20,051 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/gotall)). The page does not explain this. One common cause in consumer apps is annual plans: they count toward MRR every month but are collected once. If new sales slowed, collections would fall while older annual subscriptions keep MRR up. That fits the timing, but it is our reading, not a disclosed fact.

MRR per subscription is about $3.24 ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/gotall)), a low figure that also fits discounted annual plans. The founder's note adds context.

> "Moving on to another app and cofounders separating" — Chenglin ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/gotall))

A team moving to its next app would explain lower spending on growth, though the note does not say whether GoTall is for sale.

## What lessons does GoTall teach about consumer app lifecycles?

- **A narrow worry can sell at scale.** A height app reached 17,221 active subscriptions and $917,193 in revenue in about 16 months ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/gotall)).
- **MRR can hide a slowdown.** Collected revenue at about a third of MRR is a warning sign worth reading next to the headline.
- **Apps often have short lives by design.** The founders are moving to another app, a common pattern for studios that ship and move on.
- **Claims about bodies need careful wording.** GoTall frames habits as support for healthy development, not as a guaranteed height gain.
