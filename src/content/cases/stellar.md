A/B testing is supposed to speed up decisions, yet many teams wait weeks for a developer to ship each test. Stellar lets marketers set up a test visually and see results in real time. Founded by Juan Chaher in the United States in February 2025, it had $35,492 in all-time revenue by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/stellar)), earned from just 25 active subscriptions.

## What does Stellar let marketers test without developers?

Stellar lets CRO marketers and growth teams launch visual A/B tests on their websites in minutes and read statistically confident results in real time ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/stellar)). The problem it targets is the slow, developer-dependent process of deploying tests, which delays insight.

> "Make data-driven decisions—today, not next quarter." — Stellar value proposition ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/stellar))

The listing places it in analytics, no-code, AI and marketing, with Stripe for billing.

## How does Stellar compare with DataFast and Simple Analytics?

All three help marketers understand their website, but they sell different jobs.

|---|---|---|---|
| Stellar | Run A/B tests | $2,507 | [verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/stellar) |

Our observation: analytics is a daily habit, so DataFast and Simple Analytics keep more than a thousand subscribers each. Testing is episodic; teams run tests in bursts. Stellar's answer is annual billing and a mid-market average price, trading customer count for revenue per account.

## What are the lessons from Stellar for marketing SaaS?

- **Remove the developer bottleneck.** Stellar's whole pitch is that marketers can ship tests themselves.
- **Annual billing smooths an episodic use case.** A year up front protects revenue between testing sprints.
- **Few accounts, solid price.** 25 subscriptions averaging about $100 a month built $35,492 lifetime revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/stellar)).
- **A free Sandbox lowers the first step.** A $0 plan lets teams try the editor before committing.
