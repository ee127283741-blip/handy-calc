Most marketplaces sell software, domains or whole startups. Tapdust sells something odder: existing online communities, the paid groups and member spaces that creators build on platforms like Whop. Founded in October 2026, it had already collected $3,700 by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tapdust)). That is a fast start, and it rests on a single large subscription.

## What is Tapdust and who buys from it?

Tapdust is a marketplace for buying and evaluating communities. Its listing names three audiences: buyers of existing communities, sponsors, and individuals looking to find their communities ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tapdust)). The problem it claims to solve is blunt.

> "Finding the right communities sucks!" — Tapdust listing on TrustMRR ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/tapdust))

The company calls itself "community-funded" and tags its market as Marketplace and Community. Its backend is Whop, which also handles its payments. Pricing and team size are not disclosed. Its domain, tapdust.com, has an Ahrefs Domain Rating of 14, and the listing classes it as B2B.

## Why does Tapdust run on Whop?

Whop is both Tapdust's backend and its payment rail, and many paid communities already live on Whop. Building on the same platform keeps sellers and buyers close to where the assets are. The trade-off is platform dependence: a marketplace for Whop-style communities rises and falls with that ecosystem.

## Which lessons from Tapdust apply to marketplace founders?

- **Niche assets can support a marketplace.** Communities are a narrow category, yet buyers paid within weeks.
- **Concentration is the main risk.** One active subscription carries all of the MRR.
- **Build where the inventory lives.** Running on Whop keeps Tapdust next to the communities it lists.
- **Verification is the product.** Like TrustMRR, the promise is better evaluation, not just listings.
