StoryShort revenue was listed at about $23.7K a month, and the founder used to fit glasses. Samuel Rondot worked as an optician before he taught himself to code and started building software. His rule is simple: never build anything that is not already working for someone else. A ShipByData listing put StoryShort, his AI short-video tool, at $23,664 MRR, without a date ([unverified, ShipByData](https://shipbydata.pages.dev/en/startup/storyshort)). Across his three apps, he told Starter Story he makes about $35K a month ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/i-cloned-3-apps-and-now-make-35k-month)).

## What does StoryShort make for video creators?

StoryShort is an AI tool that creates short videos from text prompts ([ShipByData](https://shipbydata.pages.dev/en/startup/storyshort)). The same listing marked it as for sale. Prices, team size and the launch date are not disclosed in the sources we found. Rondot described StoryShort to the Slovak outlet Startitup as the follow-up product he built after his first app ([Startitup](https://www.startitup.sk/zarabam-32-000-eur-mesacne-tym-ze-vylepsujem-cudzie-napady-samuel-vymenil-pracu-v-optike-za-vlastny-biznis-z-pohodlia-gauca/)).

## How did a former optician start building SaaS?

Rondot left optics for his own software business, Startitup reported in a profile whose headline says he earns by improving other people's ideas ([Startitup](https://www.startitup.sk/zarabam-32-000-eur-mesacne-tym-ze-vylepsujem-cudzie-napady-samuel-vymenil-pracu-v-optike-za-vlastny-biznis-z-pohodlia-gauca/)). He is self-taught. Unlike the no-code founders on this site, he writes the products himself; what he lacked was an engineering degree or career, not the willingness to learn.

> "I cloned 3 apps and now make $35K/month" — Samuel Rondot, Starter Story interview title ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/i-cloned-3-apps-and-now-make-35k-month))

## Why does Samuel Rondot only clone products that already work?

Rondot told Starter Story he will not build anything that does not already exist and already have some traction, because it lowers his chance of failure ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/i-cloned-3-apps-and-now-make-35k-month)). He looks for working products, mostly on X, that he personally likes, then checks traction, traffic sources and how simple the product is to build. The improvement comes after the copy.

## How does StoryShort compare with Faceless.video and STOPPR?

[Faceless.video](/cases/faceless-video/) sells almost the same promise, AI short videos for faceless channels, and was built by a songwriter on Bubble. [STOPPR](/cases/stoppr/) was built by an ex-quant trader who cloned a successful habit-quitting app. All three founders came from outside software. StoryShort and STOPPR share the clone-first method; Faceless.video reports a larger scale than StoryShort's listed MRR.

Our reading: cloning removes the riskiest question, whether anyone wants the product, and leaves the founder competing on execution and distribution.

## What lessons does StoryShort offer self-taught founders?

- **Start from proven demand.** Rondot only builds products that already have traction.
- **A career change is not a barrier.** An optician learned enough to ship and run three apps.
- **Keep products simple.** Build simplicity is one of his checks before starting.
- **Read revenue labels.** StoryShort's MRR is an undated third-party listing; the portfolio total is self-reported.
