Most subscription businesses on TrustMRR sell software. BackPedal sells a small GPS tracker you hide on a bike, then charges every year for the promise that someone will help get the bike back if it is stolen. In November 2026 that model showed $43,667 in monthly recurring revenue and more than $1.5 million in all-time revenue ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/backpedal-ltd)). Only 383 subscriptions sit behind those numbers.

## What does BackPedal actually sell to cyclists?

BackPedal sells theft protection, not just a gadget. The company-written listing describes the offer as GPS tracking and 24/7 recovery for a bike or ebike, paid as an upfront tracker fee plus a recurring subscription ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/backpedal-ltd)). The audience named on the listing is families, commuters, racers and delivery riders, all of whom own bikes that are expensive to replace.

> "GPS tracking and 24/7 recovery for your bike or ebike." — BackPedal listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/backpedal-ltd))

The product site is at [backpedal.co](https://www.backpedal.co/). The only tool named in the tech stack is Stripe, which handles the billing.

## How much revenue does BackPedal make from 383 subscriptions?

BackPedal had $43,667 in MRR and 383 active subscriptions in November 2026, and it collected $50,187 in the 30 days before the snapshot ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/backpedal-ltd)). All-time revenue was $1,545,686. TrustMRR checks these figures through a Stripe API key.

MRR divided by subscriptions is about $114 per subscription ([calc, from TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/backpedal-ltd)), far above the £12.75 monthly equivalent of the entry plan. A likely reading is that one subscription can cover a fleet or several bikes, or that tracker fees are counted, but the plan mix is not disclosed. Profit and team size are not disclosed either.

## Why did BackPedal pick hardware plus a subscription?

The hardware creates the reason to keep paying. A tracker on its own is a one-off sale; a recovery service only works while the subscription is active, so the renewal is tied to the fear of theft rather than to feature updates. BackPedal was founded in September 2021 in the United Kingdom ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/backpedal-ltd)), which gives it five years of renewals behind the all-time figure.

Marketing channels are not listed. The founder's X account has about 150 followers, so unlike many TrustMRR leaders this business did not grow on a personal audience.

## How does BackPedal compare with Store Stock Checker and SoldComps?

BackPedal is the odd one out among the commerce cases on this site because it ships a physical device. [Store Stock Checker](/cases/store-stock-checker/) and [SoldComps](/cases/soldcomps/) are both software tools for buyers and resellers: no inventory, no shipping, no recovery team.

Our observation: the hardware makes BackPedal harder to copy than a scraper or a browser tool, and it explains why a few hundred subscriptions can carry a high revenue figure. The cost is operational. A recovery promise needs people and processes behind it, which software-only tools never have to fund.

## What can founders learn from BackPedal's recovery model?

- **Sell the outcome, not the device.** BackPedal leads with getting a stolen bike back, and the tracker is the means.
- **Tie renewals to real risk.** Protection only works while the subscription runs, which gives customers a concrete reason to stay.
- **Few customers can be enough.** 383 subscriptions supported $43,667 MRR in November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/backpedal-ltd)).
- **Audience is optional.** A founder with about 150 X followers still built a seven-figure lifetime business in a niche market.
