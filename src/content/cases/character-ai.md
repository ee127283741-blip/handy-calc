Character.AI revenue never caught up with its popularity. The app let millions of people chat with AI characters, from anime heroes to fictional therapists, and its run rate passed $30M a year in 2025 ([founder-reported, Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2025/08/20/character-ai-in-talks-to-sell-or-raise-new-funding-as-chatbot-costs-pile-up/)). But every conversation cost compute, its founders had already left for Google, and lawsuits over teen users piled up. In October 2026 the company filed for Chapter 11 ([media-reported, Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/10/disney-character-ai-chapter-11-bankruptcy-1237155582/)).

## Why did Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas start Character.AI?

Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas left Google in 2021 after it declined to release a chatbot they had built, and founded Character.AI to ship it themselves ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=2105823)). Users could create characters with a personality and talk to them for hours. The product spread fast among young users, who used it for role-play, companionship and fan fiction. It earned money mainly through c.ai+, a $9.99-a-month subscription with faster responses and extra features ([third-party estimate, Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/character-ai/)). Team size is not disclosed.

## What did Google's $2.7B deal mean for Character.AI?

Google paid about $2.7B in August 2024 for a non-exclusive license to Character.AI's technology, and Shazeer and De Freitas rejoined Google's DeepMind ([media-reported, WSJ via PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=2105823)). The deal returned money to investors and staff without a full acquisition. It also left Character.AI as a standalone company without its founders and without the research team that had built its models. It had to turn a popular app into a business on its own.

## How much revenue did Character.AI make before bankruptcy?

Character.AI made more than $30M a year on a run-rate basis in 2025, about $2.5M a month (calc) ([founder-reported, Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2025/08/20/character-ai-in-talks-to-sell-or-raise-new-funding-as-chatbot-costs-pile-up/)). The same report said the company was weighing a sale or new funding because inference and infrastructure costs were eating into that momentum. Its total revenue is not disclosed. Anand, who took over in June 2025, repositioned it as an entertainment company and promised stricter safety rules for teens.

> "doubling down on entertainment, doubling down on trust and safety" — Karandeep Anand ([founder-reported, CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/07/03/tech/character-ai-ceo-chatbots-kids-safety))

## How does Character.AI compare with Midjourney and Suno?

All three are consumer AI products that people use for fun, but only Character.AI paid for long, open-ended conversations. [Midjourney](/cases/midjourney/) charges for images and grew profitably on subscriptions; [Suno](/cases/suno/) charges for songs. Each image or song is a bounded job. A Character.AI user could chat for hours on a cheap plan or for free, so heavy users cost the most. That cost structure, plus safety lawsuits over minors, made the business harder than its user numbers suggested.

Our reading: engagement is not the same as revenue. Character.AI had the kind of daily use most apps want, but it charged a small monthly fee against open-ended compute. The Google deal paid out early backers, yet it left the company without its founders right when it needed to fix that model.

## Which lessons from Character.AI apply to consumer AI apps?

- **Price the heaviest users.** Unlimited conversations on a $9.99 plan meant the most loyal users were the most expensive.
- **Engagement can hide a weak business.** Hours of daily use did not turn into enough revenue to cover compute.
- **A license deal can hollow out a startup.** Google's payment returned money but took the founders and core researchers.
- **Safety is a business risk.** Lawsuits and age limits for teen users cut into the product's core audience.
