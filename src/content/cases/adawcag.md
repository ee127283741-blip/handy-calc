For a small restaurant, an ADA website demand letter can arrive out of nowhere and cost far more than the website did. ADAWCAG sells protection against that risk. The US company, founded in February 2026, had $6,659 in lifetime revenue and $1,344 in monthly recurring revenue by November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/adawcag)). It is one of the smallest companies on this site, and that is part of what makes it useful to study.

## What does ADAWCAG fix on a small business website?

ADAWCAG fixes the accessibility problems in a website's actual code. A free scan shows where a site fails, with no signup, and then a certified human team makes the fixes and documents them, so the owner has evidence of good-faith compliance if a demand letter arrives ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/adawcag)).

> "Fixed pricing from $497, not $50,000" — ADAWCAG pricing on TrustMRR ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/adawcag))

The services listed include Section 508 and WCAG 2.1 AA documentation and manual audits. The company says it appears in the W3C WAI list of web accessibility evaluation tools and is registered as a federal contractor on SAM.gov.

## Why does ADAWCAG pair a free scanner with human fixes?

The free scanner is the top of the funnel; the human fix is the product. About 5,000 users have used the service, according to the company, but only 6 hold active subscriptions ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/adawcag)). That gap is typical of a free tool that feeds a high-ticket service.

The positioning is explicit about what is sold: real compliance by fixing the actual code, audited by a certified human, rather than a scan report alone. That is what lets a small company charge a fixed project fee.
