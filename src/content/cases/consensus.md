Consensus AI revenue is small next to the AI giants, and that is the point of this case. Consensus is an AI search engine that answers questions only from peer-reviewed research. It said in August 2024 that it was on pace for about $1.5M in annualized revenue ([founder-reported, Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-14/consensus-nabs-11-million-to-build-ai-search-tool-for-academics)). That is a modest number, earned in a niche that general chatbots also serve. Its bet is that people who need evidence will pay for a tool that shows its sources.

## What does Consensus do differently from a chatbot?

Consensus answers from scientific papers, not the open web. A user asks a question such as whether a supplement improves sleep, and Consensus searches peer-reviewed studies, summarizes what they found and links each claim to a paper. Eric Olson, the CEO, and Christian Salem founded the company ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-14/consensus-nabs-11-million-to-build-ai-search-tool-for-academics)). Its users include students, researchers and professionals such as clinicians. Founding date, team size and plan prices are not disclosed in the sources we checked.

## Why did Union Square Ventures back Consensus?

Union Square Ventures led an $11.5M Series A in July 2024 ([founder-reported, PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2024/consensus-raises-11-5-million-to-expand-ai-powered-scientific-search-engine)). The case for investing is specialization: a search engine restricted to research can be more trustworthy for scientific questions than a general assistant that mixes blogs, forums and papers. Olson put the strategy in four words.

> "Specialization is our moat" — Eric Olson ([founder-reported, PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2024/consensus-raises-11-5-million-to-expand-ai-powered-scientific-search-engine))

## How does Consensus compare with Perplexity and Jenni AI?

Consensus is a narrow answer engine; [Perplexity](/cases/perplexity/) is a broad one. Perplexity answers almost any question from the web and has raised billions, while Consensus answers research questions from papers on a much smaller budget. [Jenni AI](/cases/jenni-ai/) serves a similar academic audience from the writing side: it helps students and researchers draft papers, while Consensus helps them find what the evidence says.

Our reading: Consensus shows the economics of a specialist in a market where generalists keep getting better. Its revenue depends on users believing that a research-only engine is worth paying for when a general chatbot is free. Citations to real papers are the feature that has to justify the subscription.

## What are the takeaways from Consensus for niche AI search?

- **Narrow sources build trust.** Answering only from peer-reviewed papers is the product, not a limitation.
- **Show the evidence.** Links to the studies behind each answer let users check claims themselves.
- **Small revenue can still raise a Series A.** Consensus raised $11.5M on about $1.5M in annualized revenue.
- **Generalists are the constant threat.** Each improvement in free chatbots raises the bar for a paid specialist.
