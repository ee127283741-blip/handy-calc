Creator Buddy revenue started with a launch most founders only dream about. Alex Finn, a creator with a large following on X, built an AI tool for growing on X with AI coding tools rather than his own code. He says the launch brought in $100K in 15 minutes and $200K in two hours ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://app-dev-and-marketing.pages.dev/i-built-an-app-with-cursor-and-made-100k-in-15-minutes)). Starter Story reports about $300K in ARR ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/creator-buddy/revenue)). The product sold because the audience already trusted the founder.

## What is Creator Buddy?

Creator Buddy is an AI assistant for X. It analyzes a user's account and past posts, then helps them write posts that perform better. Alex Finn built it in 2024 using AI coding tools such as Cursor and later Claude Code, and Starter Story describes his background as non-technical ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/creator-buddy/tools)). The tool pulls post data from X, which costs about $5,000 a month. Team size and prices are not disclosed.

> "I built an app with Cursor and made $100K in 15 minutes" — Alex Finn ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://app-dev-and-marketing.pages.dev/i-built-an-app-with-cursor-and-made-100k-in-15-minutes))

## How did Creator Buddy make $100K in 15 minutes?

Creator Buddy made $100K in 15 minutes because Finn sold it to an audience that was waiting for it. He had built a following on X by posting about growing on X, so the people most likely to buy were already watching ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://app-dev-and-marketing.pages.dev/i-built-an-app-with-cursor-and-made-100k-in-15-minutes)). Launch-day sales reached $200K in two hours. Much of that was likely upfront payments, so ARR, about $300K, is a better measure of the ongoing business ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/creator-buddy/revenue)).

## What does Creator Buddy's X data bill mean for the business?

Creator Buddy depends on X's data, which costs about $5,000 a month ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/creator-buddy/tools)). That is a large fixed cost for a solo product, about 20% of the roughly $25K a month implied by its ARR (calc). It also means one platform's pricing decisions can change the business overnight. Tools built on another company's data inherit that company's leverage.

## How does Creator Buddy compare with Tweet Hunter and TrendFeed?

Creator Buddy competes with [Tweet Hunter](/cases/tweet-hunter/), an earlier X growth tool that was later sold, and sits near [TrendFeed](/cases/trendfeed/), whose non-technical founder also launched to an existing audience. Tweet Hunter grew over years; Creator Buddy front-loaded its revenue with one launch. Both depend on X's data and rules.

Our reading: Creator Buddy is the clearest case in this library of distribution beating engineering. Finn's audience did the selling; AI tools did the building. The question for any creator-built app is whether customers stay after launch-day excitement, which ARR, not launch sales, answers. [Postiz](/cases/postiz/) is the opposite approach: its customers find a free open source scheduler rather than a founder's feed.

## What can creators learn from Creator Buddy?

- **Sell to the audience you built.** Finn's followers bought $200K in two hours.
- **Measure ARR, not launch day.** Upfront launch sales overstate the steady business.
- **Know your platform risk.** A $5,000-a-month data bill ties Creator Buddy to X's pricing.
- **AI tools let creators ship software.** A non-technical founder built the product with Cursor and Claude Code.
