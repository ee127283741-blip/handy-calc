Cognition AI revenue grew 73 times in nine months. Devin, the autonomous coding agent Cognition launched with a viral demo in 2024, went from $1M ARR in September 2024 to $73M ARR in June 2025 ([founder-reported, Cognition blog](https://cognition.com/blog/funding-growth-and-the-next-frontier-of-ai-coding-agents)). Then Cognition bought most of Windsurf, the AI code editor whose $3B sale to OpenAI had just collapsed. Scott Wu wrote that the acquisition more than doubled Cognition's ARR.

## How did Scott Wu and the Cognition team start?

Cognition was founded in 2023 by Scott Wu, Steven Hao and Walden Yan, a team known for competitive programming ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition_AI)). Its first product, Devin, was pitched as an AI software engineer rather than an assistant. Devin takes a ticket, plans the change, writes code in its own environment, runs tests and opens a pull request. That positioning aimed Devin at engineering managers with a backlog, not at individual developers typing in an editor.

## How did Devin's ARR grow from $1M to $73M?

Devin's ARR grew about 73x between September 2024 and June 2025, from $1M to $73M ([founder-reported, Cognition blog](https://cognition.com/blog/funding-growth-and-the-next-frontier-of-ai-coding-agents)). At $73M ARR that is about $6.1M a month (calc). Cognition sold Devin to companies that let it work through real tickets, so revenue grew as customers gave it more of their engineering work. Team size, total revenue and plan prices are not disclosed in the sources we checked.

## Why did Cognition buy Windsurf?

Cognition bought Windsurf to add an editor and an enterprise customer base to its agent. In July 2025 Google paid to license Windsurf's technology and hired its founders (see the [Windsurf](/cases/windsurf/) case), and Cognition agreed to buy what remained: the product, the brand and the rest of the team. Cognition said Windsurf brought $82M of ARR and more than 350 enterprise customers, with enterprise ARR doubling quarter over quarter ([founder-reported, Cognition blog](https://cognition.com/blog/windsurf)). In September 2025 Wu wrote that combined enterprise ARR was up more than 30% in the seven weeks after the deal ([founder-reported, Cognition blog](https://cognition.com/blog/funding-growth-and-the-next-frontier-of-ai-coding-agents)).

> "It's a privilege to welcome Windsurf to Cognition." — Scott Wu ([founder-reported, Scott Wu on X](https://x.com/ScottWu46/status/1944820058494263559))

## How does Cognition compare with Cursor and Windsurf?

Cognition sells an agent that works on its own; [Cursor](/cases/cursor/) sells an editor that a developer drives. That difference shapes who buys. Cursor spread bottom-up through individual developers, while Devin was sold to teams that hand it whole tasks. With [Windsurf](/cases/windsurf/), Cognition now owns both shapes: an editor for hands-on work and an agent for delegated work. [Replit](/cases/replit/) is a third approach, with an agent aimed at people who are not professional developers.

Our reading: the Windsurf deal let Cognition skip years of building an editor and an enterprise sales list. CNBC reported a $10.2B valuation two months later ([media-reported, CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/08/cognition-valued-at-10point2-billion-two-months-after-windsurf-.html)). The risk is integration: two products, two cultures and two pricing models had to become one company quickly.

## What lessons does Cognition hold for AI coding startups?

- **Sell the outcome, not the tool.** Devin was pitched as an engineer that finishes tickets, and its ARR grew 73x in nine months.
- **A broken deal can be a bargain.** Windsurf's collapsed sale to OpenAI let Cognition buy $82M of ARR and 350+ enterprise customers.
- **Report the parts honestly.** Cognition gave Devin's ARR and Windsurf's ARR separately before saying the deal doubled the total.
- **Agents and editors converge.** Owning both lets one company serve hands-on and hands-off coding.
