Artisan AI ARR reached about $5M on the back of three words on a billboard. Artisan, which sells an AI sales rep named Ava, ran a San Francisco ad campaign telling companies to "Stop Hiring Humans." When it raised a $25M Series A in April 2025, it reported about $5M in ARR and roughly 250 customers ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/09/artisan-the-stop-hiring-humans-ai-agent-startup-raises-25m-and-is-still-hiring-humans)). TechCrunch's headline pointed out the irony: the company was still hiring humans.

## What does Artisan's Ava do?

Ava is an AI sales development rep. It builds lists of leads, researches each prospect and writes and sends outbound emails, the work junior sales staff usually do. Jaspar Carmichael-Jack, the CEO, and Sam Stallings founded Artisan, and it went through Y Combinator ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/09/artisan-the-stop-hiring-humans-ai-agent-startup-raises-25m-and-is-still-hiring-humans)). Plan prices and team size are not disclosed.

## Did the "Stop Hiring Humans" campaign work for Artisan?

It got Artisan noticed. The billboards drew wide attention and plenty of anger online. TechCrunch reported the backlash alongside the funding, noting that Artisan itself kept hiring people while selling an AI replacement for them ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/09/artisan-the-stop-hiring-humans-ai-agent-startup-raises-25m-and-is-still-hiring-humans)). Provocation is cheap marketing, but it also attaches a company to a slogan many buyers dislike.

## How much ARR did Artisan have at its Series A?

Artisan had about $5M in ARR and roughly 250 customers at its April 2025 Series A, about $417K a month (calc) ([founder-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/09/artisan-the-stop-hiring-humans-ai-agent-startup-raises-25m-and-is-still-hiring-humans)). That is an average of about $20K a year per customer (calc). Glade Brook Capital led the $25M round, with Y Combinator and HubSpot Ventures among other backers ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/09/artisan-the-stop-hiring-humans-ai-agent-startup-raises-25m-and-is-still-hiring-humans)). Total revenue is not disclosed.

## How does Artisan compare with 11x and Clay?

Artisan and [11x](/cases/11x/) sell the same thing, an AI sales rep, and both rely on bold marketing. 11x said it was approaching $10M ARR, but TechCrunch reported ([unverified, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/a16z-and-benchmark-backed-11x-has-been-claiming-customers-it-doesnt-have)) that it counted trial contracts as ARR, so we label that figure unverified. Artisan's $5M figure has not been publicly disputed. [Clay](/cases/clay/) takes a different route: it sells data enrichment and workflow tools that human sales teams use, rather than replacing the rep. [Instantly](/cases/instantly/) sells the email-sending infrastructure underneath many outbound campaigns.

Our reading: the AI SDR category is full of trials and churn, because buyers can test a rep for a month and leave if meetings do not appear. Artisan's roughly $20K of ARR per customer suggests mid-sized contracts. Whether they renew is what will separate it from the hype the billboards created.

[Fathom AI](/cases/fathom-ai/) takes the opposite approach: it uses 12 AI agents inside a three-person company with no engineering team.

## What are the lessons from Artisan's provocative marketing?

- **Provocation buys reach.** "Stop Hiring Humans" made a small startup famous without a large ad budget.
- **It also buys enemies.** The same slogan made Artisan a target for criticism and media irony.
- **Reported ARR should survive scrutiny.** Artisan's $5M figure stands in contrast to 11x's disputed claims.
- **Retention is the test for AI reps.** Buyers can trial an AI rep for a month, so renewals matter more than new logos.
