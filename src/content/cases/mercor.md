Mercor revenue is the largest number in this library that is not software revenue. Mercor's gross annualized run rate passed $2B in June 2026 ([media-reported, Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/137121-mercor-doubles-to-2b-gross-revenue-run-rate-as-ai-labs-buy-expert-data/)). Mercor does not sell seats. It hires doctors, lawyers, bankers and engineers by the hour, has them write and grade the data AI labs use to train models, and bills the labs. Most of each dollar goes back out to the experts, which is why the word "gross" matters.

## How did Mercor go from AI recruiting to AI training data?

Mercor started as an AI recruiting tool and found that its biggest buyers were AI labs. Brendan Foody, Adarsh Hiremath and Surya Midha founded it in 2023 to match software contractors with jobs using AI interviews. As labs began paying for expert feedback to improve their models, Mercor's network of vetted professionals became the product. When it raised a $100M Series B at a $2B valuation in February 2025, Bloomberg described its CEO as a 21-year-old Thiel Fellow ([media-reported, Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-20/ai-startup-led-by-21-year-old-thiel-fellow-lands-2-billion-valuation)).

## How much money does Mercor make?

Mercor's reported run rate grew about fourfold in nine months. TechCrunch reported in September 2025 that it was approaching $450M in annualized run-rate revenue ([media-reported, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/09/sources-ai-training-startup-mercor-eyes-10b-valuation-on-450m-run-rate/)). By June 2026 the gross run rate had passed $2B ([media-reported, Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/137121-mercor-doubles-to-2b-gross-revenue-run-rate-as-ai-labs-buy-expert-data/)), or about $166M a month (calc). The company's net revenue, after paying experts, is not disclosed, and neither is total revenue to date.

## Where does Mercor's money go?

Most of it goes to the experts. Felicis, which led the Series C, wrote that Mercor was paying more than $1.5M a day to about 30,000 experts in October 2025 ([media-reported, Felicis](https://www.felicis.com/blog/the-horizon-of-expertise)). That is roughly $550M a year in payouts (calc), against a run rate TechCrunch put near $450M only a month earlier. Those two figures were measured at different times, but together they show that payouts are a large share of what Mercor bills. Its team size and the fee it keeps are not disclosed.

> "This round values Mercor at $10 billion, 5x our Series B valuation." — Brendan Foody ([founder-reported, Mercor blog](https://www.mercor.com/blog/series-c/))

## How does Mercor compare with other careers and hiring cases?

Mercor is the only marketplace among the careers cases here, and that changes how its revenue reads. [HeadshotPro](/cases/headshotpro/) and [AiApply](/cases/aiapply/) sell software to job seekers and keep nearly all of what they charge. Mercor passes most of its billings to experts, so its $2B gross run rate cannot be compared directly with their revenue or with software ARR at companies like [Harvey](/cases/harvey/). Its closest comparison is a staffing firm, with AI labs as the clients.

What it shares with the software cases is speed. Mercor's valuation went from $2B in February 2025 to $10B in October 2025 ([founder-reported, Mercor blog](https://www.mercor.com/blog/series-c/)), as AI labs competed for expert data. Our reading: Mercor's growth depends on a few very large buyers, so a change in how labs train models would show up in its revenue quickly.

## What can marketplace founders learn from Mercor?

- **Follow the buyer who pays most.** Mercor began in recruiting but grew by selling expert work to AI labs.
- **Say gross when it is gross.** A marketplace's billings and its net revenue are different numbers; label which one you report.
- **Supply is the moat.** A vetted network of 30,000 experts is harder to copy than the software that matches them.
- **Concentration cuts both ways.** A few large lab customers can double revenue in months and can reduce it just as fast.
