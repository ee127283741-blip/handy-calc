Marky started as a tool for self-publishing authors and ended up selling to dentists, law firms and plumbers. Along the way it crossed $1,017,162 in all-time revenue, with $22,101 in MRR in November 2026 ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/marky)). A founder message on the same page claims most of last year's revenue turned into profit.

## Who does Marky make social media posts for?

Marky makes posts for local service businesses. The TrustMRR listing names healthcare practices, professional services firms, home services, retail, hospitality and real estate professionals ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/marky)). Yet the one-line tagline at the top of the same page still reads "a tool for self-publishing authors", which suggests the audience shifted after launch.

The homepage explains the product: type in your website, and Marky learns your voice, style and customers, then generates posts with your logo and colors, schedules them and publishes to Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, X, Google, TikTok and Pinterest ([founder-reported, Marky homepage](https://mymarky.ai/)). Every post waits for approval before it goes out. The homepage compares it to ChatGPT, Canva and a scheduler combined.

Marky reported $1,017,162 in all-time revenue and $22,101 in MRR from 476 active subscriptions in November 2026, with $16,203 in the last 30 days ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/marky)). The founder message adds trailing figures that TrustMRR does not verify.

## Which channels brought Marky its customers?

The listing names four channels: Facebook, Meta ads, word of mouth and influencers ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/marky)). For local business owners, Facebook is where both the ads and the audience live, which fits.

The homepage claims 120,000+ businesses and 5hr+ saved a week ([founder-reported, Marky homepage](https://mymarky.ai/)), while the listing estimates about 200,000 users. Either way, a few hundred paid subscriptions sit on top of a much larger free or trial base. The stack includes React, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Supabase and OpenAI. Who leads the company is a little unclear: TrustMRR lists Arian Adeli as founder, while the message is signed by Josiah Coad, so we name both.

Our observation: Postiz is a scheduler first and lets users bring their own content. Marky writes the content for people who don't want to, which is why it sells to plumbers rather than social media managers. Its lifetime revenue passed one million dollars while MRR stayed in the low twenty thousands, which suggests a business that has held steady rather than one still growing fast.

## What lessons does Marky offer marketing tool founders?

- **Follow the buyer who pays.** Marky's tagline still mentions authors, but its listing now targets local businesses ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/marky)).
- **Do the work, not just the scheduling.** Generating on-brand posts from a website removes the blank-page problem.
- **Keep approval in the loop.** "You approve every post" reassures owners worried about AI mistakes.
- **Steady MRR can be very profitable.** The founder claims $336K net income on $516K TTM revenue, if the claim holds.
