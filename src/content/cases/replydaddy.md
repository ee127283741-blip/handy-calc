ReplyDaddy MRR depends on who you ask, which makes it a useful case. Neel Seth, a former product lead who left a corporate job to build AI tools, wrote on LinkedIn that ReplyDaddy did about $5,000 in MRR ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/replydaddy/founders)). An outside tracker, MRR Story, put it closer to $1,500 ([third-party estimate, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/replydaddy/monetization)). Seth's own site reported more than $20K in revenue over four months. ReplyDaddy shows what a product person who is not an engineer can now ship with AI.

## What does ReplyDaddy do?

ReplyDaddy finds Reddit posts where people ask for recommendations in a product's category, such as "what's the best tool for X," and helps founders reply with a useful answer that mentions their product. Reddit threads also rank in Google and feed AI answers, so a good reply can bring visitors for months. The product started as a script Seth used for himself and grew into a SaaS, according to a payments provider's case study ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/de/case-studies/replydaddy)). Prices and team size are not disclosed.

## How did a product lead build ReplyDaddy with AI?

Neel Seth worked as a product lead, the person who decides what gets built, not the engineer who builds it. Starter Story describes him leaving the corporate world to build profitable AI SaaS tools such as ReplyDaddy, with AI as his coding partner ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/replydaddy/founders)). AI coding tools let product people turn specs straight into working software. Seth's habit of setting public goals also shows a product manager's mindset: he publicly targeted one new subscription a day.

> "we already do 5,000 USD MRR." — Neel Seth ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/replydaddy/founders))

## How much MRR does ReplyDaddy really have?

ReplyDaddy's MRR is somewhere between the two published figures. Seth's LinkedIn post said about $5,000 ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/replydaddy/founders)); MRR Story estimated about $1,500 ([third-party estimate, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/replydaddy/monetization)). Seth's site reported $20K+ in total revenue from September to December 2025 ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/replydaddy/monetization)), about $5K a month on average (calc), which is closer to his own figure.

## How does ReplyDaddy compare with ChatSEO and Gojiberry AI?

ReplyDaddy, [ChatSEO](/cases/chatseo/) and [Gojiberry AI](/cases/gojiberry-ai/) all sell AI-powered lead generation to small companies. Gojiberry finds buying signals on LinkedIn and sends outreach; ChatSEO targets search visibility. ReplyDaddy works in Reddit threads, where people ask for recommendations openly. Like Gojiberry, it was built by a solo founder who used AI to move fast, and it competes in a market where many small tools chase the same founders.

Our reading: the gap between $1,500 and $5,000 is common for small SaaS, where founders share their best month and trackers guess from public signals. Total revenue over a period, like the $20K+ figure here, is often the most honest number to watch. [Postiz](/cases/postiz/) shows Reddit from the founder's side: its open source scheduler drew install requests on a self-hosting forum.

## What lessons does ReplyDaddy offer product managers who want to build?

- **Product skills transfer.** Deciding what to build is the hard part; AI now handles much of the coding.
- **Start with your own script.** ReplyDaddy began as a tool Seth used himself.
- **Fish where buyers ask.** Reddit threads asking for recommendations are leads that already want a product.
- **Expect different MRR numbers.** Founder posts and outside trackers often disagree, so compare totals over time.
