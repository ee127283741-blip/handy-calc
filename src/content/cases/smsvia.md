Almost every app now asks for a phone number before it lets you in. SMSVia turns that small annoyance into a subscription. The iOS app, founded in January 2026 by a founder listed as Onur, had earned $13,500 all time by November 2026, with $1,487 collected in the last 30 days ([verified, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/smsvia)). Its revenue is checked through RevenueCat, the subscription layer it uses inside the app.

## What problem does SMSVia solve for app sign-ups?

SMSVia gives users a virtual number so they never hand their real one to a new service. People rent temporary or long-term numbers from countries worldwide and receive verification codes for apps such as WhatsApp and Telegram, according to the listing ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/smsvia)).

> "No SIM needed. Get a virtual number in 150+ countries, receive SMS codes in seconds, and keep your real number private." — SMSVia listing on TrustMRR ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/smsvia))

The pitch is privacy: keeping a personal number away from spam, data breaches and unwanted contacts. The listing names B2C users and app store optimization as its marketing channel.

## Why does SMSVia charge by the week?

SMSVia sells a $4.99 weekly plan next to a $19.99 monthly plan ([founder-reported, TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/smsvia)). A weekly plan fits a job that is often short: someone needs a number to pass one verification, then may not need it again for a while.

The gap between last-30-days revenue and MRR also hints at this. Revenue collected in the month ran higher than the recurring figure, which is what you would expect if some buyers pay once or churn quickly. The app's App Store rating was 4.0 out of 5 from 8 ratings at the time.

Our observation: Voicy and BreadSync sell a tool people use again and again. SMSVia sells a moment of need, a code that has to arrive now. That makes app store search, which the listing names as its only channel, a natural fit, but it also makes recurring revenue harder to hold.

## What are the takeaways for indie developers from SMSVia?

- **Small, urgent jobs sell.** A verification code is a tiny task, but people pay when a sign-up is blocked.
- **Short plans match short needs.** A weekly price lowers the barrier for one-off users.
- **Privacy is a clear message.** "Keep your real number private" needs no explanation.
- **Recurring revenue is the hard part.** $1,192 MRR against $1,487 collected in 30 days shows how much comes from non-recurring buying.
