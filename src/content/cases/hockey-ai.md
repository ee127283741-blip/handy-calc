Hockey AI revenue is small, about $1K a month, and its founders are 14 and 17. Eli and Harris Zimmerman, brothers from Long Island, cannot code. They used Rork, an AI app builder, to make an app that watches hockey clips and gives coaching feedback. In its first month it had more than 2,000 downloads and about $1K a month in revenue, after the brothers shipped 114 test builds ([founder-reported, Rork](https://rork.com/blog/eli-hockey-ai-case-study)). It is the youngest founder team in this library.

## How did two teenagers build Hockey AI?

Eli and Harris Zimmerman built Hockey AI by describing features to Rork and testing each version on their phones. Rork says they shipped 114 TestFlight builds, Apple's beta versions, in about a month, fixing and adjusting after every round ([Rork](https://rork.com/blog/eli-hockey-ai-case-study)). Players upload a clip of a drill or game, and the app analyzes skating, shooting and stickhandling and returns tips.

> "an AI-powered hockey analysis app designed to help players improve their skills through personalized video feedback" — Hockey AI App Store listing ([founder-reported, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6756755012))

## What does Hockey AI earn in its first month?

Hockey AI earned about $1K a month in its first month, with 2,000+ downloads ([founder-reported, Rork](https://rork.com/blog/eli-hockey-ai-case-study)). The App Store lists plans at $6.99 a month or $39.99 a year ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6756755012)). Revenue was uneven: Rork notes the brothers had two days in a row with zero revenue shortly before its interview. Their targets are $5K a month and then $20K a month within six months; those are goals, so we label them unverified. Total revenue is not disclosed.

## What do Hockey AI's 114 builds say about AI app building?

The 114 builds show that AI app builders turn development into fast trial and error. Without code, the brothers could not debug in the traditional way; instead, they described a change, built a new version and tested it, over and over ([Rork](https://rork.com/blog/eli-hockey-ai-case-study)). That loop is cheap enough for teenagers with no budget. It also means quality depends on patience and testing, not on engineering knowledge.

## How does Hockey AI compare with Wrestle AI and 3AK Track?

Hockey AI, [Wrestle AI](/cases/wrestle-ai/) and [3AK Track](/cases/3ak-track/) are all Rork-built AI coaches for one sport. Wrestle AI's 18-year-old founder reached about $17K a month ([founder-reported, Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/how-i-grew-my-mobile-app-to-17k-per-month)); 3AK Track's college athletes started with 150,000+ followers and made $10K in month one ([founder-reported, Rork](https://rork.com/blog/3ak-case-study)). Hockey AI is the earliest and smallest of the three, at about $1K a month, and its founders are the youngest.

Our reading: the build is no longer the hard part for any of these founders. The gap between $1K and $17K a month comes from distribution, an audience or a marketing engine. Hockey AI's next step is the same one the others solved: reaching many more players than the brothers know personally.

## What are the lessons from Hockey AI for young builders?

- **Age is no longer a barrier to shipping.** A 14-year-old and a 17-year-old launched a paid AI app.
- **Iterate in small steps.** 114 test builds replaced traditional debugging.
- **Expect uneven early revenue.** Two days of zero sales are normal for a new app.
- **Distribution decides the next stage.** Similar apps earn far more once they reach a large audience.
